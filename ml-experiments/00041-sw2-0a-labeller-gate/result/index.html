    Experiment 41: can a labeller pass a known-answer gate on switching events?

    We asked whether a program can reliably tell when electricity load really moved from one substation to a neighbour, using only the power readings and the engineers' switching log. To test that without knowing the real answers, we built 300 test windows of power readings. In 150 windows we added a fake transfer of load between two substations. In 120 windows we added nothing but gave the window the log time of a real switching job (we call these placebo windows). In the last 30 we added a fake transfer and put the logged time 1 to 3 hours off. A labeller (here a written set of rules, and separately the AI model Sonnet) read each window and said whether a transfer was visible, and when it started and ended. Sonnet called none of the 120 placebo windows visible. It found 74% of the large fake transfers (25 of 34, those of 25% or more of the receiving substation's load lasting 6 hours or more), under our bar of 80%. In a further check that was not planned, it found about 9 in 10 of the large, long transfers when the receiving substation has its own power readings (46 of 52), and 6 in 10 when it does not (25 of 42).

    Partly supported: Sonnet called no placebo window visible and timed starts and ends well, but found only 0.74 of the large, long fake transfers, under the 0.80 bar. On 120 placebo cards Sonnet called 0 visible (95% interval 0.00 to 0.03). It found 25 of 34 injected transfers of 25% or more of the receiving load lasting 6 hours or more (0.74; 0.57 to 0.85), or 71 of 94 (0.76; 0.66 to 0.83) with 60 further cards from the same band. Where a metered receiver exists the recall is 0.89 (46 of 52; 0.77 to 0.95); where the receiver is unmetered and only the donor steps it is 0.60 (25 of 42; 0.44 to 0.73). The rules-only labeller reaches 0.65 (22 of 34) with 3 of 120 placebo cards called visible. The maintainer waived the bar for naming the receiving substations after seeing the rules-only results, so the waiver is post hoc.

    The first test set had tells

    A Sonnet reviewer found that the first 300 test cards were easier to reject than real ones, so all gate figures here come from a rebuilt set. In the first set the injected cards' log times ended in :05 or :35 only, while placebo cards carried real minutes; every injected card showed a metered side B while 39 of 120 placebo cards showed "side B: not metered"; and the injected start times were spread over the whole day while the placebo times kept real clock times. In the rebuilt sets (test2, testbig2) both kinds of card take a real operation's log times, sheet sides, duration, and clock time, shifted by whole weeks into a quiet period. The minute, side-B and night-share distributions now match. The first set gave Sonnet a placebo rate of 1 of 120 (1 of 81 without the side-B tell) and a recall of 0.92 on its 25 large, long cards; those figures are not used. The 100 development cards came from the first generator.

    Setup

    • The hindsight model (a model that sees weather and the following months, so it predicts demand better than any live forecast could) is a leave-one-month-out XGBoost quantile model (q10, q50, q90) on experiment 6's 13 input columns for each of the 16 substations, with every logged closure (padded 24 hours) removed from training. The hindsight model is supporting evidence on a card and is never the deciding evidence.
    • Cards hold the log times, step features F1 to F7 with percentiles against up to 400 quiet-week draws, and hourly values. The 300 test cards are 150 injected, 120 placebo, and 30 injected with the log time 1 to 3 hours off. A further 60 injected cards in the 25%-or-more, 6-hour-or-longer band were added after the first scoring, at the planning session's request, because 34 cards in that band give a wide recall interval.
    • The injected cards follow the real mix of sheets. 60 of 150 injected cards sit on a sheet with no metered side B, so the load goes to an unmetered receiver and only the donor steps. The design assumed 15%; the real log has 30% to 40%.
    • Both labellers saw the same cards. The rules-only labeller applies percentile bars (0.97 at the close, 0.90 at the open, 0.985 for one-sided) that were set before the first development card was scored and not changed. The Sonnet labeller read ten cards per run with a written brief and no plots. The second Sonnet labeller read the 168 test cards the first called visible or low confidence, plus a random quarter of the rest.

    Hindsight model checks

    Excluding logged closures removed contamination, and the hindsight model did not absorb the closures. In the 7 days either side of 358 side-windows of closures over 3 days, the mean hindsight z is 0.004 (standard error 0.054), and 19.4% of observations fall outside the q10 to q90 band (nominal 20%). Training with the logged closures removed gave a lower out-of-event mean absolute error than removing the same volume at random positions in 5 of 6 substations tested; the gap is 0.043 MW on substation 3 (interval 0.008 to 0.081) and 0.010 MW on substation 14 (0.001 to 0.020), and the other four intervals include zero.

    Both labellers miss small and short transfers, and Sonnet reaches 0.80 only for transfers of 25% or more lasting over 1 day. The over-1-day band gives Sonnet 0.83 (30 of 36; 0.68 to 0.92) and the rules 0.75. Transfers of 25% or more lasting 6 to 24 hours give 0.71 and 0.57. Recall is 0.14 to 0.38 under 10% of the receiving load and 0.50 to 0.63 for 10% to 25%.

For the 42 where only the donor steps, Sonnet's is 0.60 (0.44 to 0.73) and the rules' 0.45 (0.31 to 0.60). This split was made after seeing the results and is not a planned row. A bar of 0.80 on the real sheet mix fails, and on cards with a metered receiver the point estimate passes.

    Receivers named (waived bar)

    The maintainer waived the receivers-named bar after the rules-only results, so the change is post hoc, and the figure is reported only. Sonnet names a receiver with a share of 30% or more in 27 of 75 cases (0.36). It names the receiver in 26 of 58 cases it calls conserved (0.45) and in 1 of 17 it calls one-sided. By count of metered receivers in the truth, it names 6 of 27 (one), 14 of 37 (two), and 7 of 11 (three). By series type, it names 20 of 54 on MVA series and 7 of 21 on MW series, and 3 of 6 on cards with an unmetered share. The rules-only labeller names 53%.

    Token cost

    Sonnet labelling cost 14.9 million subagent tokens for 1,156 card labellings, about 12,900 each. Labeller 1 used 10.5 million on 820 cards (100 development, 300 test, 60 further, then 300 test and 60 further after the rebuild) and labeller 2 used 4.4 million on 336 cards, as the harness reports each subagent run. The development set and the first, flawed test set cost 8.3 million of the total. No Opus run was needed, because the injected truth settles every disagreement. The full run SW2-0b is about 770 cards (533 operations, 36 edge events, 50 short closures, and about 150 alarms); at the same rate, one labeller costs about 10 million tokens and a second labeller on half of them about 5 million more.

    What was not done

    • The development set confirmed that Sonnet labels in the right schema (100 cards, event F1 0.75); no prompt was tuned, and the rules-only thresholds were not tuned either.
    • No card used a plot. The design allowed one.
    • Bank holidays are not excluded from the same-weekday reference.
    • The hindsight model uses 150 trees at learning rate 0.1, shallower than experiment 17's fits.
    • The absorption check against random exclusion ran on 6 of the 16 substations.
    • The kappa subset is not random: it holds the 168 cards that the first labeller called visible or low confidence plus a random quarter of the rest, so kappa describes agreement on that enriched sample.
    • The real-operation cards of SW2-0b have not been compared with the hidden test cards yet; that check comes before the full run.

    Lessons for later experiments

    • Score detectors only in the band where Sonnet's recall has a point estimate of 0.80 or more: 25% or more of the receiving load, lasting over 1 day.
    • A detector's recall against labelled events overstates its recall against all real switching, because Sonnet misses between a third and a half of transfers of 10% to 25% and 40% of donor-only transfers of 25% or more.
    • The second labeller agrees with the first (kappa 0.79), so a second labeller on a sample of the cards in SW2-0b is plausible, though kappa was measured on an enriched sample and agreement on the other cards is untested.

    Known defect in the labelled set

    The labelled set's moved_share_of_receiver column is missing for most events. The labeller wrote donor and receiver series ids as text, and the calculation that turns the moved MW into a share of the receiving load compares them with whole numbers, so the share is empty for any event whose receivers are text. Counts of events by share band in the labelled-set page were corrected for events lasting over 1 day; use experiment 44's load_events for every other use.
