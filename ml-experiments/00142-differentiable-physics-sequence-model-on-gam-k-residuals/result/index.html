    Differentiable physics: a small sequence model on GAM-K's recent residuals

    Take-home: a small neural network trained on one year of GAM-K's forecast errors did not learn to adapt when those errors change, so we did not score it. GAM-K is our demand model with a Kalman filter (a method that updates a forecast from its recent errors). We added a known change to real errors, either a +3-point step for 8 weeks or a +3-point shift on Saturday evenings, and checked whether the network, trained on unchanged errors, corrected for the change. It recovered none of it, in two architectures, and the sign was wrong for the step. The experiment therefore stops at this check, and the verdict is inconclusive: the day-ahead forecast score was not measured, and the comparison with the Kalman filter and recipe R (subtract 0.75 times the last 14 days' mean error) was not made. Whether more history would help is untested.

    Question

    Can a small neural network, trained on the stored residuals of GAM-K, do the job of the Kalman filter and recipe R, and does it give better tails? GAM-K is the convex demand model with a Kalman filter on ECMWF ensemble weather (experiment 111). Recipe R subtracts 0.75 times the mean of the last 14 days' errors by half-hour (experiment 115); the same-weekday profile added only 0.011 points. The network pools the substations with a substation embedding, so patterns shared between substations can be learned. Sequence models of recent errors follow Hochreiter and Schmidhuber (1997) and Cho et al. (2014) (the gated recurrent unit used here); the pinball loss is the standard training loss of quantile regression (Koenker and Bassett, 1978).

    Design

    This section is the plan as pre-registered; the gate failed, so only the samples, the gate fits, and the tuning-month fits ran (see Results).

    • Substations. The six substations whose member forecasts experiment 111 stored (D5, D10, D11, D14, D16, and D9). Experiment 111's ensemble store holds seven series (those six and D18), so the other DP series have no stored GAM-K member forecasts, and no demand model is refitted. D9 is trained on but reported beside the five decision substations.
    • One sample per (substation, run). 810 runs per substation, of which about 530 precede the scored year: about 3,200 training samples, and about 1,100 for the odd-months-only fit. Consecutive samples share 13 of 14 input days, so the independent information is about one year of days per substation. Target: the 48 half-hours of day D+1 (leads 15.5 to 39 hours), as actual minus K0's median forecast.
    • Clock. Every day, the day type, the holiday flag, and the output index use the UTC day and the UTC half-hour (0 to 47), the day D+1 being a UTC day. No local clock enters.
    • Input. The 14 UTC days before the origin's day, plus a 15th partial day, as vectors of 48 half-hour residuals from SiteData.stream (observed minus the chained GAM fit, driven by the analysis proxy, ending at the origin's latest usable reading, 3.5 hours before the 09:00 UTC origin) with a reading mask; each day carries its day type (five types, as in experiment 134) and a holiday-like flag. Missing readings are zero with mask zero. The target day enters through its day type, holiday-like flag, Christmas-window flag, and the calibrated ECMWF ensemble-mean temperature and irradiance at its 48 half-hours (published by the origin, so available at forecast time; past days use the analysis proxy, as in experiment 111). The target day's actuals are never an input.
    • Network. A one-layer gated recurrent unit (32 hidden units) over the 15 day vectors, a substation embedding (4 dimensions), and a two-layer head (64 units) producing 144 numbers per sample: a median correction per half-hour, and a lower and an upper log-scale per half-hour that stretch K0's own quantile offsets (the distance of each K0 quantile from K0's median), so the 11 quantiles cannot cross. About 35,000 parameters (about 42,000 with the linear skip layer added after the first gate run). AdamW, weight decay, dropout 0.1. Early stopping uses month-block validation folds, purged by 15 days either side of the held-out block; the early-stopped model is the one used (no refit).
    • Arms. K0; R; SEQ-ridge (a ridge regression of the same median correction on the same inputs, the linear reference; if the neural network does not beat it, a neural network is not needed); SEQ-blind (the SEQ-quantile network with the residual input zeroed, so that any gain from the weather and day-type scale heads alone can be told apart from reading the residuals); SEQ-median (median correction trained on absolute error; the spread is rebuilt by experiment 111's routine as for every other arm); SEQ-quantile (the correction and the two scales trained with the pinball loss at the 11 quantiles).
    • Training sets. Replicate comparison: train on the odd (tuning) months only and score the even months. Scored-year comparison: train on all runs whose valid times fall before 2025-07-01 (embargoing the last 2 days), early stopping on a month-block fold held out of those. The final forecast averages the fold models (3 folds for the replicate fit, 5 for the scored-year fit) and 2 seeds each; the seed is fixed per job.
    • Compute. About 3,000 samples and 35,000 parameters: seconds per fit, on the GPU if it is free and on the CPU otherwise, far inside the 2 GPU-hour cap. Every script runs under systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=16G -p OOMPolicy=continue with one thread per worker.
    • Other leads. The network outputs day D+1 only. Every arm built on it equals K0 at other leads, so the all-lead columns show R's lead-band behaviour against K0 and SEQ's equal to K0.
    • Reuse. Rows, periods, scoring, bootstrap, breakdowns, and charts are experiment 134's, copied into this folder with the prefix e00142_. No change to src/ or packages/.

    Planned contrasts (99% month-resampled paired intervals; D-labels only)

    • C0. SEQ-quantile minus SEQ-blind (does reading the residuals help beyond the weather and day-type scale heads).
    • C1. SEQ-median minus R at D+1, scored year (decides the verdict), and replicate months.
    • C2. SEQ-quantile minus R at D+1, scored year, and replicate months.
    • C3. SEQ-quantile minus SEQ-median at D+1 (does the quantile loss help the tails).
    • C4. SEQ-median minus K0, SEQ-quantile minus K0, and SEQ-median minus SEQ-ridge.
    • Beside them: all lead bands, winter, winter evening peaks (16:00 to 20:00), cold nights (daily mean under about 3 degrees C) with q95 coverage, holidays and the days after them, and Christmas; tail coverage and pinball at q01, q05, q95, q99; the training-versus-validation gap.

    Predictions

    • P1. SEQ-median minus R is between -0.03 and +0.05 points at D+1 in the scored year, with a 99% interval about 0.03 wide; the most likely outcome is a null.
    • P2. SEQ-quantile minus SEQ-median is between -0.05 and +0.02, and SEQ-quantile minus R is between -0.05 and +0.03.
    • P3. Cold-night q95 coverage of SEQ-quantile is between 74% and 82% (K0: 73 to 78%); it does not reach 90%.
    • P4. SEQ-quantile minus SEQ-blind is between -0.03 and 0.00: most of any tail gain comes from the weather and day-type heads, not from the residual history.
    • P5. SEQ-ridge scores within 0.02 points of SEQ-median.
    • P6. The validation pinball loss exceeds the training loss by 10% or more in the replicate fit and by less than that in the scored-year fit.
    • P7. Holidays and Christmas have too few rows (about 400 and 100 per substation-year) for any contrast to exclude zero.

    Decision rule

    The threshold is 0.02 points at D+1, applied to the point estimate; the 99% interval must also exclude zero. A serving-path model is a new component that has to be trained, versioned, and monitored, so a smaller gain does not justify it. The primary arm for the verdict is SEQ-median against R (C1); SEQ-quantile against SEQ-median (C3) decides the tail claim only. SEQ-median is supported if C1 is at most -0.02 with the interval excluding zero in the scored year, the replicate months agree in sign, and SEQ-median beats SEQ-ridge. It is small gain if the interval excludes zero but the estimate is above -0.02. A tail gain is supported if C3 excludes zero below zero and cold-night q95 coverage moves at least 5 points towards 95% without SEQ-blind doing the same. Otherwise the verdict is not supported, and a clean negative is a fine result. A failed gate caps the verdict at inconclusive. The scored-year network trains on all pre-scored months while R's kappa was tuned on the odd months only; this asymmetry favours the network and is a caveat.

    Known-answer gate on real residuals (before any scored fit)

    Two injections are added to the real residual inputs of April to June 2024 (before every tuning month) and targets of the validation folds, and the network was trained on clean data. (a) A level step of +3 points for eight weeks: the network must recover at least 30% of the step from the third day on and more than R does. (b) A +3-point shift on Saturdays 17:00 to 21:00 UTC for eight weeks: it must recover at least 15% of the shift from the third injected Saturday on, as experiment 134's gate required of RB. The networks are scored on the validation folds that they did not train on, and the gate tests the architecture's ability to adapt on real residuals, not its usefulness.

    The absorption test. A second network is trained with the injection present in the training weeks A (the first four of the eight), and is evaluated on the later held-out weeks B twice, with the injection present and absent. It passes if, on the clean held-out weeks, its correction at the injected half-hours is under 25% of the shift: it must adapt from the residual inputs and not memorise the calendar position. A failed gate stops the experiment before any replicate or scored forecast is read. The gate ran twice, and the absorption test was redefined after the first run (see Results).

    What this experiment will not say

    Six substations, five of them decision substations, and one independent winter; the network is trained on GAM-K's residuals on ensemble-driven weather, not on the production XGBoost blend; the result bears on a research engine, not on a model release.

    Results

    • Gate, first architecture (gated recurrent unit alone). Level step of +3 points for eight weeks (April to June 2024, before every tuning month): the network recovered -5% of it from the third day (gate: at least 30% and more than R); R recovered 0%, because recipe R removes each day's mean. Saturday-evening shift of +3 points: the network recovered 0.04% (gate: 15%). The absorption test was ill-defined as first written (it compared against zero and not against the clean-trained network, and returned -54%), so that number is not used.
    • Gate, second architecture (post hoc revision before any replicate or scored read). A linear skip from the 14-day mean residual by half-hour, the previous day, and the partial current day was added, which can represent recipe R exactly. The neural network recovered -10% of the level step and -0.1% of the Saturday shift; R recovered 0% and -9%. The absorption test (training with the shift in four of the eight weeks, evaluating the other four on clean inputs, correction minus the clean-trained network's) gave 2.2% on clean weeks, below the 25% limit and 2.6% on injected weeks, so the network did not memorise the calendar, but it did not adapt either.
    • Fit quality on the tuning months. Day-ahead mean absolute error of the median on three held-out month blocks (purged by 15 days): network 3.97, 4.07, 4.43 against K0 4.02, 4.06, 4.57 points, a reduction of 0% to 3% (the mean of two seeds per block; the second block's reduction is about 0%). Over the two seeds, the validation error exceeds the training error by -0.2%, 12%, and 31% on the three blocks; the three blocks have about 700 training samples each.
    • Arms and rows not run. SEQ-median, SEQ-quantile, SEQ-ridge, and SEQ-blind were not scored in the replicate months or the scored year, as the plan requires after a failed gate. The code for the fits and frames is in the folder but was not run.
    • Predictions. P1 to P5 and P7 were not tested. P6 held for the replicate-type fit (the validation error exceeded the training error by more than 10% in two of three blocks).
    • Checks. No audit trigger fired except trigger 1 (a failed control), whose explanation is the small training set and whose investigation is the first-architecture and second-architecture comparison above.
    • Caveats. The gate period (April to June 2024) is spring, not winter. The skip path could represent recipe R, so a failure to recover the shift means the optimiser found no use for it in about 1,100 noisy samples, not that the architecture could not express it.
    • What to try next. Train on longer history (the 2021 to 2024 ensemble backfill, if the maintainer approves its use) or on simulated residuals, and re-run this gate before any score.
