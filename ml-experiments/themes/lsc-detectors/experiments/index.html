    LSC detectors: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00332 lsc-curtailment-cost-of-label-free-mask Treating as missing the readings that a label-free load shift detector flags (arm A of experiment 327) lowers curtailment cost during and after load shifts, against no mask (arm C), without raising it on ordinary half-hours. not supported not-for-production Masking the load shifts that a label-free detector finds lowers curtailment cost by about a third when the forecast is judged against the normal running arrangement. Judged against the power the electricity network actually carried, which is what a curtailment instruction responds to, the mask changes cost by -3.0% with an interval from -24% to +30%, and it costs 1.3% more on ordinary half-hours. Adding the true moved megawatts to the true spans lowered cost during a shift, but adding them to the label-free mask did not, so this result cannot say what a live service needs. primary
    00331 primary-demand-recency-weights-on-gam-and-kalman When the additive (GAM) model with a Kalman filter on its daily residual is refitted monthly, giving older training rows exponentially less weight forecasts the next month better than equal weights on the full history, and no worse than training on the last 24 months only. not supported not-for-production Training the monthly refit mostly on recent data improves the next month's forecast by about 0.025 points of capacity, but a plain 24-month window gets the same gain, so smooth recency weights add nothing and no weights should go into the research default. Half of the gain comes from one Primary substation whose demand shifted in 2023. Giving extra weight to rows from the same time of year in earlier years gave a further 0.012 points and is worth its own test. This is a lab result on past weather with a Kalman filter that already adapts to level changes. also relevant
    00328 lsc-regime-changes-detect-then-model-part-1 A permanent change in the configuration of an electricity network (a large new generator, a new factory) can be found offline from a Primary's own history, and a forecast that keeps the effects of the old behaviour fitted on the full history and adds new effects that exist only from the change onward scores better after the change than the same forecast refitted on a recent window only. not supported not-for-production Adding effects for a detected permanent change does not forecast better than refitting the additive model on the last 24 months, in a lab setting where the model is given past weather. The detectors find a 10% step of a Primary's load within 60 days only 26% of the time at an acceptable false-alarm rate, so there is little time after the alarm for the extra effects to help. On this base, handling a regime change comes down to how often the model is refitted. The rolling-GAM detector might still help when building substation cards, which this experiment did not test. primary
    00327 lsc-label-free-detect-mask-train-pipeline A forecast of the normal running arrangement (NRA) can be trained and corrected on power with the load shifts set to missing, where a label-free detector finds the load shifts. The detector reads only how unusual the observed power is inside the predictive bands of a demand model that uses no lagged power, and no NGED switching label enters any threshold, mask, or training row. partly supported candidate A forecast of the normal running arrangement recovers faster after a load shift when the readings that a label-free detector flags are treated as missing: during and after injected shifts the 95% pinball loss falls from 1.79 to 1.04 points of capacity. On ordinary days the masked forecast is no worse, and slightly better. Without one site (D11) the gain shrinks to 0.30 points, and retraining the demand model on masked power adds nothing beyond masking the filter. primary
    00315 lsc-neighbour-residual-filter A real load shift moves load between neighbouring Primary substations, so the 24-hour mean residuals at the two substations have opposite signs, while weather and forecast error give the same sign at neighbours. Keeping a 24-hour mean residual alarm only when a neighbour does not contradict it therefore cuts the alarms per matched shift at equal recall. small gain not-for-production Checking whether neighbouring substations' residuals contradict an alarm removes about a third of the 24-hour residual detector's false alarms and loses none of the real load shifts it found. The gain in alarms per found shift is about a quarter, which is a real but modest improvement, and the two substations that raise 17% to 20% of its false alarms (D8 and D9) keep the same number. The filter is usable for event labels for NGED, but it does not change the detector's accuracy much. primary
    00312 lsc-how-good-must-a-detector-be-against-the-band-head A load shift detector that is far from perfect can still beat the best forecast that uses no detector, so a detector need not be near-perfect to be worth building. The one detector-based arm in experiment 297 that beat that forecast was the one that subtracts the true megawatts from the readings during a shift and adds them back to the forecast. This experiment degrades that arm one axis at a time to find where it stops beating the no-detector forecast. It also asks whether the surprise of recent readings against GAM_ONLY's residual bands (band position), or perfect knowledge of a shift, added as inputs to the no-detector quantile head, improves the head. This is question 3 of the LSC decision program, redefined by the Discuss ruling of 2026-10-23. not supported not-for-production A load shift detector does not need to be near-perfect to improve on the best forecast that uses no detector: with the true megawatts added back, finding half the shifts, learning of each up to 3 days late, or misjudging the megawatts by half still lowers curtailment cost against that forecast. The detector tested earlier (RO-NC) finds only a third of the shifts, below the break-even of one half (measured with every other property perfect). The surprise of recent readings against the forecast's bands (band position) found more, 37% of shifts on even months, but still under one half and with about 90% of its alarms false, so the next step is a detector that finds more shifts. primary
    00309 lsc-two-forecast-gap-choosing-without-truth NGED sees two forecasts and not the true switching state. A forecast with no lagged power (GAM_ONLY) forecasts the normal running arrangement, and a forecast with lags (K0) forecasts what will flow while an abnormal arrangement lasts. A rule that reads only the gap between the two forecasts, how long it has lasted, and a neighbour check can choose between them nearly as well as knowing the truth. This experiment is part 2 of the brief briefs/sw_lsc_two_forecasts_divergence_and_selection_brief.md, and it builds on experiment 308. not supported not-for-production NGED cannot pick between the two forecasts from the gap alone. A rule on the gap, or on the 24-hour mean of measured power minus the no-lag forecast, did no better than always using the no- lag forecast in the injected worlds, and worse than always using the forecast with lags in the real record. An oracle that knows the true switching state gained little, except in the 72 hours after a real shift ended, so the most promising signal to test next is a flag for the end of a shift. The gap is a filtered copy of the measured-power residual, so it adds no information that the measured power lacks. primary
    00308 lsc-two-forecast-gap-as-a-detector The gap between the forecast with lags (K0, the Kalman filter on recent power) and the forecast without lags (GAM_ONLY, which forecasts the normal running arrangement) at a forecast origin already works as a load shift detector. NGED cannot see the true switching state, so this gap is the only signal it has. This is part 1 of the brief briefs/sw_lsc_two_forecasts_divergence_and_selection_brief.md. supported not-for-production The gap between the two forecasts flagged 11 of 35 real load shifts while they were still on, about as many as the learned score (10) when both thresholds are set to the same false alarm rate on odd months. The median alarm came 41 hours after the onset. On the held-out even months the gap raised about twice the learned score's false alarms (1.21 against 0.55 per Primary-year). A 24-hour mean of recent forecast error flagged 20 of 35. Experiment 309 tests whether the gap can choose between the two forecasts. primary
    00302 lsc-learned-score-without-nged-labels Experiment 227's carried alarm source (the re-ranker of experiment 195 at a budget of 20 unexplained odd-month candidates, "LR_b20") uses NGED labels twice: its training negatives drop every logged or labelled span, every D8 drop, and the 17 block days, and its threshold counts "unexplained" candidates, which means no logged closure within 24 hours. A re-ranker trained on every real candidate as a negative, and thresholded by a rule that reads no label, still matches about as many of the 35 logged load shifts lasting over 1 day at about the same number of alarms per match, with ordinary-day harm inside the limit. not supported not-for-production A learned load-shift alarm trained without NGED labels matched 14 of the 35 logged load shifts, against 20 to 21 with labels, so the pre-registered test failed. The whole loss sits (post hoc) in load shifts that start in the months the score trained on, because every real candidate there, real load shifts included, was taught as a negative. On the 20 load shifts in months the score never saw, the label-free score found 13 to 14, against 13 for the labelled one, a difference too small to resolve with 20 load shifts. The next test should score a label-free score only on months it never trained on, with more than 20 load shifts. primary
    00298 lsc-long-deep-detector-and-refit-cleaning A trivial detector on the daily mean residual of the lagless convex additive model (GAM_ONLY) finds load shifts that last days to weeks and cover at least 10% of a Primary substation's load, which the existing hour-scale detectors miss, and excluding the days it flags from the monthly refit stops the forecast learning those shifts as the normal pattern. not supported not-for-production A simple detector on the daily mean error of the forecast that uses no recent power finds about as many of the long, deep real load shifts as the learned score LR_b20 (10 to 12 of 19 for four of the six variants). The cumulative-sum variant B also finds 27 of the 29 multi-day drops at D8, which are probably a gas engine and not load shifts; variant C finds 11. Leaving the days a detector flags out of the monthly refit cuts the share of a 30-day transfer that the forecast learns as normal from about 19% to about 10% to 13%. Excluding the true shift days instead cuts the share to 2.8%. The detectors recover less because each needs 1 to 3 days to fire and misses about 40% of long injected transfers and over half of the real ones. Tell NGED that transfers of 14 days or more are partly learned as normal, and try a detector that fires on the first day next. primary
    00297 lsc-need-a-detector-no-detector-arms-against-the-mask A forecast that never learns which readings are load shifts can match a forecast that uses a detector, because its bands and its recent-power correction can be made to tell a load shift from ordinary variation by themselves. This is question 2 of the LSC decision program. not supported not-for-production A forecast with no detector that recalibrates its bands and reads how far the recent-power correction has moved scores better than the RO-NC detector near load shifts, so with RO-NC a detector is not shown to be needed. Perfect knowledge with the true megawatts added back still lowers curtailment cost by 6.5% (3.9 to 9.2) against that forecast, so the next step is to run the better LSC detector (LR_b20) on the same simulated shifts and judge it against a forecast that has the recalibrated bands. primary
    00296 lsc-benefit-by-event-class-long-deep-versus-small-shifts Load shifts of at least 2 days and at least 10% of a Primary substation's load carry most of the benefit of knowing every load shift exactly, and shifts that are shorter than 12 hours or under 10% of load carry little. This is the question behind the "long-and-deep" detector of the LSC decision program. supported not-for-production A detector that finds only load shifts of at least 2 days and at least 10% of a substation's load would deliver about 96% of the upper-tail forecast benefit and about 90% of the curtailment-cost benefit of knowing every shift, if it were perfect. Detecting shifts from 6 hours to 2 days would add 3% to 8% more, and detecting shifts down to 5% of load would add 1% to 2%. The next step is to weight these shares by how often a real long-and-deep detector finds each class, which waits for Part 1 of the long-deep detector experiment. primary
    00295 regime-segmentation-part-b-persistent-regimes-without-labels A segmentation of a Primary's whole history with hindsight, using only changes in the distribution of the residual of a lagless convex additive model (GAM), finds regime changes without labels. A regime is the whole period, without gaps, during which one configuration holds. For D8's gas engine, the regime is the whole period in which the engine is installed and included in the meter, including the spells when the engine is off. Whether the engine is running, and whether a load shift is in force, are states inside a regime. A boundary is a regime change when the distribution does not return to its earlier form and no neighbouring Primary shows the opposite change, and a state change otherwise. not supported not-for-production Looking only at the residual of a forecast that uses no recent power, a segmentation finds a lasting change of 25% of a Primary's load and dates it with a median error of 0 days, but it misses about half of 10% changes and cannot reliably tell a 14 to 60 day load shift from a lasting change. At D8 it finds the 2023-08-15 break exactly, but only at penalties that also find 2.5 or more regime changes per Primary-year at the other Primaries. An offset for the latest regime lowers the forecast error after a step by 2.2 points of capacity, but a plain 60-day mean residual does as well, so the segmentation is not worth adding for that purpose. primary
    00294 regime-aware-model-at-d8-engine-state-term D8's metered power excludes a gas engine's output (experiment 194) while the engine, which runs in spells (experiment 291), takes net load below what the weather and calendar predict. A single model trained across those spells has no way to know which half-hours the engine ran, so its forecast and its bands carry the engine as noise. A model that keeps the calendar, weather, and solar shapes shared across the whole history but adds one regime-specific term, an offset per half-hour the engine runs (non-negative, taking power off net load), forecasts D8 better than the single model. The offset is estimated from the data, and its size is not assumed. not supported not-for-production Telling the forecast when D8's gas engine is running would make it much better when the state is read at the half-hour being forecast, but the detector builds that state from the very reading being forecast. With only the state of the last settled day, the engine term made the forecast 10% worse; trained on that stale state, it gained only 3%, no better than widening the bands from past residuals. A forecast of whether the engine runs, or NGED's own record of its running hours, would be the data to test next. also relevant
    00291 d8-drops-engine-spells-load-shift D8's 29 multi-day drops (experiment 159) are spells in which a gas engine of up to three units runs, and D8's own metered power no longer includes the engine's output (experiment 194). If so, the engine detector of experiment 196 flags the drops' half-hours far more often than matched windows elsewhere on D8, the drops' depths cluster at multiples of one engine unit, and most of D8's load-shift alarms that fall inside drops are engine edges. partly supported not-for-production D8's multi-day drops coincide with the engine detector's running states, which is consistent with the gas engine running: the detector flags 27 of the 28 drops that began after the engine left D8's reading, against about 1 in 5 comparable stretches of D8, and it flags almost nothing at three other Primaries on the same dates. This cannot be called proved, because the drops and the detector both read D8's residual, and the depths of the drops do not fall at whole multiples of the engine's 1.97 MW unit. D8's drops should be flagged as a probable generator regime and reported apart from the load-shift scoring until NGED confirms the engine's running hours, and the next step is a forecast model for D8 that is told when the engine is running. primary
    00290 lsc-long-shift-cut-date-robustness The 19% of a 30-day transfer that experiment 274 found learned as normal is stable across fit-cut dates, so that the share learned lies within 15% to 25% at every cut date. not supported not-for-production The forecast with no recent power learns about a fifth of a 30-day load transfer as normal, but the share depends on the date: it ranges from 9% to 29% across 12 end dates, so experiment 274's 2.49-point bias is a typical value, not a fixed one. Quote the share as a range of about 9% to 29%. The cause of the spread is not located. primary
    00283 lsc-perfect-knowledge-value-to-forecast-and-curtailment Knowing every load shift exactly (its true start and end) lowers the error of the forecast of the normal running arrangement (NRA) in the 48 hours after a shift, and lowers the curtailment cost of the forecast of observed power in the days around a shift, while leaving ordinary rows unchanged. This is question 1 of the LSC decision program. partly supported not-for-production Perfect knowledge of load shifts lowers the forecast's error most in the 48 hours after a shift ends (23% lower upper-tail score, 95% interval 9% to 39%) and by under 1% over a whole year, because shifts are rare. Curtailment cost against observed power falls by 8% (6% to 10%) near a shift only when the known shift's megawatts are added back to the forecast, and does not fall when recent readings are merely set aside. The next step is to ask how good a detector must be to keep that gain, using the megawatts-added-back arm as the target. primary
    00277 lsc-curtailment-metric-on-existing-forecasts The curtailment-cost metric of experiment 268 (studies.node_curtailment, sorted quantiles) ranks the four existing forecasts as the other metrics do, and can resolve the differences between them well enough to judge the load-shift questions of the decision program. supported not-for-production Over a year of ordinary days, the curtailment metric ranks the four existing forecasts the way the other scores do, and it can tell the GAM stack from BG2 (BG2 costs GBP 7.35 million more on the six Primaries at the 0.5 limit, a figure that ranks forecasts and is not money saved). Inside the windows around the 7 logged load transfers, the GAM stack cannot be separated from BG2, because those windows hold only 926 to 2,923 scored half-hours. Questions about load shifts need injected worlds with many more shifts, not the real record. also relevant
    00274 lsc-long-arrangement-audit-gam-refit A forecast with no lagged power learns a long-lasting change of running arrangement as the normal pattern, because the monthly refit trains on whatever the meter recorded. The learned share grows with the duration of the arrangement, and the duration beyond which the forecast of the normal running arrangement (NRA) is wrong by more than a stated tolerance sets the limit that can be told to NGED. not supported not-for-production A forecast that uses no recent power still learns a load transfer as normal, and the forecast learns the transfer quickly: a transfer lasting only 7 days leaves the forecast 1.2 points of effective capacity too high for the next 2 days, and a transfer lasting 30 days leaves it 2.5 points too high (about a fifth of the transfer). No tested duration, down to 7 days, was short enough to avoid this. Masking known transfers out of training is the obvious remedy, but this experiment did not test it, and the next experiment should test it together with transfers of 1 to 6 days. primary
    00271 lsc-load-shifts-net-out-at-bulk-supply-points A load shift between two Primary substations under the same bulk supply point (BSP) leaves the power flow at the BSP unchanged, so it barely changes the curtailment cost at the BSP, and only a load shift that crosses from one BSP to the other changes the cost noticeably. The step 7 program brief for the load-shift (LSC) decision program asks this in order to know whether the money question can be asked at BSP level. partly supported not-for-production A load shift between two Primary substations under the same bulk supply point changes the curtailment cost at that bulk supply point by 0.2% at a random time and 0.7% just before days that would be curtailed (interval up to 1.08%), because the two Primaries' forecast errors partly cancel. A load shift across the boundary between the two bulk supply points changes it by 1.4% at a random time and 5.5% just before curtailed days. Only 4 of the 54 hand-filtered real events that involve two Primaries cross the boundary, so the curtailment metric at bulk supply points cannot say whether detecting load shifts is worth money, and that question has to be asked at Primary substations. primary
    00268 lsc-curtailment-metric-v2-member-wise-ens A member-wise input path added to studies.node_curtailment (ensemble members of the node's demand and of each farm's output, paired by member, reduced to the quantiles the rule reads) gives the same avoidable cost as the quantile path when the members are drawn from the same quantile function, and scores nodes that experiment 255 could not (Primaries that connect a farm, and the bulk supply points D13 and D17 with member-wise inputs). partly supported not-for-production The curtailment metric can now take ensemble members for a substation's demand and for each solar farm, and on experiment 255's rows it reproduces the earlier answer. A third of the stored solar-farm forecasts list their quantiles out of order. Sorting them moves the cost of some forecasts by up to 3.7% and leaves every comparison between forecasts with the same sign, but it swaps which of two near-equal forecasts is cheapest. Forecasts that are already quantile sets can be scored now. Forecasts driven by ensemble members need member frames that we did not find in the experiment folders we searched. also relevant
    00258 lsc-mask-out-of-sample-all-primaries-whole-history Experiment 227 found that LR_b20 (the learned alarm score of experiment 195, at the threshold that leaves 20 unexplained odd-month candidates) with a mask recovers 83.5% of the logged-oracle gain in the 72 hours after a logged load shift and raises the ordinary-day score by -0.007 points (-0.039 to +0.014). That rests on 11 load shifts at 6 Primary substations, 27 months of cached ensemble forecasts, a threshold chosen on the scored rows, and months that were the re-ranker's training negatives. On all 15 Primary series with a residual from the frozen convex model of experiment 130 (arm B), over the whole residual history of about 4.5 years, and scored only on rows the learned score was not trained on, the LR_b20 mask keeps at least half of that gain and keeps its ordinary-day harm inside +/-0.05 points. inconclusive not-for-production On rows it was never trained on, the carried load shift mask recovers about half of the best-case gain after a load shift (47%, with a wide range of 18% to 85%) and changes ordinary days by less than 0.05 points. Over every row of 4.5 years it scores slightly worse than no mask, because a few long alarms over logged closures add more excess score than the mask removes elsewhere. The check against simulated load shifts failed, so the result is inconclusive, and this page gives no support for porting the mask. primary
    00254 lsc-alarm-precision-against-full-log-with-chance-base-rate Experiment 195's loose rule (a logged closure within 24 hours "explains" an alarm) overcounts real alarms, because a random window is explained about 45% of the time (experiment 159, which counts any time inside a closure or within 24 hours of it), and the strict rule (experiment 195's step-and- end match) undercounts them, because the log's times are approximate. Once the share expected by chance is removed, a large share of the alarms from LR_b20, the learned alarm source, that are not strict matches still coincide with a logged closure. supported not-for-production We estimate that about 45% of the alarms from the learned load shift screen at its strictest threshold (LR_b20) are real load shifts, with 2.7 false alarms for each logged load shift it finds. The looser sources, LR_b80 and RO_b80, which keep 80 candidate alarms, are real about 28% and 25% of the time. An event table built from LR_b20 would carry an estimated precision of about 45%. primary
    00253 lsc-end-time-pair-rule When a load shift moves demand between two metered Primary substations, the end of the shift is estimated better from the pair than from either substation alone, because the true end restores the pair's summed demand and an unrelated step on one substation does not. Using the pair removes most of the disagreement between the end times that the two substations' cards show (a real shift between D5 and D16 on 14 January 2026 is shown as ending after 9.2 days on one card and 2.0 days on the other). inconclusive not-for-production Reading the two substations together makes their end times agree when an unrelated step follows the true end: the median gap falls from 8.5 to 2.5 hours and the share of moved MW with a correct end rises by 0.04 (from 0.593 to 0.633). On ordinary transfers the same rule falls by about 0.02 (from 0.788 to 0.770), so the experiment cannot say the pair rule is safe to port. The pair rule does not change the end times of the real 14 January 2026 shift between substations D5 and D16, because D16 shows no demand step large enough to be the end, and none of D5's 13 candidate steps is matched by an opposite step on D16. primary
    00249 lsc-self-training-on-consistent-events The learned load-shift score of experiment 195 is trained only on injected transfers.
