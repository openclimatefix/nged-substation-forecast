    Solar disaggregation: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00289 summer-gap-weather-input-discriminator The best-guess stack's weaker April to June score than BG2 at the decision Primaries comes from the ensemble's sunshine forecast (or its ratio calibration), and not from demand absorbing the solar term or from the error-correction layers. partly supported not-for-production Giving the best-guess stack the sunshine that actually happened would close about 70% of its April to June gap to BG2, but the same change helps the stack nearly as much in July to September, when the stack already matches BG2. Most of the April to June gap beyond the July to September gap therefore stays. That points to the solar term in the demand model, or to the error-correction layers, and away from the ensemble sunshine forecast. A refit with realised sunshine as the solar driver, and a check of the solar term at D5 and D9 in April to June, are the next steps. also relevant
    00282 pv-plant-curve-audit The curve that turns sunshine into power in our solar models departs from the metered farms' measured curve by more than 5% of capacity somewhere in the clear-sky range, and the departure is the flat-top level (the inverter limit), not the panel tilt or the temperature loss. not supported not-for-production The curve that turns sunshine into solar power stays within 5 percentage points of capacity at 3 of the 6 metered solar farms and departs from their measured output at the other 3 in different directions, so no single fix to the inverter limit repairs it. The best-guess demand stack assumes panels tilted 35 degrees and the farms fit best at about 20 degrees, which makes the stack over-predict output on clear half-hours in December (measured is 0.66 of the stack, median over farms) and under-predict it from April to July by 5 to 8%. Swapping in the pooled shape of the other farms does not help, so the next experiment should try the 20 degree tilt inside the stack. also relevant
    00280 pv-empirical-shape-and-capacity-in-arm-d The April to June 2026 gap between arm D (the monthly-refit generalised additive model of experiments 155 and 220, scored on experiment 178's rows) and BG2 comes from its solar term: the pinned solar capacity is too high and the south-facing fleet shape is not the shape the metered farms show. Replacing the fleet shape by the pooled empirical shape of experiment 269, at the capacity that shape's real-series fit supports, lowers arm D's April to June error at the substations where the pinned capacity looks too high (D5, D9, and D16). not supported not-for-production Putting the empirical solar shape into the forecast, at the pinned capacity or at the lower capacity the real series supports, does not lower the April to June error at the substations where the pinned capacity looked too high. The change is under 0.01 MW against an error of about 0.5 MW, and the intervals are wide. A capacity cut that is correct for the whole of April to June is not the fix either: at D5 it raises the error. This experiment finds no sign that the shape or the capacity of the solar term closes the summer gap to BG2. also relevant
    00269 pv-capacity-gain-decomposition Most of the 0.090 gain that experiment 238 found from using a nearby farm's recorded output as the solar shape comes from the farm's plant shape (tilt, orientation, flat top) and from the capacity unit in which recovery is read, and little comes from the farm's measured sunshine. partly supported not-for-production For the five substations and six farms tested, the measured sunshine at a nearby farm explains almost none of the extra solar output that its recorded output helped to find. The extra output found comes from the farms' plant shape (panel tilt, panel orientation, and the flat top of the output curve). A table learned from other farms' output and driven by the satellite irradiance reproduces that plant shape. Using the table finds 0.966 of an injected solar farm's output when recovery is read as the capacity ratio of experiment 231 (0.826 with the physical fleet shape), and 0.995 when it is read over clear-sky daylight energy. In these tests the satellite irradiance was not the limit, and a metered farm beside each substation was not needed. Switching to the table lowers the fitted solar capacities of the five decision substations by 0.2 to 0.4 MW. The effect on the sunny-half-hour forecast score has not been run. also relevant
    00267 pv-capacity-early-years-and-register-floor The inferred capacity of unmetered solar at the five decision Primaries falls below the register floor in season-year 2021 (July 2021 to June 2022) because the capacity fit recovers only about 0.78 of an injected farm and the 2021 fit window is thin, not because the register is shared to the wrong district. Dividing by the median recovery of the metered farms, and using the output of a metered farm within 25 km as the sunshine shape, lifts the estimates to the floor. not supported not-for-production The 2021 shortfall at D10 and D11 is not a missing recovery correction and not a thin fit window: the card already divides by the metered farms' recovery, and the 2021 season-year has 12 months of data at every Primary. Using a nearby metered farm's output as the sunshine shape makes the shortfall no smaller (the estimates fall by 7% to 22%, partly because the two shapes are divided by different recoveries), so the shortfall at those two Primaries remains unexplained. Candidate causes are the register's postcode-district share, a gap of about 20 months in the early data, and a fit that is too low in its first full year. Show 2021 at D10 and D11 as a known shortfall. also relevant
    00266 summer-gap-diagnostics-solar-term The best-guess demand stack (experiment 251's arm 1) trails BG2, the production-style XGBoost forecast, in April to June 2026 mainly because of its explicit solar term (the subtracted estimate of unmetered rooftop solar output), and not because of the neural-network head, the two-number summary of the ensemble's solar response, the Kalman filter, or the heating and lighting terms. The same defect explains the loss at D5, an anonymised Primary substation, on sunny half-hours. partly supported not-for-production The best-guess stack trails BG2 in April to June because of the solar term at two of the six substations (D5 and D9 on daylight half-hours) and a night-time error at a third (D10), not because of the neural-network head, the two-number summary, the Kalman filter, or the ensemble sunshine forecast; the head and summary actually reduce the gap. The pinned solar capacity looks too high in those months, not too low, and clear mornings are forecast worse than clear afternoons. Candidate next experiments are the export clip at D9, a morning-and-afternoon solar shape, and a look at D10's night residuals; the stack's added parts need no change for this gap. also relevant
    00241 pv-capacity-calibrated-interval The recoveries that experiment 129 measured when it injected real solar farms into a Primary substation's power can be turned into a calibrated estimate of unmetered solar capacity: dividing the capacity found by the Primary's median recovery over the other farms corrects its bias, and the spread of those recoveries gives an 80% interval that covers the truth about 80% of the time. partly supported not-for-production Dividing the solar capacity found by the typical recovery of other real farms removes most of the bias: the estimate lands within 10% of the truth at all five Primaries, against a typical error of 19% without the correction. The 80% interval built the same way covers the truth 59% to 79% of the time, which is too uncertain to label as an 80% interval, and it fails completely when the demand follows cloud (0% to 2%). Keep showing the range as a spread over scenarios, with the correction applied. also relevant
    00239 pv-capacity-seasonal-efficiency-curve The metered solar farms' output falls 6% to 9% from spring to summer by a pattern that no clip or heat term explains. A seasonal efficiency curve of 12 monthly factors, shared by all farms, fitted on the farms' own output, and then applied to the solar shape in a Primary substation's capacity fit, lets that fit find more of an injected real solar farm. not supported not-for-production Twelve monthly efficiency factors fitted on the metered farms' output do not close the recovery shortfall. The farm-only fit rises by 0.003, below its target, and the recovery of an injected farm rises by 0.039 or 0.025 depending on whether the injected farm keeps its low-sun output. The farms fall below the satellite shape in November to January, and show no fall of 6% to 9% from spring to summer. Keeping the injected farm's low-sun output, which experiment 231 zeroed, raises the recovery by 0.033 (paired median over farms) on its own. also relevant
    00238 pv-capacity-nearest-farm-sunshine-reference Using the recorded output of a metered solar farm within 30 km of a Primary substation as the measure of sunshine, in place of the CAMS satellite irradiance, lets the capacity fit of experiment 129 find more of an injected real solar farm. partly supported not-for-production A nearby farm's measured output finds more of an injected solar farm than the satellite irradiance does: 0.867 against 0.780 of its output, and at least 0.05 more at all five Primaries. The gain is 0.02 to 0.14 when the two farms are 5 to 25 km apart and 0.17 to 0.24 for the one pair of farms 2 km apart, and it falls to 0.052, reaching 0.05 at 3 of 5 Primaries, when recovery is read in the capacity unit of experiment 231. The farm may carry plant effects as well as sunshine, so the result does not yet show that the satellite irradiance is the limit. also relevant
    00236 pv-capacity-clear-sky-fit Fitting the capacity of the unmetered solar behind a Primary substation only on half-hours with a clear sky and a high sun, where the irradiance error is smallest, finds more of an injected real solar farm than the fit of experiment 129 on all rows. refuted not-for-production Fitting the solar capacity only on clear, high-sun half-hours did not find more of an injected solar farm: 0.818 against 0.827 for all rows, with an interval 1.8 times wider. The farm alone fits better on those half-hours (0.965), so the demand terms seem to absorb more of the solar signal when only clear half-hours are used (0.147 against 0.104). Do not restrict the rows by sky condition; reduce demand absorption instead. also relevant
    00235 pv-capacity-matched-day-pairs The capacity of unmetered solar behind a Primary substation can be read from the difference in net power between a sunny day and a cloudy day of the same day type and the same temperature, because ordinary demand cancels in the difference. That estimator should find more of an injected real solar farm than the convex fit of experiment 129 (arm 2), which finds 0.78 to 0.88. refuted not-for-production Pairing sunny and cloudy days removed none of a synthetic load that follows cloud, so the pairs give no evidence that a real cloud-following demand cancels. With the load added, the load adds 0.3 of the injected capacity to the pair estimator's recovery, against 0.2 for the convex fit, and a December-and-January test finds 0.33 of the farm. Pairs of days with at least 10 hours of daylight find 0.91 of the farm, which agrees with experiment 231's finding that winter rows weaken the fit. The load is built to follow the pairing variable exactly, so 0.3 is a worst case. also relevant
    00231 pv-capacity-audit-where-recovery-goes Of the roughly 0.2 of an injected solar farm's capacity that the capacity estimator of experiment 129 (arm 2) does not find, most is lost because demand terms absorb part of the solar signal, a quarter or more is lost to the irradiance input, and neither the winter rows nor the definition of capacity (the divisor) explains much. partly supported not-for-production The capacity estimator misses 0.17 of an injected solar farm mainly for two reasons: the demand terms of the fit absorb part of the solar signal (58% of the miss), and the fixed solar shape does not reach the farm's flat top (47%). Better sunshine data does not help, because fitting the farm alone on clear half-hours finds no more than fitting it on all daylight. The next experiments should target demand absorption and the shape's seasonal flat top, not the irradiance input. also relevant
    00218 metered-solar-farm-plant-fit The six metered solar farms differ in panel tilt, panel azimuth, ratio of direct-current to alternating-current capacity, heat loss, and inverter clipping, so a plant model with those parameters fitted per farm reproduces each farm's output better than the one fleet shape (tilt 35 degrees, facing south, DC:AC ratio 1.15, 0.4% loss per kelvin, cell temperature equal to air temperature plus 25 K times the shape), and explains why the flat top of farms C, D, E, and F falls 6% to 9% from spring to summer (experiment 183). not supported not-for-production Giving each metered solar farm its own panel tilt, azimuth, heat loss, and inverter limit fits its output only 0.003 better than one fleet shape (a fraction of output fitted of 0.953 against 0.950), so the plant is probably not what limits the fit. About 5% of the squared metered output stays unexplained, and the sunshine data is the likelier source. Per-farm plant parameters look unpromising for forecasting; a better sunshine forecast looks more promising, because the same plants fit only 0.91 with the ensemble forecast. also relevant
    00215 dp-pv-step-scan-real-series-register-match The step path of experiment 207 (total-variation weight 3, April to August rows), run on the real 2019 to 2026 series of the Primaries, finds real changes in solar capacity, and the steps that increase capacity fall near the dates when feed-in tariff solar installations were commissioned in the Primary's area. partly supported not-for-production The method that finds jumps in solar capacity finds about as many in the real Primary series as it invents when none exists, so we cannot call any of them real. The only list of dated installations we hold ends in March 2021, before the months the fit uses, so no change could be checked against it. The changes do not alter the forecast, but they should not appear on the substation cards as confirmed. A list covering 2021 to 2026, or the metered record of a known installation, would settle the question. also relevant
    00207 dp-meter-regime-steps-e2-step-capacity-known-answer A convex solar-capacity path that allows a few sudden jumps (a total-variation penalty on the month-to-month change) finds both the size and the date of a known solar-farm step added to a Primary's power, where a smooth path blurs the step and a path that steps once a year places it within the wrong month. partly supported not-for-production A solar capacity that is free to change each month finds a known solar-farm step added to a Primary's power, to within 2% of its size at the pre-registered penalty, and places it within one month of the true date. At that penalty the fit also invents steps above 0.5 MW in a year without any, at 2 of 5 Primaries, and a stronger penalty that avoids them still finds 89% to 96% of the step. A capacity that changes once a year finds only 24% to 49% of the step. also relevant
    00206 dp-meter-regime-steps-e1-farm-outages-in-primary When a metered solar farm's meter reads zero for days while the sun is high, the farm's output stays in the Primary substation's supplied power, so the Primary reads more solar generation (lower net power) during the outage than in the 14 days either side. not supported not-for-production When a metered solar farm's meter reads zero for days, the power of the Primary substation we could test (D10) does not change by that farm's output. Meter failures at farms A and C therefore do not explain capacity steps at D10, and the other decision Primaries could not be tested. The farms may connect to substations we could not test, so the connection list from NGED would settle it. also relevant
    00183 metered-farm-flat-top-cause The flat top at farms C, D, E, and F is a fixed export cap, so its level does not change with season or with the sun's azimuth. not supported not-for-production The flat top of solar farms C, D, E, and F is lower in summer than in spring (by 6% to 9% of its mean level at the 90th percentile, in even and odd years alike), so the flat top does not behave like a fixed export cap. The per-farm plant fit should therefore fit panel tilt, panel azimuth, and a loss that grows with heat or a gradual flattening near the inverter limit, not only one clip level. More cloud in summer half-hours could lower the 90th percentile, because the clear-sky filter is modelled and not observed; the 99th percentile falls by less (1% to 6%). primary
    00182 switching-d8-disaggregated-farms-and-drops D8 (Marsh Lane) is a disaggregated Primary: the metadata says the output of two solar farms was added back to its reading, and that both farms' analogues are not working. When an analogue fails, D8's reading loses that farm's output, so D8's multi-day drops (experiment 159) coincide with a stuck, zero, or missing reading at the farms, and the drop depth matches the farms' lost output. inconclusive not-for-production We cannot tell from the farms' own readings whether D8's multi-day drops are the two disaggregated farms dropping out, because the readings for the farms are almost entirely absent from the power table and so look the same inside and outside the drops. D8's shortfall is larger in daylight than at night in 24 of 29 drops, which is consistent with lost solar output, but it is also large in the evening, so the daylight pattern does not settle it. The next step is to ask NGED for the farms' output and capacity, or to model the farms from weather and compare the modelled output with the drop depth. also relevant
    00179 metered-solar-farm-exploration At least 4 of the 6 metered solar farms show AC clipping, meaning output that flattens below the level that clear-sky irradiance alone would give, so that the AC limit can be separated from the DC capacity. not supported not-for-production Four of the six metered solar farms have output that flattens at about 1.05 times their effective capacity once clear-sky irradiance passes about 650 W/m² (read off Figure 1), but the pre-registered test was too strict to say so, and the flat top could be an export cap (a limit on grid export set by the network operator), a panel-angle effect, or a summer-heat effect rather than a limit in the inverters. Before any DC-versus-AC follow-up, check each farm's export-cap record and get an independent DC capacity. primary
    00157 differentiable-physics-joint-pv-capacity-known-answer Letting the convex demand model (studies.dp_gam) fit the solar capacity as a smooth non-negative path across months, with a penalty on growth, finds more of a known solar farm's injected output than the two-stage capacity of experiment 129, which steps once per season-year and was recovered at 0.78 to 0.88. not supported not-for-production In this test, letting the solar capacity change month by month did not find more of an injected solar farm than a capacity that changes once a year. For the five farms tested, one scale of the fixed fleet solar shape fits a median of 93% of a farm's output, so the target of 0.95 to 1.05 was out of reach for most farms before the fit ran. No forecast was run, and the monthly capacity is not worth taking to the forecast stage. also relevant
    00152 dp-mva-fold-aware-gam-k-at-mva-only-primaries At the MVA-only Primaries, the GAM-K forecast driven by the ensemble mean (experiment 136's arm A2) forecasts the MVA reading better when it models the fold: it fits the demand model through the meter (experiment 110's L0), corrects the forecast with a Kalman filter whose state is the signed flow, and builds its quantiles by folding a signed predictive distribution. The gain is concentrated at Stickney (D12), the one trial Primary where reverse flow is the usual state on sunny middays, and the change is harmless at the Primaries without reverse flow. inconclusive not-for-production At Stickney (D12), the one Primary with clear reverse flow, drawing the quantiles around a signed flow and folding them at zero lowered the score by about 0.4 points on sunny middays against the same fit with symmetric quantiles, and it did no harm at the other seven Primaries. The whole-year gain against reading the apparent power as signed power is of similar size, but its interval in the scored year includes zero. The known-answer test on five Primaries with hidden farms passed on the sign at the larger injection and failed at the smaller injection and on the upper tail's coverage, so the experiment does not support a port yet. also relevant
    00139 differentiable-physics-pv-as-of-capacity-in-gam-k Feeding the convex model with the Kalman filter (GAM-K, experiment 111) the as-of solar capacity of experiment 129 in place of experiment 45's pinned capacity lowers the quantile score on the sunniest third of half-hours, and does not raise it on the darkest third. The mechanism is that the pinned capacity is a hindsight growth prior, and the as-of capacity is read from each Primary's own power before the forecast origin. refuted not-for-production Replacing the pinned solar capacity with a capacity read from each substation's own power did not improve the forecast, and made sunny half-hours worse by 0.11 points of capacity. A more accurate capacity (the hindsight arm) only matched the pin, so the accuracy of the capacity number is not what limits the solar part of the forecast. This experiment does not locate the remaining sunny-day error; it did not test the ensemble's cloud forecast, the solar shape, the Kalman filter, or the non-solar load. also relevant
    00129 differentiable-physics-pv-capacity-known-answer-test The AC capacity of the unmetered solar behind each of the five decision Primaries can be inferred offline, one value per September-to-August season-year, from CAMS irradiance, ERA5 temperature, and the Primary's net power, with an 80% range that holds its coverage when the real panels differ from the assumed south-facing shape and when a day-long cloud-driven demand is present. partly supported not-for-production The capacity of the small solar panels behind five Primary substations can be read from the substations' power. For 2025/26 the arm 2 estimate is 3.3 to 4.5 MW of AC capacity per Primary, and it grew by 0.4 to 2.8 MW since 2021/22. The range around each estimate is 1.2 to 1.6 MW wide, but it is a spread over test scenarios and not a calibrated 80% interval. The method has not been shown to work on real panels that face other ways or on overcast-day demand, so a card should show the range with those caveats. also relevant
    00113 differentiable-physics-n5-convex-identification-at-realistic-noise On synthetic substations built from the real calendar and weather, with noise as large and as autocorrelated as the convex model's real residuals, the convex model's 80% month-block bootstrap intervals cover the truth 70 to 90% of the time, separately for each hour band's heating slope and for the latest season-year's PV capacity. Labelled separately, not in the verdict: the day-of-year harmonics and the drift term make those intervals wide, by absorbing the seasonal cycles of temperature and clear-sky PV. refuted not-for-production At real noise levels, the convex model's 80% month-block bootstrap intervals contain the true heating slope and the true rooftop PV capacity 84% of the time each, but a heating slope is weak (the interval is about 38% of the slope wide). The pre-registered verdict is refuted, because with independent noise the capacity interval covers the truth only 52% of the time, and the cause is mainly a bias of about 0.24 points from the drift penalty at its tuned step of 3,000 (at Part 4's step of 30 the bias is 0.02 points and coverage 78%, a post hoc check). The truth here has the model's own form, so the test cannot show the bias that a wrong PV shape causes. also relevant
    00110 differentiable-physics-5-embedded-generation-and-capacity The convex differentiable-physics model can represent the generation side of a Primary substation without making its demand forecast worse: an MVA meter through an observation layer, a flat unmetered generator found where one exists and not invented where none does, and an export-limited PV clip; and its PV capacity stays the same when the routes by which demand terms can imitate solar are closed. inconclusive not-for-production The demand forecast could not yet account for generators connected behind a substation. Rooftop solar capacity fitted from the data was not precise or consistent enough to trust, so the capacity from outside estimates stays in use. A generator-finding component reported a generator at every substation tested, so it stays switched off. A limit on exported solar helped only at the one substation known to export-limit. Treating the meter as apparent power improved the forecast at the 8 substations that report only apparent power by 0.13 points of capacity, but the test of the meter's flow direction failed, so that gain is provisional. also relevant
    00104 differentiable-physics-2-simple-structural-model A convex demand-minus-solar model, trained on every usable year with a slowly drifting level per hour band, forecasts Primary demand better than the same model trained on the last 12 months. inconclusive not-for-production A simple model of demand minus solar, trained on 12 months, scored 0.25 points better than lagless XGBoost with its default start (post hoc; a lower score is better). Most of that lead is in the forecast spread, because the error of the mean forecast differs by only 0.08 points, and four of the five pre-registered positive controls failed, so the verdict is inconclusive. More years of history did not clearly help: the scored year and the tuning year gave opposite signs for two history-window contrasts, and a first version of this page had the wrong sign for both because its spread carried the previous year's mean bias. also relevant
    00103 differentiable-physics-1-data-look The trial area's power records hold enough clean, demand-only history for the series to go ahead as designed: 6 MW Primaries with at least 12 months before July 2025, and 8 MVA Primaries with over 3 site-years each of rows with no sunlight. supported not-for-production The data lets the series go ahead, with three changes: start 13 of the 16 Primaries later than their first record (19.4 months later at D4), fix the lockdown window in advance because the data does not isolate it from drift, and count eligible long-history MW sites at each tuning year's first day (5 at 2024-07-01, so 4 of 5 must improve in Part 2's tuning contrast). The power timestamps show no 30-minute error on either side of the 2026-03-26 fix: at the 6 metered photovoltaic series the median offsets are +0.9 minutes before it and +4.7 after, and the correction is right to within 3 minutes, but offsets at the Primaries are not resolved beyond 0 to +8 minutes. also relevant
