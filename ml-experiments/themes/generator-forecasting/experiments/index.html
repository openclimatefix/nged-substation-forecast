    Generator forecasting: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00326 wind-research-default-1-change-arms Each of four changes, added alone to Wind farms research default 1, lowers the quantile score on the kept rows of the three metered wind farms W1 to W3 at 1 to 10 day leads: (a) 2 m temperature and an air-density column as XGBoost inputs, (b) hour of day and day of year as XGBoost inputs, (c) the ERA5-trained neural adapter's effective wind speed as an extra XGBoost column, and (d) one wind-speed scale factor, fitted on the hindcast to the implied wind, applied to every member's 100 m speed before the XGBoost model. At most one is adopted, in the order c, b, a, d. supported candidate Feeding XGBoost the neural adapter's corrected wind speed lowers the wind forecast's score by 0.10 points of capacity, about 0.9% of the default's score, at 1 to 10 day leads (99.75% interval -0.19 to -0.01). The change is negative at every farm and at short and long leads. The three other changes were not separable from zero. Only a port that puts the adapter in the production service can use the gain, so the maintainer decides. primary
    00324 wind-farm-solar-cross-breeds-long-run The solar-farm findings (a mixture-density network (MDN) fed by a physical plant forecast beats the plant alone, lead features help it, and one pooled head across leads matches separate heads per lead band) carry over to the three metered wind farms W1 to W3, when the plant is the physics core with the experiment 285 wind-speed adapter and the weather is the 51-member ensemble. partly supported not-for-production A small neural network that corrects the quantiles of the wind farms' physics-based forecast lowers the quantile score by 0.27 points of capacity against XGBoost plus a lookup table of offsets, on the half-hours when the farm is not idle, at days 1 to 2 and at days 3 to 10. The gain disappears when the idle half-hours are included. The upper tail gets no better. The network needs the farm's own forecast history to learn from. primary
    00313 metered-farm-pure-xgboost-and-mdn-vs-plant A predictor built only from weather and calendar inputs, with no plant model anywhere in the pipeline (not even as an input column), scores close to the plant-based forecast in own history and falls behind it where the plant carries what missing history or a long lead cannot. partly supported candidate A forecast that sees only weather and the calendar, with no physical plant model anywhere, scores almost as well as the plant-based forecast for the six solar farms, up to 10 days ahead, even when the farm has no history. The plant's added value is small, between 0% and 3.4%, and its intervals include zero except for XGBoost. For wind, the same kind of network also matches the best plant-based forecast, so a plant model is not needed to be competitive on these farms. also relevant
    00307 wind-farm-power-as-wind-sensor The wind that the three metered wind farms' own power implies (the fitted physics core's power curve run backwards) is closer to a high-resolution analysis than to ERA5 at 9 to 14 m/s, and at least half of the ensemble's shortfall (observed power below the forecast's median on 34.1% of rows instead of 50%) is in the spread and timing of the members, not in their mean speed. not supported not-for-production The ensemble's low wind-power forecast comes mostly from wind speeds that are too slow, by about 1.1 m/s on average and more at longer leads. Multiplying every member's wind speed by a factor fitted on the 14 months before the scored year closed about 78% of the gap to the ERA5 oracle, while widening the members or shifting them in time closed almost none. The median is slightly better calibrated when the factor is fitted to the wind that the farms' power implies than when it is fitted to ERA5 on the same rows (1.7 percentage points), but the quantile score is not better. primary
    00303 wind-adapter-in-production-setting Experiment 285 found that, with ERA5 as the weather, a small neural adapter in front of the physics core of a wind farm scored better than XGBoost. On the 51 ensemble members in the scored year, an adapter fine-tuned on the ensemble hindcast beats XGBoost plus experiment 259's offset table (the benchmark that matters), because the adapter corrects the wind speed itself and the table corrects only the power. partly supported not-for-production A small neural correction of the wind speed, fitted to ERA5 and run on the 51 ensemble members, does about as well as XGBoost with a lookup table of offsets (0.09 points better, too little to justify porting). Fitting the correction on the one year of ensemble hindcasts made the forecast worse, probably because that year differs from the scored year; we did not test the cause. primary
    00288 pooled-head-on-public-wind-farms A neural head that adds 11 quantile offsets to the XGBoost wind forecast (R0) and was trained on dozens of public onshore wind farms (metered output from Elexon's Balancing Mechanism Reporting Service) lowers the quantile score of the three metered wind farms (W1 to W3) against R0 alone, and a head trained on the pool beats the same head trained on W1 to W3 only, because experiment 259's head had too few farm-years to learn a correction that transfers. A second hypothesis: XGBoost keeps improving as the training history grows from 12 to 48 months while the physics core is flat after 24 months, and a pool of public farms replaces the missing history. not supported not-for-production Training the correction on 40 public wind farms did not help the forecasts for the three NGED wind farms. Applied directly, it made them worse, because the public farms' capacity, turbine rating, and hub height let the head memorise each farm. Without those inputs it did no harm, and after a short fine-tune on the three farms' own data it gained about as much as a simple offset table fitted on those farms, so the public farms added nothing measurable. primary
    00285 wind-effective-wind-adapters A small bounded correction of the wind speed that the physics core of a wind farm reads, learned from the wind direction, the 10 m to 100 m speed ratio, the 2 m temperature, and the hour of day, closes at least half of XGBoost's lead over the core on the three metered wind farms (W1, W2, W3) when ERA5 is the weather. A correction of each ensemble member's wind speed towards ERA5's removes the ensemble's low bias. partly supported candidate The physics model of a wind farm matched XGBoost once a small neural network adjusted the wind speed it takes as input, so XGBoost's lead can be reproduced by a smooth correction of wind speed. On the ensemble weather, mapping each member's wind speed to ERA5's helped by 0.31 points but fixed only a small part of the ensemble's low bias, and a lookup table of power offsets by lead day helped more. primary
    00276 wind-xgboost-vs-physics-gap-diagnostics The XGBoost model beats the physics core on the three metered wind farms (W1, W2, W3) because it learns the metering noise (the reactive-power floor of the apparent-power series, the deadband, and the dip near cut-in) that the physics core ignores. refuted not-for-production XGBoost beats the physics model of the wind farms because it corrects the wind speed using the wind direction, the 10 m wind, and the hour of day. It does not learn the metering noise: the lead is as large on rows without idle readings as on all rows. No change to the physics model's power curve, spread, or scale closed more than about a sixth of the gap, so the next step is a flexible correction to the physics model's wind speed. primary
    00262 wind-aifs-members-and-climatology-by-lead For the three metered wind farms (W1 to W3), a lead-dependent mixture of the ECMWF ensemble's member forecasts, the AIFS ensemble's member forecasts (both run through experiment 230's ERA5-trained XGBoost wind model A0r), and climatology lowers the quantile score and the 0.99 pinball loss against the ECMWF members alone, after both are corrected by a table of offsets per lead day, quantile, and farm; and a small neural gate that reads the two ensembles' spread and disagreement chooses better weights than weights by lead alone. partly supported candidate Adding the AIFS ensemble's wind forecasts to the ECMWF ensemble's improves the wind forecast at 3 to 6 days ahead, by about 5% of the quantile score, but does not improve the high-power tail that matters for curtailment. Climatology and a small neural network that picks the mixing weights add nothing once the table of offsets has corrected the common low bias. The AIFS arms also need testing on a second year, because one year of Tuesday runs sets the weights. primary
    00261 wind-low-bias-decomposition The low bias in the three metered wind farms' forecast (observed power below the physics core's median on 34% of rows, not 50%) comes mostly from the ensemble's weather input, not from the core, the farms, or the mixing of the 51 members. partly supported not-for-production The wind forecast's low median comes mostly from the ensemble's weather: its 10 m and 100 m wind speeds are slower than ERA5's, and the same physics core fed ERA5 wind has 52.8% of rows below its median. Mixing the 51 members adds about a tenth of the shift, and training the physics core and the XGBoost model on 9 months of ensemble forecasts removes between a fifth and a third of the gap. The fix to test next is a correction of the ensemble's wind speeds before the model reads them. primary
    00259 wind-plant-plus-quantile-offset-head For the three metered wind farms (W1 to W3), a small neural network that reads the 51-member mixture of a wind model and the ensemble's wind summaries, and adds an offset to each of 11 quantiles, lowers the quantile score against the wind model alone (R1, the physics core), matches the XGBoost best guess (R0), and lowers the pinball loss at the 0.99 quantile, once it can learn from daily runs of the ensemble forecast over 15 months (about 440 runs) instead of the 13 to 40 Tuesday runs that experiment 221's head saw. partly supported candidate A correction learned from 15 months of daily ensemble forecasts lowered the wind forecast's quantile score by 4.5% to 5.5% on the three metered wind farms, but the neural network and the ensemble's wind summaries were not what earned it. The 51 ensemble members' median sits too low: the observed power was below the physics core's median on 34% of rows, not 50%. A lookup table with one offset per lead day, quantile, and farm recovers 92% of the gain. The open question is where that low bias comes from. primary
    00256 wind-temperature-density-shear-columns Adding 2 m air temperature (arm T1) to the six wind columns of the XGBoost wind model lowers the quantile score on the three metered wind farms (W1 to W3), because air density and stability change the power a turbine produces at a given forecast wind speed. A density column (T2) carries the same information as temperature, and a shear and solar-elevation column set (T3) adds a further gain at night. partly supported candidate Giving the XGBoost wind forecast the air temperature helps a little, about 0.7% of the score, and the evidence just misses the bar we set beforehand. Columns that carry the time of day and the season help more, and their gain sits in daytime and summer rows. That pattern is not explained by the air density we could test (a function of temperature alone) or by shear, and it is consistent with a correction for biases in the ensemble wind at certain hours and seasons. A repeat on other months would show whether the time-of-day and season columns still help. primary
    00242 wind-physics-core-part-3-member-tails For the three metered wind farms (W1, W2, W3), running each of the 51 ensemble members through an ERA5-trained wind model and mixing the 51 forecasts gives better 0.01 and 0.99 quantiles than running the model once on the ensemble mean of the weather inputs and applying the same spread factor, because the model is non-linear near cut-in and rated wind, so a model of the mean weather is not the mean of the model. supported not-for-production Keep running all 51 ensemble members through the wind model. Running the ensemble mean once raises the error at the 99% quantile by a factor of 2.1 for the physics model and 3.3 for XGBoost, probably because averaging the members removes the high winds that produce full output, and the single spread factor tried here does not repair that. The physics model and XGBoost differ by under 0.01 points on the tails, so the choice between them does not matter here. primary
    00240 no-xgboost-forecast-sprinkle-part-2 A small XGBoost residual model on the plant forecast's median error, an XGBoost spread model for the quantile offsets around it, or both, close at least half of the gap in quantile score between the XGBoost-free metered solar forecast (the plant fed the mean and spread of 51 ensemble members, experiment 224's arm 3) and the full XGBoost forecast (experiment 224's arm 5) at 12 to 36 hour leads. partly supported candidate For solar farms, the best way to add XGBoost to the physical plant forecast is to let XGBoost predict the spread around the plant's mean, not to correct the mean: that forecast scores 4.3% better than the plant alone and 1.6% better than full XGBoost, and wins at the upper tail that matters for curtailment. Correcting the mean with XGBoost made the quantile score 0.18 points worse, about as much as a model trained on shuffled residuals. For wind, experiment 230's XGBoost stage on the physics core closes 71% of the gap to full XGBoost at 12 to 36 hours, and 44% over all leads. also relevant
    00234 no-xgboost-forecast-audit-part-1 For Primary substation demand, a metered solar farm, and a metered wind farm, the best forecast that uses no XGBoost anywhere scores within 2% of the quantile score of the best forecast that uses XGBoost, at 12 to 36 hour leads. partly supported candidate An XGBoost-free forecast of Primary substation demand scores 7.5% better than BG2, the XGBoost best guess, at 12 to 36 hours ahead, on one year and six substations. The XGBoost-free solar and wind forecasts score 2.8% and 5.0% worse, and lose at the 0.01, 0.05, and 0.95 quantiles. Only the solar plant model's 0.99 quantile beats XGBoost, and that is the tail that matters for curtailment. The next experiment tests small XGBoost components inside the XGBoost-free solar and wind forecasts. also relevant
    00233 wind-physics-core-part-2-capacity-availability For the three metered wind farms (W1, W2, W3), the ratio of observed power to the power that the fitted physics core of experiment 230 expects, taken over the half-hours when the core expects rated power and averaged over a trailing 60 days, shows an injected capacity loss of 25% within 60 days of the loss starting, at every farm, and raises no alarm on a slow drift of the observed power of the size seen in the flat top of metered solar farms (experiment 183). not supported not-for-production A monitor that compares observed power with the wind-turbine model only in strong wind, where the power curve is flat, cannot show a 25% loss of capacity in time at any of the three wind farms, because strong-wind half-hours are rare: a trailing 60-day window holds enough of them to give a reading on only 21% of days at W1, 45% at W2, and 5% at W3. A variant added after the results were seen, which uses every half-hour at 20% of maximum output or more, finds a 25% loss with a median delay of 27 days at W1, 52 at W2, and 34 at W3, but a smooth 9% seasonal swing then raises false alarms, and a 10% loss is found at only 27% to 73% of start dates. The next step to test is a monitor on the whole power curve with a seasonal baseline; the real series also shows single months with low output (W2 in winter 2025 to 2026, W3 in 2024 to 2025) that nothing here explains. primary
    00230 wind-physics-core-part-1 For the three metered wind farms (W1, W2, W3), a physics core fitted by gradient descent (hub-height wind from the ERA5 100 m and 10 m speeds, an air-density correction, a smooth monotone power curve with a cut-out, and an idle layer for the reactive-power floor of the MVA series) run through the 51 ENS members scores within 0.1 points of capacity of the XGBoost best guess (experiment 33) on the quantile score, and the measured gap between the ERA5-input ceiling and a simple power curve is under 0.02 points, which would mean the 25 km weather cell, and not the turbine physics, limits the forecast. not supported not-for-production A physics model of a wind farm, fitted to the three metered farms, forecasts them slightly worse than the XGBoost best guess, so it should not replace it. Most of the forecast error comes from the error in the ensemble wind forecast, not from the power curve: with ERA5 wind as the forecast, the same physics core cuts the score by 43%. The idle layer and the small neural adapter add nothing, and training the physics core on ERA5 instead of the ensemble control member improves it by 0.4 points. primary
    00223 dp-bsp-generators-as-children-and-double-count-test The metered generators that NGED lists under a bulk supply point (BSP) are children of that BSP as much as the Primaries are: the BSP's reading is the Primaries' demand plus the unmetered load minus the generators' output. If the Primaries' readings already include a generator's output, adding that generator to the sum double-counts it. This experiment first tests, from the readings alone, which generators the BSP's remainder carries and which a Primary already carries, and then asks how much the BSP's forecast improves when the generators the BSP carries are summed as children. inconclusive not-for-production The two bulk supply points' readings move with the metered generators that NGED lists under them, and the Primaries' readings show only small coefficients on the same generators. The size of the movement is not the clean one-for-one that adding the generator to the sum needs, so the experiment did not test whether adding the generators improves the bulk supply point forecast. A better test is to separate the unmetered solar and the battery's price-driven dispatch first. also relevant
    00221 wind-correction-head-on-stored-predictions A small neural-network head, fed the nine quantiles of the wind best guess (experiment 33's ERA5-trained mixture over the 51 ENS members, with the power-curve base margin and the spread factor) plus the ensemble mean and spread of the 100 m wind speed and the direction sector, lowers the quantile score at lead days 1 and 2 against the best guess by at least 0.1 points of capacity. The head is pooled over the three farms and trained on earlier months only. inconclusive not-for-production Training a small neural network to correct our wind-farm forecast did not help at 1 to 2 days ahead: the best version scored 0.05 points better and the version that also sees the weather forecast scored 0.18 points worse, with intervals that include zero. With only three farms and 9 scored months, the network may learn the months it trains on and narrow the forecast band too far on the next month. The stored forecast's band was already near the best width. A different kind of correction, or many more farms, is the next thing to try. primary
    00210 wind-idle-noise-mva-artefact H0: the noise in the three metered wind series (W1 and W3 in MVA, W2 in MW) occurs only at low wind speed, because the meters have a deadband, the reactive power drifts, and reactive power control runs only while the turbines turn. If H0 holds, a rule on ERA5 100 m wind speed alone, with no observed power, finds the rows to drop from training and evaluation. not supported not-for-production The noisy near-zero rows in the three metered wind series do not occur only at low wind. A rule on ERA5 wind speed alone drops about 9% to 15% of half-hours but still misses about 60% of the idle-band half-hours, probably because ERA5's 25 km cell is a poor guide to the wind at each farm (untested). The rows this experiment flags are stored for the main wind experiment to use, and W3's first 2 years are a candidate to drop. primary
    00102 simp1-simplifying-the-bg2-best-guess-before-production A single weekly-refitted XGBoost model without the three power lags, the solar offset, the 14-day correction, or the blend scores within 0.15 points of capacity of BG2 at the 16 Primary substations, within 0.30 at the 4 bulk and grid supply points, within 0.10 at the 6 solar farms, and within 0.30 at the 3 wind farms. We expect the Primary claim to fail and the other three to be close calls (see the prediction). Separately, the solar offset helps most where it should, at midday in summer at the 5 Primary substations that have one. partly supported candidate Removing the three recent-power inputs from the best-guess XGBoost forecast makes it worse at Primary substations (by 0.36 points of capacity once one substation's commissioning months are cut, and most of that comes from one substation whose demand level fell in March 2026) and at supply points (by 0.28). The wind farm forecasts lost nothing measurable and the solar farm forecasts little. The blend of 5 models can shrink to 3, and the solar offset lowers the score by 0.18 points at midday in summer at the 5 substations that use it. also relevant
    00072 p35-step-loss-monitor-for-solar-farms For the 5 metered solar farms with a long record (labelled A, B, C, D, and F), a monitor can find a loss of generating capacity, such as a lost inverter, from the farm's own power telemetry. The monitor divides each day's power by the power a physics model expects on clear, unclipped hours, divides that ratio again by the same day's ratio at the other farms, and fits a piecewise-constant level to the result with an L0 penalty (an optimal-partitioning change-point fit). It finds a 25% loss that lasts 30 days, starting from April to September, within 28 days of its onset in at least 80% of injections, at no more than 0.5 false alarms per farm-year. It finds more injected losses of 10% than two simpler monitors that are tuned to the same false-alarm rate: a rolling median of the same signal, and the hysteresis rule of the capacity studies (called HYST here) run on the same signal. refuted not-for-production A change-point monitor of a solar farm's daily output found 0.84 of 25% losses lasting 90 days and 0.45 of those lasting 30 days, below the 0.80 the plan needed, so the verdict is refuted. On 10% losses it did not beat a rolling median by an interval that excludes zero (+0.045, 97.5% interval -0.015 to +0.090). primary
    00057 product-trained-xgboost-solar-farms For the six metered solar farms, an XGBoost quantile model trained on ENS and given UKV's shortwave radiation, total cloud, and temperature as extra input columns scores lower on the quantile score at lead hours 24 to 54 than the same model trained on ENS alone, with every arm trained on the same window, rows, and seeds. UKV is read from the CEDA archive, which is not the live product, so the contrast is a ceiling read. inconclusive not-for-production Giving UKV forecasts to an ENS-trained solar-farm model changed the quantile score by -0.035 points of capacity at 1 to 2 days ahead (95% interval -0.119 to +0.037), which is too small to resolve. The data cannot show a gain and cannot rule out the 0.10-point gain that would justify a new weather feed. UKV helped the six solar farms far less than it helped the three wind farms. primary
    00054 product-trained-xgboost-wind-farms For the three metered wind farms, an XGBoost quantile model trained on ENS and given UKV's wind as extra input columns scores lower on the quantile score at lead days 1 and 2 than the same model trained on ENS alone, with every arm trained on the same window, rows, and seeds. UKV is read from the CEDA archive, which is not the live product, so the contrast is a ceiling read. small gain not-for-production Giving the Met Office's UKV wind forecast to an ENS-trained wind-farm model lowered the quantile score by about 0.2 points of capacity at 1 to 2 days ahead (95% interval 0.07 to 0.32), and a shuffled-UKV placebo shows the gain is information and not extra columns. The UKV data come from a research archive and not the live feed, so the gain is an upper limit on what a service could get. primary
    00052 more-nwp-products-for-wind-farms For the three metered wind farms, averaging the quantiles of the experiment 33 wind best guess (the ERA5-trained mixture over the 51 ENS members, with the power-curve margin and the spread factor) with the quantiles of the same ERA5-trained model fed ICON-EU's and IFS 0.25 degree's forecast wind lowers the quantile score at 1 to 2 days ahead, with weights chosen only on earlier months. inconclusive not-for-production Averaging the wind best guess with two extra weather forecasts, ICON-EU and the ECMWF high-resolution forecast, scored 0.055 points of capacity better at lead days 1 and 2, but the 95% interval (-0.200 to +0.072) includes zero.
