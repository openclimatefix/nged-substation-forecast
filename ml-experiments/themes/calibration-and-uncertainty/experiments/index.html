    Calibration and uncertainty: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00330 primary-demand-standard-architecture-arm The maintainer prefers one standard architecture (a physics core, an optional neural input adapter, an optional mixture-density output head) across demand, wind, and solar forecasts, unless a different architecture gains strongly. Primary demand has the physics core already (the convex stack with a Kalman filter). One small network, trained jointly to correct the median and to give a mixture-density band, fed through a learned two-layer adapter of the ensemble summaries, scores within 0.03 points of Primary demand research default 3, so demand can share the standard architecture at little or no loss. partly supported not-for-production A single neural network with a learned input adapter and a mixture-density output, placed on top of the frozen convex stack, forecasts Primary demand at least as well as the current research default from 0.5 to 168 hours, so demand does not need a different architecture from wind and solar at those leads. The gain comes from the mixture-density output (the median-only version gains nothing), and most of it comes from one Primary, D10. The 7-to-10-day range is untested, and the cold-night and sunny-daylight tails are too uncertain to call the architecture safe. also relevant
    00329 primary-demand-default-3-member-solar-distribution-at-7-to-10-days At 168 to 240 hours the 51 ensemble members have little skill for individual days, so the forecast centres on average sunshine and the half-hours that turn out sunny have realised demand below the median. The weakness of Primary demand research default 3 on sunny daylight (5.07 against the manual heuristic's 4.82 in experiment 317, for the convex chain before any blend) is therefore a fault of the predictive distribution (the lower tail on realised-sunny half-hours), not of the median. Replacing the two-number Gaussian summary of the members' solar responses with the 51 responses themselves (X1), mixing them with a climatology of the response (X2), or adding the members of the two previous daily runs (X3) fixes the distribution. not supported not-for-production The 7-to-10-day weakness on sunny half-hours no longer exists in the research demand forecast: the two-number solar summary and the daylight-only blend with the manual heuristic already took it from 5.2% worse than the heuristic to 6.6% better, so no new distribution method is needed there. What remains is one coastal Primary (D5), where the forecast is 11% worse than the heuristic, which points back at the solar term and not at the width of the bands. also relevant
    00327 lsc-label-free-detect-mask-train-pipeline A forecast of the normal running arrangement (NRA) can be trained and corrected on power with the load shifts set to missing, where a label-free detector finds the load shifts. The detector reads only how unusual the observed power is inside the predictive bands of a demand model that uses no lagged power, and no NGED switching label enters any threshold, mask, or training row. partly supported candidate A forecast of the normal running arrangement recovers faster after a load shift when the readings that a label-free detector flags are treated as missing: during and after injected shifts the 95% pinball loss falls from 1.79 to 1.04 points of capacity. On ordinary days the masked forecast is no worse, and slightly better. Without one site (D11) the gain shrinks to 0.30 points, and retraining the demand model on masked power adds nothing beyond masking the filter. also relevant
    00326 wind-research-default-1-change-arms Each of four changes, added alone to Wind farms research default 1, lowers the quantile score on the kept rows of the three metered wind farms W1 to W3 at 1 to 10 day leads: (a) 2 m temperature and an air-density column as XGBoost inputs, (b) hour of day and day of year as XGBoost inputs, (c) the ERA5-trained neural adapter's effective wind speed as an extra XGBoost column, and (d) one wind-speed scale factor, fitted on the hindcast to the implied wind, applied to every member's 100 m speed before the XGBoost model. At most one is adopted, in the order c, b, a, d. supported candidate Feeding XGBoost the neural adapter's corrected wind speed lowers the wind forecast's score by 0.10 points of capacity, about 0.9% of the default's score, at 1 to 10 day leads (99.75% interval -0.19 to -0.01). The change is negative at every farm and at short and long leads. The three other changes were not separable from zero. Only a port that puts the adapter in the production service can use the gain, so the maintainer decides. also relevant
    00324 wind-farm-solar-cross-breeds-long-run The solar-farm findings (a mixture-density network (MDN) fed by a physical plant forecast beats the plant alone, lead features help it, and one pooled head across leads matches separate heads per lead band) carry over to the three metered wind farms W1 to W3, when the plant is the physics core with the experiment 285 wind-speed adapter and the weather is the 51-member ensemble. partly supported not-for-production A small neural network that corrects the quantiles of the wind farms' physics-based forecast lowers the quantile score by 0.27 points of capacity against XGBoost plus a lookup table of offsets, on the half-hours when the farm is not idle, at days 1 to 2 and at days 3 to 10. The gain disappears when the idle half-hours are included. The upper tail gets no better. The network needs the farm's own forecast history to learn from. also relevant
    00322 primary-demand-default-2-daylight-only-heuristic-blend-and-sunshine-graft-replication (Stages 1 and 2) In experiment 320 the heuristic blend's gain at 168 to 240 hours sits on sunny daylight (-0.236 points) and in April to October, while its harm sits on cold winter nights, which are night half-hours. Confining the blend of experiment 319 to half-hours with the sun above 5 degrees (D1) keeps the gain with no harm on cold winter nights by construction, and a night weight that is a function of the share of the 51 ensemble members below 3 degrees C (D3) recovers the nights where the heuristic helps without harming cold ones. (Stage 3, a diagnostic) Experiment 316's sunshine graft, which recovered 0.36 of the depth-1 XGBoost refit's advantage on the worst April-to-June half-hours, replicates in April and June 2025. partly supported candidate At 7 to 10 days the blend with the manual heuristic gains about 4% at all hours, and about 2.4% when it is used only by day, which is the version with no harm on the coldest winter nights. Counting how many of the 51 ensemble members are cold finds 72% of those nights (the ensemble mean finds 41%), and a hard switch on that count keeps the whole 4% with almost no cold-night harm, but that switch was not pre-registered. The sunshine response of the depth-1 XGBoost refit explains about half of the refit's advantage on the worst April-to-June half-hours in both years, so a flexible sunshine term in the convex fit is worth testing. also relevant
    00321 metered-farm-lower-tail-recalibration-and-tail-head Metered solar farms research default 1 (the blend assembled by experiment 300, scored to 10 days by experiment 310) lets the actual output fall below its 0.01 quantile 3.3% to 3.7% of the time, and no mixture-density head tried in experiment 318 changes that. Default plus a recalibration table, a zero-inflated lower tail, or a tail-targeted head brings the share of actual values strictly below the 0.01 quantile close to 1% at leads of 1 to 10 days, without raising the quantile score and without losing experiment 318's 0.99 gain. partly supported candidate The solar-farm forecast's lowest quantile was already close to correct: only 1.2% to 1.4% of actual values fall strictly below the 0.01 quantile, because the earlier 3.3% counted the 3.0% of half-hours that read exactly zero. A recalibration table, a zero-inflated head, and a tail head each lower the own-history score by 0.04 points or less (the unshrunk table raises it by 0.03 to 0.05 points), so none is adopted. A shrunk recalibration table does lower the score by about 0.05 points for a farm with no history, and two heads lower the 0.99 pinball loss by 3.7% to 8.5% while covering the 0.99 quantile only 98.3% to 98.9% of the time. also relevant
    00320 primary-demand-default-2-night-aware-heuristic-blend-and-night-band-width Primary demand research default 2 has two weak spots. (1) At 168 to 240 hours a blend with the manual heuristic lowers the score by 4.5%, but a single weight per lead band and season harms cold winter nights, where the convex chain is the better forecast. Default plus B1 (the blend switched off on half-hours forecast to be cold winter nights) or default plus B2 (a chain weight that is a logistic function of the ensemble-mean temperature, per lead band and day or night) keeps the gain without the harm. (2) The prediction bands are too wide at night, most clearly at one Primary (D10, whose band heads gained 0.144 points, 88% of it at night). Default plus N (a width multiplier on the quantile offsets from the median, fitted per Primary and per night or day block from the previous 90 days) removes that excess width at every Primary. partly supported candidate A blend with the manual heuristic still improves the 7 to 10 day Primary demand forecast by about 4%, and the harm it does on the coldest winter nights cannot be removed by switching it off when the weather forecast says the night will be cold, because at 7 to 10 days that forecast spots only between one and two cold nights in three. Narrowing the night prediction bands helps one Primary (D10, by 0.25 points on night half-hours), leaves the other five unchanged or slightly worse, and makes the cold-night tails worse. also relevant
    00319 primary-demand-default-1-solar-summary-and-heuristic-blend-at-7-to-10-days Primary demand research default 1 has two weak spots at 168 to 240 hours: sunny daylight half-hours, where it scores 5.2% worse than the manual heuristic, and April to October, where it beats climatology by only 4%. Default plus x1 (the two-number solar summary S, the mean and standard deviation of the 51 ensemble members' solar responses, extended from 0.5 to 168 hours out to 240 hours) lowers the sunny-daylight score at 168 to 240 hours. Default plus x2 (a blend of the convex chain's median with the manual heuristic's, with the weight fitted per 24-hour lead band and season group) lowers the pooled score at 168 to 240 hours, mostly in April to October. partly supported candidate The demand forecast for 7 to 10 days ahead improves by about 2% when its solar term also uses the spread between the 51 weather-forecast members, and the gain is largest on sunny days (6.6%), where the forecast had been worse than the manual heuristic that National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED) staff use. Blending in the manual heuristic improves the forecast by 4.5%, but the blend worsens the coldest winter nights beyond the agreed tolerance, so only the solar change is adopted. also relevant
    00318 metered-farm-mdn-head-more-features Giving the mixture-density head of Metered solar farms research default 1 the forecast lead (lead in whole days), the hour of day and day of year as sine and cosine pairs, and the ensemble spread of the shortwave irradiance lowers its quantile score, mostly beyond 36 hours and in the lower tail, and one head trained on all lead bands beats one head per band. partly supported candidate Giving the solar-farm head more inputs does not improve its overall score at any lead from 1 to 10 days, so the head's inputs are not what limits the forecast. The extra clock and cloud-spread inputs do lower the loss at the 0.99 quantile (the tail that matters for curtailment) by about 5% at days 3 to 7, and none of the changes fixes the lower tail, where 3.3% to 3.7% of actual values fall below the 0.01 quantile, where 1% is expected. Training one head for all leads makes the network with no plant nearly as good as the plant-based head at days 8 to 10 (0.4% worse), so the plant adds little there. also relevant
    00317 primary-demand-default-1-at-7-to-10-days The ensemble weather already reaches 10 days, so the convex model of Primary demand research default 1 can be driven to 240 hours, and with a spread table fitted per 24-hour lead band its quantile score stays better than the manual heuristic and climatology through day 9 and ties climatology on day 10. partly supported not-for-production The demand forecast we use for research still has a lower error than a table of typical demand by month and time of day (climatology) at 9 to 10 days ahead, by about 11%, because the temperature forecast keeps some skill there. Its advantage over NGED's manual heuristic shrinks to 5% to 9% beyond 7 days, and the 95% interval of that advantage includes zero. The advantage sits in winter and at two of the six substations. The forecast is useful out to 10 days on cold winter nights and no better than the heuristic on sunny daylight half-hours. also relevant
    00316 primary-demand-night-level-and-single-input-terms (Stage 0) The gain of the band heads of experiments 311 and 314 at D10, an anonymised Primary substation, is mostly a night-time level error of Primary demand research default 1 and not a better description of the uncertainty. (Stage 1) The April-to-June gap between arm D and BG2 at D5 and D9, two anonymised Primaries, sits mainly in arm D's response to sunshine and not in its recent-power or calendar terms. inconclusive not-for-production The band heads help at one Primary substation mainly by narrowing its night-time bands, not by correcting a level error: a night level offset reproduces only 14% of the gain, with a wide interval. For the April-to-June gap to BG2, a post hoc test points at the sunshine response of the best-guess model (0.36 of the gap recovered on the worst half-hours, but only 0.19 at one of the two Primaries), and a differently fitted surrogate in the audit did not reproduce it, so the ranking of the families is unreliable. also relevant
    00314 primary-demand-gated-or-pinball-band-head Experiment 311's mixture-density band head lowered the quantile score of Primary demand research default 1 but harmed sunny daylight half-hours, and its gain rested on D10. Keeping the default's bands on sunny daylight half-hours (gate), training the head directly on the 11-level pinball loss (quantile network), or both, removes the harm and leaves a gain that does not depend on one Primary. partly supported candidate Experiment 311's neural band head was stopped by harm on sunny daylight half-hours. Keeping the current bands on those half-hours fixes that harm, and the gated head scores 0.041 points better than the current Primary demand forecast bands (research default 1, lower is better). The gain is still almost entirely one substation, D10: without it the gain is 0.015 points and its interval touches zero, so the gated head is a candidate and not an adoption. Training the head on the quantile score directly gave no improvement. primary
    00312 lsc-how-good-must-a-detector-be-against-the-band-head A load shift detector that is far from perfect can still beat the best forecast that uses no detector, so a detector need not be near-perfect to be worth building. The one detector-based arm in experiment 297 that beat that forecast was the one that subtracts the true megawatts from the readings during a shift and adds them back to the forecast. This experiment degrades that arm one axis at a time to find where it stops beating the no-detector forecast. It also asks whether the surprise of recent readings against GAM_ONLY's residual bands (band position), or perfect knowledge of a shift, added as inputs to the no-detector quantile head, improves the head. This is question 3 of the LSC decision program, redefined by the Discuss ruling of 2026-10-23. not supported not-for-production A load shift detector does not need to be near-perfect to improve on the best forecast that uses no detector: with the true megawatts added back, finding half the shifts, learning of each up to 3 days late, or misjudging the megawatts by half still lowers curtailment cost against that forecast. The detector tested earlier (RO-NC) finds only a third of the shifts, below the break-even of one half (measured with every other property perfect). The surprise of recent readings against the forecast's bands (band position) found more, 37% of shifts on even months, but still under one half and with about 90% of its alarms false, so the next step is a detector that finds more shifts. also relevant
    00311 primary-demand-mixture-density-band-head Primary demand research default 1 (the GAM stack A+H+S of experiments 220 and 251) gets its bands from a smooth spread table and a cold-day widening of the upper offsets, around a median shifted by the neural head. A mixture-density neural network, which reads the weather state and may be skewed, gives bands with a lower quantile score, and the gain sits in the protected tail groups. partly supported candidate A neural network that sets the forecast bands from the weather state scores 0.037 points better than the current Primary demand bands over the scored year, and 0.034 better in the year before, but the gain comes almost entirely from one substation (D10) and the new bands are worse on sunny daylight half-hours. The band head is a candidate, not an adoption; the next test should keep the current bands on sunny daylight half-hours or train the head on the quantile score directly. primary
    00304 metered-farm-node-sum-tail For the solar farms that share a bulk supply point, adding each farm's quantiles overstates the upper tail of the farms' summed output, adding independent bands understates it, and summing the 51 ensemble members' farm forecasts member by member gives calibrated 0.95 and 0.99 quantiles, because the ensemble carries the farms' shared dependence on cloud. not supported not-for-production The band for the combined output of the three farms beneath one substation comes out too wide if the weather forecast's 51 members are summed member by member, because the members' forecasts for the three farms move together almost perfectly while the farms' actual forecast errors are less closely linked. That band is almost the same as the band from adding each farm's quantiles. A band that assumes the farms' errors are unrelated is too narrow. A band from a Gaussian copula with a correlation fitted on the errors of the other 11 months comes closest to the stated probability. Only 5 half-hours in the scored year exceed 0.75 times a substation's maximum export, so this experiment cannot say what the band widths mean for curtailment cost. also relevant
    00303 wind-adapter-in-production-setting Experiment 285 found that, with ERA5 as the weather, a small neural adapter in front of the physics core of a wind farm scored better than XGBoost. On the 51 ensemble members in the scored year, an adapter fine-tuned on the ensemble hindcast beats XGBoost plus experiment 259's offset table (the benchmark that matters), because the adapter corrects the wind speed itself and the table corrects only the power. partly supported not-for-production A small neural correction of the wind speed, fitted to ERA5 and run on the 51 ensemble members, does about as well as XGBoost with a lookup table of offsets (0.09 points better, too little to justify porting). Fitting the correction on the one year of ensemble hindcasts made the forecast worse, probably because that year differs from the scored year; we did not test the cause. also relevant
    00301 metered-farm-outage-state-band A small availability state (online, partly down, offline) estimated from the last 6 to 48 hours of a solar farm's power against its plant forecast improves the forecast band's lower tail during and after an outage. inconclusive not-for-production Solar-farm outages are too rare in the scored year to test an availability state: five of the six farms have at most 34 outage half-hours, and the sixth has 165. Too few half-hours remain to separate a 10% gain from chance, so the experiment stopped before fitting anything. If outages matter, the next test needs a longer period, such as the full history of farms A, B, and F. also relevant
    00300 metered-farm-best-guess-assembly The assembled candidate (spread-binned plant P2, a generalised additive model (GAM) band on the plant's residuals, a mixture-density head (MDN), blended per quantile with XGBoost) scores better than plant-fed XGBoost on the quantile score and at the 0.99 quantile, and its advantage is larger for a farm with no history because the plant carries what the missing history cannot. partly supported candidate The assembled solar-farm forecast (a physical power model, two correction stages, and a blend with XGBoost) scores 3.3% better than plant-fed XGBoost (XGBoost on the plant forecast, its ensemble spread, the unit mean, and the availability ratio, with no weather features of its own) on the quantile score, which averages the pinball loss over 11 quantile levels, when each farm trains on its own history. It is 43% better at the 0.99 quantile, which bounds how much output NGED may need to curtail. With no history for a farm, the gain over XGBoost is smaller (2.0%) and below the 3% we predicted, so the physical model helps less than we expected when a farm's history is missing. also relevant
    00297 lsc-need-a-detector-no-detector-arms-against-the-mask A forecast that never learns which readings are load shifts can match a forecast that uses a detector, because its bands and its recent-power correction can be made to tell a load shift from ordinary variation by themselves. This is question 2 of the LSC decision program. not supported not-for-production A forecast with no detector that recalibrates its bands and reads how far the recent-power correction has moved scores better than the RO-NC detector near load shifts, so with RO-NC a detector is not shown to be needed. Perfect knowledge with the true megawatts added back still lowers curtailment cost by 6.5% (3.9 to 9.2) against that forecast, so the next step is to run the better LSC detector (LR_b20) on the same simulated shifts and judge it against a forecast that has the recalibrated bands. also relevant
    00294 regime-aware-model-at-d8-engine-state-term D8's metered power excludes a gas engine's output (experiment 194) while the engine, which runs in spells (experiment 291), takes net load below what the weather and calendar predict. A single model trained across those spells has no way to know which half-hours the engine ran, so its forecast and its bands carry the engine as noise. A model that keeps the calendar, weather, and solar shapes shared across the whole history but adds one regime-specific term, an offset per half-hour the engine runs (non-negative, taking power off net load), forecasts D8 better than the single model. The offset is estimated from the data, and its size is not assumed. not supported not-for-production Telling the forecast when D8's gas engine is running would make it much better when the state is read at the half-hour being forecast, but the detector builds that state from the very reading being forecast. With only the state of the last settled day, the engine term made the forecast 10% worse; trained on that stale state, it gained only 3%, no better than widening the bands from past residuals. A forecast of whether the engine runs, or NGED's own record of its running hours, would be the data to test next. also relevant
    00293 metered-farm-plant-tilt-and-clip The six metered farms fit best at a 15 to 20 degree tilt (experiment 282), while the spread-binned plant (P2 of experiment 270) assumes 35 degrees and caps its forecast at the 0.995 quantile of the tuning months' output. Replacing the fleet tilt with 20 degrees and the hard cap with a higher, soft cap lowers P2's April to July under-prediction and its 0.99 pinball loss. not supported not-for-production Changing the solar power model's panel tilt from 35 to 20 degrees fixes most of its winter over-prediction on clear days and improves the lowest 1% quantile of the forecast by 4%, but it leaves the overall forecast band and the 99% quantile unchanged and slightly worsens summer. A higher or softer cap on the power does nothing, so the shape of the power model is not what limits the upper tail of the forecast band. A tilt between 20 and 35 degrees, or a tilt that varies with the season, is the next tilt to test. also relevant
    00292 metered-farm-ens-neighbourhood A day-ahead cloud field is often right in shape and wrong in position by tens of kilometres, so summaries of the ensemble's irradiance over the hexagonal cells around a metered solar farm carry information that the farm's own cell lacks. Giving H5 (the plant plus a neural network that outputs all 11 quantile offsets) those summaries lowers its quantile score at 12 to 36 hour leads. not supported not-for-production At the six metered solar farms tested, the ensemble's irradiance on the cells around a farm adds almost nothing to the day-ahead forecast: the quantile score improves by 0.36%, against the 2% the plan needed, and the gain is not robust to dropping one farm. Adjacent cells, 15 km apart, differ from the farm's own cell by only about 3% of the irradiance, so the ensemble mean is too smooth between them to show where a cloud edge will fall. A weather source of a different kind is the next experiment to run, rather than more processing of the same ensemble. also relevant
    00288 pooled-head-on-public-wind-farms A neural head that adds 11 quantile offsets to the XGBoost wind forecast (R0) and was trained on dozens of public onshore wind farms (metered output from Elexon's Balancing Mechanism Reporting Service) lowers the quantile score of the three metered wind farms (W1 to W3) against R0 alone, and a head trained on the pool beats the same head trained on W1 to W3 only, because experiment 259's head had too few farm-years to learn a correction that transfers. A second hypothesis: XGBoost keeps improving as the training history grows from 12 to 48 months while the physics core is flat after 24 months, and a pool of public farms replaces the missing history. not supported not-for-production Training the correction on 40 public wind farms did not help the forecasts for the three NGED wind farms. Applied directly, it made them worse, because the public farms' capacity, turbine rating, and hub height let the head memorise each farm.
