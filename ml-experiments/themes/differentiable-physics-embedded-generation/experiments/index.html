    Differentiable physics: embedded generation: experiments

    This page lists every experiment tried in the theme, newest first. An experiment that belongs mainly to another theme but bears on this one is marked "also relevant".

    Experiment Hypothesis Verdict Production status Take-home message Relevance
    00306 summer-gap-solar-interactions-with-temperature-and-day-type The April-to-June daylight excess of arm D (the convex demand model with a Kalman filter on recent errors) over BG2 (the production-style XGBoost forecast) at the substations D5 and D9 is an additive-structure problem. On cold clear spring days heating demand and rooftop solar output are both large, and an additive model must give the solar term one weight on every day. Experiment 299 found the D5 excess largest on clear midday half-hours (+1.67 points). Letting the solar shape interact with temperature (shape times the heating hinge) and with day type (shape times a weekend indicator) lets the model give those days a different solar weight. refuted not-for-production Letting the rooftop-solar term vary with temperature and with weekdays versus weekends does not close the April-to-June gap to BG2: at the two substations with the gap, the extra columns close about 0% of it. Two linear interaction columns therefore do not explain the gap. The next test is what BG2 uses that the convex demand model does not (item 5 of the summer-gap brief). also relevant
    00299 summer-gap-timing-night-level-and-error-correction The April-to-June excess of arm D (the convex demand model with a Kalman filter on recent errors) over BG2 (the production-style XGBoost forecast) at the substations D5 and D9 is a step that starts at the March clock change and sits at short leads, which would point at a local-time mismatch or at BG2's use of recent power. D10's night-time level offset of about -2 points (21:00 to 07:30 UTC) is a step on one date, not a smooth drift with temperature. The Kalman filter and the correction head are net harmful at D5 and D9 in April to June. not supported not-for-production The spring excess of the best-guess demand forecast over BG2 grows with sunshine, appearing on clear midday half-hours at D5 and across the whole day at D9. It is not concentrated in the 4 weeks after the clock change or at short leads, so the next test is the solar term's response on clear half-hours at D5. The Kalman filter and the correction heads are not the cause: removing the filter changes little, and at D5 the heads reduce the excess. D10's night-time offset varies partly with month and has no single date on which it changes. also relevant
    00294 regime-aware-model-at-d8-engine-state-term D8's metered power excludes a gas engine's output (experiment 194) while the engine, which runs in spells (experiment 291), takes net load below what the weather and calendar predict. A single model trained across those spells has no way to know which half-hours the engine ran, so its forecast and its bands carry the engine as noise. A model that keeps the calendar, weather, and solar shapes shared across the whole history but adds one regime-specific term, an offset per half-hour the engine runs (non-negative, taking power off net load), forecasts D8 better than the single model. The offset is estimated from the data, and its size is not assumed. not supported not-for-production Telling the forecast when D8's gas engine is running would make it much better when the state is read at the half-hour being forecast, but the detector builds that state from the very reading being forecast. With only the state of the last settled day, the engine term made the forecast 10% worse; trained on that stale state, it gained only 3%, no better than widening the bands from past residuals. A forecast of whether the engine runs, or NGED's own record of its running hours, would be the data to test next. primary
    00291 d8-drops-engine-spells-load-shift D8's 29 multi-day drops (experiment 159) are spells in which a gas engine of up to three units runs, and D8's own metered power no longer includes the engine's output (experiment 194). If so, the engine detector of experiment 196 flags the drops' half-hours far more often than matched windows elsewhere on D8, the drops' depths cluster at multiples of one engine unit, and most of D8's load-shift alarms that fall inside drops are engine edges. partly supported not-for-production D8's multi-day drops coincide with the engine detector's running states, which is consistent with the gas engine running: the detector flags 27 of the 28 drops that began after the engine left D8's reading, against about 1 in 5 comparable stretches of D8, and it flags almost nothing at three other Primaries on the same dates. This cannot be called proved, because the drops and the detector both read D8's residual, and the depths of the drops do not fall at whole multiples of the engine's 1.97 MW unit. D8's drops should be flagged as a probable generator regime and reported apart from the load-shift scoring until NGED confirms the engine's running hours, and the next step is a forecast model for D8 that is told when the engine is running. also relevant
    00289 summer-gap-weather-input-discriminator The best-guess stack's weaker April to June score than BG2 at the decision Primaries comes from the ensemble's sunshine forecast (or its ratio calibration), and not from demand absorbing the solar term or from the error-correction layers. partly supported not-for-production Giving the best-guess stack the sunshine that actually happened would close about 70% of its April to June gap to BG2, but the same change helps the stack nearly as much in July to September, when the stack already matches BG2. Most of the April to June gap beyond the July to September gap therefore stays. That points to the solar term in the demand model, or to the error-correction layers, and away from the ensemble sunshine forecast. A refit with realised sunshine as the solar driver, and a check of the solar term at D5 and D9 in April to June, are the next steps. also relevant
    00282 pv-plant-curve-audit The curve that turns sunshine into power in our solar models departs from the metered farms' measured curve by more than 5% of capacity somewhere in the clear-sky range, and the departure is the flat-top level (the inverter limit), not the panel tilt or the temperature loss. not supported not-for-production The curve that turns sunshine into solar power stays within 5 percentage points of capacity at 3 of the 6 metered solar farms and departs from their measured output at the other 3 in different directions, so no single fix to the inverter limit repairs it. The best-guess demand stack assumes panels tilted 35 degrees and the farms fit best at about 20 degrees, which makes the stack over-predict output on clear half-hours in December (measured is 0.66 of the stack, median over farms) and under-predict it from April to July by 5 to 8%. Swapping in the pooled shape of the other farms does not help, so the next experiment should try the 20 degree tilt inside the stack. also relevant
    00280 pv-empirical-shape-and-capacity-in-arm-d The April to June 2026 gap between arm D (the monthly-refit generalised additive model of experiments 155 and 220, scored on experiment 178's rows) and BG2 comes from its solar term: the pinned solar capacity is too high and the south-facing fleet shape is not the shape the metered farms show. Replacing the fleet shape by the pooled empirical shape of experiment 269, at the capacity that shape's real-series fit supports, lowers arm D's April to June error at the substations where the pinned capacity looks too high (D5, D9, and D16). not supported not-for-production Putting the empirical solar shape into the forecast, at the pinned capacity or at the lower capacity the real series supports, does not lower the April to June error at the substations where the pinned capacity looked too high. The change is under 0.01 MW against an error of about 0.5 MW, and the intervals are wide. A capacity cut that is correct for the whole of April to June is not the fix either: at D5 it raises the error. This experiment finds no sign that the shape or the capacity of the solar term closes the summer gap to BG2. primary
    00269 pv-capacity-gain-decomposition Most of the 0.090 gain that experiment 238 found from using a nearby farm's recorded output as the solar shape comes from the farm's plant shape (tilt, orientation, flat top) and from the capacity unit in which recovery is read, and little comes from the farm's measured sunshine. partly supported not-for-production For the five substations and six farms tested, the measured sunshine at a nearby farm explains almost none of the extra solar output that its recorded output helped to find. The extra output found comes from the farms' plant shape (panel tilt, panel orientation, and the flat top of the output curve). A table learned from other farms' output and driven by the satellite irradiance reproduces that plant shape. Using the table finds 0.966 of an injected solar farm's output when recovery is read as the capacity ratio of experiment 231 (0.826 with the physical fleet shape), and 0.995 when it is read over clear-sky daylight energy. In these tests the satellite irradiance was not the limit, and a metered farm beside each substation was not needed. Switching to the table lowers the fitted solar capacities of the five decision substations by 0.2 to 0.4 MW. The effect on the sunny-half-hour forecast score has not been run. primary
    00267 pv-capacity-early-years-and-register-floor The inferred capacity of unmetered solar at the five decision Primaries falls below the register floor in season-year 2021 (July 2021 to June 2022) because the capacity fit recovers only about 0.78 of an injected farm and the 2021 fit window is thin, not because the register is shared to the wrong district. Dividing by the median recovery of the metered farms, and using the output of a metered farm within 25 km as the sunshine shape, lifts the estimates to the floor. not supported not-for-production The 2021 shortfall at D10 and D11 is not a missing recovery correction and not a thin fit window: the card already divides by the metered farms' recovery, and the 2021 season-year has 12 months of data at every Primary. Using a nearby metered farm's output as the sunshine shape makes the shortfall no smaller (the estimates fall by 7% to 22%, partly because the two shapes are divided by different recoveries), so the shortfall at those two Primaries remains unexplained. Candidate causes are the register's postcode-district share, a gap of about 20 months in the early data, and a fit that is too low in its first full year. Show 2021 at D10 and D11 as a known shortfall. primary
    00266 summer-gap-diagnostics-solar-term The best-guess demand stack (experiment 251's arm 1) trails BG2, the production-style XGBoost forecast, in April to June 2026 mainly because of its explicit solar term (the subtracted estimate of unmetered rooftop solar output), and not because of the neural-network head, the two-number summary of the ensemble's solar response, the Kalman filter, or the heating and lighting terms. The same defect explains the loss at D5, an anonymised Primary substation, on sunny half-hours. partly supported not-for-production The best-guess stack trails BG2 in April to June because of the solar term at two of the six substations (D5 and D9 on daylight half-hours) and a night-time error at a third (D10), not because of the neural-network head, the two-number summary, the Kalman filter, or the ensemble sunshine forecast; the head and summary actually reduce the gap. The pinned solar capacity looks too high in those months, not too low, and clear mornings are forecast worse than clear afternoons. Candidate next experiments are the export clip at D9, a morning-and-afternoon solar shape, and a look at D10's night residuals; the stack's added parts need no change for this gap. also relevant
    00265 dp-gas-engine-bands-prior-and-visibility-switch Widening a Primary's forecast bands from its own past residuals (experiment 264, arm 2b) has coverage far from nominal in the first quarter, when there is no history, and after a change in the site's behaviour. A prior taken from the other Primaries' residuals, shrunk towards the site's own trailing residuals as they accrue, fixes the first quarter. A causal switch on the share of large drops in the trailing 30 days returns the widening to normal when D8's engine becomes visible in the readings (around April 2026). Together they hold D8's q05-to-q95 coverage within 5 points of 0.90 in every quarter, without raising the cost in quantile score, and without harming Primaries that have no engine. not supported not-for-production In this year of forecasts, the other five Primaries' residuals did not predict D8's errors, so a starting estimate from them left D8's bands too narrow in the first quarter, and returning to normal widening after the engine's drops faded made them too narrow again. Widening from D8's own history (experiment 264) remained the best of the arms tried. We tested no other fix for the first quarter, and none of the other five Primaries had a heavy lower tail. primary
    00264 dp-gas-engine-asymmetric-bands-at-d8 D8's forecast bands are too narrow in both directions, and part of the lower-tail miss is a 6 MW gas engine (probably three units of about 2 MW) that runs in spells and cannot be detected. A spread that widens as-of, using only residuals already resolved at the forecast origin, and that widens most in the direction the engine pushes (net demand falls), gives honest q05 to q95 bands at D8 without raising the quantile score at the Primaries that have no engine. not supported not-for-production A forecast band that learns from the residuals it has already seen narrows most of the gap at D8: the quantile score falls by 9% to 19% and the share of readings inside the 90% band rises from 42% to between 80% and 87%. The extra gain of the engine mixture over simple widening is not evidence of an engine, because the mixture fits a running engine at two substations that have none. Plain two-sided widening with an earlier residual history is the option these results support, and NGED's own output for the engine would be the data to test an engine model. primary
    00252 gam-stack-at-eight-more-primaries The best-guess GAM stack (GAM-K with the heating split by day type, shared filter noise, pinned solar capacity, ensemble-mean weather, winter night offset, and the 60% PV clip) scores within 0.05 points of BG2 over leads 0.5 to 168 hours at D2, D3, D4, D8, D12, D19, D21, and D22, so that every substation card with a BG2 line can also show a best-guess line and example weeks. not supported not-for-production The best-guess demand forecast (the stack) does not transfer unchanged to the eight primary substations whose meters record apparent power. Without a solar term it is at most 0.05 points worse than BG2 (the production-style forecast) at only two of them. Fitting a solar term at each monthly refit puts five of the eight at or below BG2 on the point estimate, so those five cards can show a best-guess line, while D8, D12, and D22 should show none. also relevant
    00250 dp-monotone-network-between-the-solar-forecast-and-the-gam A small neural network that is monotone in the explicit solar forecast, saturates, and reads the clear-sky index, the hour, and the clip cap maps the solar forecast to the solar output that the metered series shows, and so closes more of D5's sunny-daylight gap to BG2 than the hard clip, because it can learn how the saturation depends on the weather. not supported not-for-production A small neural network that sits between the solar forecast and the demand model improves the sunny-day forecast at D5 by about 0.03 MW on top of the hard clip, which is less than half of what is left to close. It does not replace the clip, so the final forecast keeps the clip alone. primary
    00248 metered-farm-architecture-ukv-inputs UKV irradiance and cloud, given as extra inputs to the two best neural heads of experiment 243 (H5, the plant plus 11 quantile offsets, and H2, the plant plus a correction), lower a metered solar farm's quantile score at 12 to 36 hours by more than the 1.8% that experiment 229 found for a blend of ensemble and UKV irradiance, because a neural head can use UKV cloud layers that a blend cannot. small gain not-for-production Giving the neural head on the solar-farm power model the Met Office's UKV irradiance and cloud forecasts lowers the day-ahead error of a metered solar farm by about 1%, the same small gain that XGBoost gets from the same columns. At these six farms, a neural head gained no more from UKV than XGBoost did, so UKV is not worth a second weather source on this evidence. The UKV archive used is a research archive, and the live UKV differs, so the 1% gain is an upper limit. also relevant
    00247 dp-learned-export-cap-for-the-solar-clip A cap on the solar term learned at each monthly cut from the metered series before that cut lowers the sunny-daylight error at D5 almost as much as experiment 180's hand-set 60% clip, because the clip only needs the level at which clear-sky midday export stops growing. partly supported not-for-production The demand forecast can learn how much solar a site can export from its own recent metered data and gets almost the same improvement on sunny days as a ceiling set by hand at 60%. One year of data is too little to prove it, so the final forecast keeps the hand-set ceiling. primary
    00245 lsc-false-steps-meter-regime-audit The false steps that failed experiment 207's gate at D10 and D11 are partly real load moved between connected Primaries: they coincide with a balanced, opposite step at a metered neighbour or with an alarm of the load-shift detectors, more often than steps drawn at random at the same Primary. not supported not-for-production The jumps in fitted solar capacity that made experiment 207's test fail are not load moved between connected Primary substations: none coincides with an equal and opposite step in a neighbouring Primary's forecast error, and the load-shift alarms fire as often in random months as at the jumps. About a third of the jumps counted against experiment 207 (15 of 41) are the net change between one August and the next April, which cannot be dated to a month. Three jumps sit beside readings that the cleaning rule flags as zero. In this sample the load-shift detectors do not explain the jumps. also relevant
    00243 metered-farm-architecture-comparison A solar-farm power model (the plant) as the base prediction, with a small neural network that adapts its inputs, its output, or both, forecasts a metered solar farm 12 to 36 hours ahead better than the plant alone and better than XGBoost, and keeps the plant's advantage on a farm it has never seen, while a neural network with no physics does not beat XGBoost on six farms. partly supported candidate A small neural network sitting on top of a solar-farm power model forecasts a metered solar farm about as well as XGBoost and improves on the power model alone by 4.4% in the quantile score, when the network outputs the whole forecast band. Networks that adjust a single number (the scale, a correction, or the sunlight fed to the power model) gain under 1%. The hybrid is far better than XGBoost at the upper tail (0.99 quantile, 29% lower pinball loss), which matters for curtailment. The neural network without physics does better still at that tail (32% lower pinball loss than XGBoost). also relevant
    00241 pv-capacity-calibrated-interval The recoveries that experiment 129 measured when it injected real solar farms into a Primary substation's power can be turned into a calibrated estimate of unmetered solar capacity: dividing the capacity found by the Primary's median recovery over the other farms corrects its bias, and the spread of those recoveries gives an 80% interval that covers the truth about 80% of the time. partly supported not-for-production Dividing the solar capacity found by the typical recovery of other real farms removes most of the bias: the estimate lands within 10% of the truth at all five Primaries, against a typical error of 19% without the correction. The 80% interval built the same way covers the truth 59% to 79% of the time, which is too uncertain to label as an 80% interval, and it fails completely when the demand follows cloud (0% to 2%). Keep showing the range as a spread over scenarios, with the correction applied. primary
    00239 pv-capacity-seasonal-efficiency-curve The metered solar farms' output falls 6% to 9% from spring to summer by a pattern that no clip or heat term explains. A seasonal efficiency curve of 12 monthly factors, shared by all farms, fitted on the farms' own output, and then applied to the solar shape in a Primary substation's capacity fit, lets that fit find more of an injected real solar farm. not supported not-for-production Twelve monthly efficiency factors fitted on the metered farms' output do not close the recovery shortfall. The farm-only fit rises by 0.003, below its target, and the recovery of an injected farm rises by 0.039 or 0.025 depending on whether the injected farm keeps its low-sun output. The farms fall below the satellite shape in November to January, and show no fall of 6% to 9% from spring to summer. Keeping the injected farm's low-sun output, which experiment 231 zeroed, raises the recovery by 0.033 (paired median over farms) on its own. primary
    00238 pv-capacity-nearest-farm-sunshine-reference Using the recorded output of a metered solar farm within 30 km of a Primary substation as the measure of sunshine, in place of the CAMS satellite irradiance, lets the capacity fit of experiment 129 find more of an injected real solar farm. partly supported not-for-production A nearby farm's measured output finds more of an injected solar farm than the satellite irradiance does: 0.867 against 0.780 of its output, and at least 0.05 more at all five Primaries. The gain is 0.02 to 0.14 when the two farms are 5 to 25 km apart and 0.17 to 0.24 for the one pair of farms 2 km apart, and it falls to 0.052, reaching 0.05 at 3 of 5 Primaries, when recovery is read in the capacity unit of experiment 231. The farm may carry plant effects as well as sunshine, so the result does not yet show that the satellite irradiance is the limit. primary
    00237 dp-two-number-solar-summary-with-clip The mean and standard deviation of the 51 ensemble members' solar responses lower the daylight quantile score of the demand forecast also when each member is passed through the clipped solar term, because cloud uncertainty and the clip both act at midday only partly on the same rows. supported candidate At D5, in one year of data, summarising the ensemble weather members as a mean and a standard deviation keeps at least about 90% of its gain when the solar output is clipped at 60% of the solar capacity held for D5 (110%, interval 91% to 141%). On the sunniest third of daylight rows the point estimate of the remaining gain is about half (interval -12% to 88%). A post hoc arm using the standard deviation alone kept the gain there, which suggests testing that form. primary
    00236 pv-capacity-clear-sky-fit Fitting the capacity of the unmetered solar behind a Primary substation only on half-hours with a clear sky and a high sun, where the irradiance error is smallest, finds more of an injected real solar farm than the fit of experiment 129 on all rows. refuted not-for-production Fitting the solar capacity only on clear, high-sun half-hours did not find more of an injected solar farm: 0.818 against 0.827 for all rows, with an interval 1.8 times wider. The farm alone fits better on those half-hours (0.965), so the demand terms seem to absorb more of the solar signal when only clear half-hours are used (0.147 against 0.104). Do not restrict the rows by sky condition; reduce demand absorption instead. primary
    00235 pv-capacity-matched-day-pairs The capacity of unmetered solar behind a Primary substation can be read from the difference in net power between a sunny day and a cloudy day of the same day type and the same temperature, because ordinary demand cancels in the difference. That estimator should find more of an injected real solar farm than the convex fit of experiment 129 (arm 2), which finds 0.78 to 0.88. refuted not-for-production Pairing sunny and cloudy days removed none of a synthetic load that follows cloud, so the pairs give no evidence that a real cloud-following demand cancels. With the load added, the load adds 0.3 of the injected capacity to the pair estimator's recovery, against 0.2 for the convex fit, and a December-and-January test finds 0.33 of the farm. Pairs of days with at least 10 hours of daylight find 0.91 of the farm, which agrees with experiment 231's finding that winter rows weaken the fit. The load is built to follow the pairing variable exactly, so 0.3 is a worst case. primary
    00232 dp-pv-clip-with-correction-heads A correction head learns demand structure, not an export limit, so it leaves D5's sunny-daylight gap to BG2 mostly open and adds to the 60% clip of experiment 180. not supported not-for-production The correction heads of experiment 201 close only 12% of the gap in forecast error between the demand forecast and BG2 on sunny half-hours at substation D5, and the gain is not clearly different from zero. Added on top of the 60% export clip, the head cancels most of the clip's gain, leaving the gap at +0.169 MW against +0.069 MW for the clip alone. D5 is one substation, the clip is a 60% cap on the solar output the forecast subtracts, and BG2 is the best-guess forecast. The result supports using the clip without a head in the final best-guess forecast. primary
    00231 pv-capacity-audit-where-recovery-goes Of the roughly 0.2 of an injected solar farm's capacity that the capacity estimator of experiment 129 (arm 2) does not find, most is lost because demand terms absorb part of the solar signal, a quarter or more is lost to the irradiance input, and neither the winter rows nor the definition of capacity (the divisor) explains much. partly supported not-for-production The capacity estimator misses 0.17 of an injected solar farm mainly for two reasons: the demand terms of the fit absorb part of the solar signal (58% of the miss), and the fixed solar shape does not reach the farm's flat top (47%). Better sunshine data does not help, because fitting the farm alone on clear half-hours finds no more than fitting it on all daylight. The next experiments should target demand absorption and the shape's seasonal flat top, not the irradiance input. primary
    00224 metered-farm-dayahead-two-number Passing each of the 51 ensemble members through a solar farm's plant model gives the farm's day- ahead output a spread that the ensemble-mean forecast does not have, and the mean and standard deviation of the 51 plant responses keep most of that information, as they did for Primary substations in experiment 193.
