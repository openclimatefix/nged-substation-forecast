    Experiment 91: a probability that a past half-hour lies inside a load-shift period

    Take-home message: a probability built from the causal switching detector is not reliably better than the detector's yes-or-no flag, and against NGED's switching logs it has almost no skill. The detector is called RO-NC here (the refit-offset neighbour-count detector) and is "causal" because it sees only data before the forecast origin. A rule that widened the forecast's uncertainty bands whenever the probability is high would widen them for only about 8% of the half-hours that NGED's logs mark as switching. The probability beats the flag only for injected transfers large enough for the detector to see. One option for a later experiment is a warning signal that needs no switching label, such as how unusual the recent power readings are.

    An abnormal running arrangement (ARA) is a period when a network operator has closed a normally open switch, so that one Primary substation (a substation that steps down to the local distribution network) carries load that belongs to a neighbour. A forecast made at an origin time takes as inputs the power readings (lags) of the 7 days before the origin, the lag window. The forecast is misled when one of those readings was taken during an ARA. We asked whether the detector can give each half-hour of the lag window a probability of lying inside an ARA.

    We added a known amount of load (a transfer) to the recorded power of 16 Primary substations, 839 times, using the draw of experiment 88. A second draw added 183 longer transfers, lasting 1 to 60 days, so the two draws hold 1,022 transfers. The probability was fitted on transfers that start in odd calendar months and scored on transfers that start in even months.

    The verdict is inconclusive. The calibrated score of the latest candidate (arm 2, defined below) has a Brier score (the mean squared error of a probability; smaller is better) 3.3% below the recalibrated yes-or-no flag (arm 1), with a 97.5% interval from -7.6% to +1.6%. The interval includes zero, so the experiment can neither support nor refute a gain, and the pre-registered prediction required at least 20%. The 20% threshold is the smallest gain judged worth carrying the extra detector output to the serving path. In a post hoc subset (chosen after seeing the results) that drops transfers whose donor substation steps by under 4 noise units (the size of the substation's usual half-hour-to-half-hour variation), the gain is 11.2% (-20.3% to -2.0%). Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (0.5 means no skill, 1.0 means perfect separation of half-hours inside a transfer from half-hours outside) is 0.53 against NGED's logs.

    Arm 2's gain over the flag is small and its interval includes zero on the pre-registered rows.

    The probability separates well for transfers under 14 days and poorly beyond.

    Probabilities above 0.5 are too high.

    Arms

    A candidate is an interval that the detector proposes, with a score, an onset, a size in noise units, and, once it has ended, a return step.

    • Arm 1: the flag. A half-hour is flagged when it lies inside a candidate whose score reaches the detector's threshold. Each flag value is replaced by the share of odd-month half-hours with that value that lay inside a transfer, so the flag is calibrated too.
    • Arm 2: for the most recent candidate whose switching has not been reversed, a logistic regression on its score, its age, the time from its onset to the half-hour, and any return step, then isotonic regression (a monotone calibration curve). A half-hour with no such candidate gets the odd-month rate.
    • Arm 3: arm 2 plus the candidate's size in noise units.
    • Post hoc arm: arm 2 with the highest-scoring candidate instead of the most recent.

    Results

    • Pre-registered rows. The even-month test set holds 3,358,215 half-hours with 85,650 inside a transfer, for the five shapes S1 to S6 that the injection uses (a shape is how the moved MW varies through the day). The four shapes S2, S3, S4, and S6 are pooled for the contrasts. Brier scores: arm 1 0.02321, arm 2 0.02243, arm 3 0.02229, post hoc arm 0.02239. Area under the curve: 0.710, 0.728, 0.729, and 0.718.
    • Contrasts (change in Brier score against arm 1, 97.5% intervals from resampling the 83 injection batches, each a set of about 10 transfers injected together). C1, arm 2: -3.3% (-7.6% to +1.6%). C2, arm 3: -4.0% (-8.2% to +0.8%). Arm 3 against arm 2: -0.7% (-1.9% to +0.9%). In 3 of the 4 even months that contain half-hours inside a transfer, arm 2 has the lower Brier score.
    • Calibration. The prediction's reliability condition (no probability bin holding at least 5% of half-hours misses the diagonal by more than 0.1) is met. It is met only because 97% of half-hours fall in the lowest bin, so the condition tests almost nothing. In the bins covering probabilities 0.55 to 0.77, only 0.29 to 0.35 of the half-hours lie inside a transfer.
    • Post hoc rows. Dropping the half-hours that the same detector already flags on the uninjected power (which holds unlabelled real switching) changes arm 2 to -1.6% (-6.1% to +2.9%). Also dropping transfers whose donor step is under 4 noise units gives the table below.

    Laboratory shortcuts and caveats

    • A live probability would need a different weather input. The detector's expected-power model uses ERA5 weather estimates, which arrive about 5 days late. A live version would use ENS (ECMWF ensemble) forecasts, and this experiment does not test one.
    • Some fitted constants saw months after the origin. The noise scales, the logistic score, and its threshold were fitted on odd months of the whole record, including months after an origin.
    • The injected power skips the monthly refits. The expected-power model is not refitted on the injected power, and its expected power is floored at 10% of its whole-record median.
    • Rows are sampled and split by month. The lag window contributes every sixth hour. A half-hour inside a transfer takes the calendar month the transfer starts in, and any other half-hour takes the month of its timestamp. Half-hours at one origin can therefore fall on both sides of the odd-even split.
    • The longer draw is smaller than planned. We planned 384 longer transfers and placed 183, because the requirement that a transfer start in a quiet period left few windows long enough.
    • The calibration curve and the regression share training rows. The isotonic regression is fitted on the logistic regression's own odd-month training rows. All scores use even months, so this does not touch the results above.
    • Arm 3 adds little at the receiving substation. A receiving substation's candidates inherit the score and size of the donor substation's alarm, so the size gives arm 3 almost no extra information there.

    What to try next

    One option is an input that measures how unusual the recent lag power is, which needs no switching label (the question of experiment 90, SW2-28). Another is a detector that sees the real switching that the logs hold, since this detector flags only 8% of the half-hours that NGED's logs mark as switching.
