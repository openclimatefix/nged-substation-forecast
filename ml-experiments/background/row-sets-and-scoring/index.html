    Row sets and scoring

    Two scores are comparable only when they cover the same rows, the same series, and the same quantile levels. This page lists the named row sets, the score and its intervals, the tail and curtailment reporting that every forecasting experiment carries, and the retraining defaults. The procedure is in the ml-experiment skill, and terms are in the glossary.

    Series sets

    • The fold-A demand set has 22 demand-side series (D1 to D22): 16 Primaries, 4 BSPs or GSPs, and a battery and a biofuel generator (glossary) (exp 3; fold A; established).
    • The generator experiments score 9 series (6 solar farms and 3 wind farms) (exp 1; fold A; established).
    • The differentiable-physics (DP) series uses five long-history MW Primaries as its decision set (NGED power telemetry) (exp 103; 5 Primaries; established).

    Row sets

    • A row set is a named set of forecast origins, series, and valid times, and experiment_store.read_predictions raises when a prediction set covers a different row set. State the row_set_id in the plan, and read the benchmark from the same row set (code).
    • No experiment scores a valid time on or after 2026-07-01. The ml-experiment skill owns the rule and the reason (code).

    fold_a_daily_gap_v1 The 292 daily 00:00 UTC runs from 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30 that fold_a_every5d_v1 does not hold Experiment 48

    • Read the defining experiment before reusing a row set (code).
    • The DP series uses season-years from July to June. Tuning is 2023-24, the replicate year is 2024-25, and the scored year is 2025-26 (FIRST_SEASON_MONTH in studies.dp_calendar; exp 109; DP series; established).
    • Count eligible long-history sites at each tuning year's first day. Five Primaries qualify at 2024-07-01 (exp 103; 5 Primaries; established).
    • Choose a row set that covers every weekday, and give each experiment a climatology row (exp 3; fold A; provisional).

    The score and its intervals

    • The score, skill, baselines, capacity, and interval level are defined in the glossary. See quantile score, skill, capacity, interval-level convention, and rolling refit. The procedure is in the ml-experiment skill (code).
    • Experiments 33, 34, and 48 use 9 quantile levels (0.05 to 0.95). Experiment 87 and later use 11 levels (those plus 0.01 and 0.99). Scores with different levels cannot be compared (exp 33, 48, 87; all experiments; established).
    • The sample resolves gains of roughly 0.3 points of capacity or more, on 16 Primaries, 9 generator series, and one validation year (experiments README) (exp 3; 16 Primaries, 9 generators, one year; provisional).
    • Half-hours within a day, and members within a run, are strongly correlated, so a row bootstrap understates the interval (code: studies.bootstrap resamples whole months).

    Tails and curtailment

    • Every forecasting experiment reports tail calibration beside the headline score. The tail table gives coverage and pinball loss at 0.01, 0.05, 0.95, and 0.99, through studies.tail_calibration (code).
    • Write q01 to q99, actual, lead_hours, and time_series_id in the predictions, so the tail and curtailment metrics can score them (code).
    • The curtailment metric is studies.node_curtailment (node plus its generators). The headline limit is a fraction f of the node's maximum observed export before the scored year, with f = 0.9 by default and 0.75 and 0.5 as sweeps. The real published thermal limit appears only as a reference mark and in the event census, because NGED curtails so that one more fault still leaves headroom. Metric details: cost-savings metrics (code).
    • Report the manual-heuristic baseline arm (capacity_forecast) as the maintainer's unconfirmed understanding of NGED's practice. Experiment 84 is the pattern to copy (exp 84; 2 bulk supply points; provisional).
    • Headline f = 0.5, instead of the default 0.9, when f = 0.9 gives fewer than 20 event half-hours. At the Boston and Skegness bulk supply points, experiment 255 found 2 event half-hours at f = 0.9 and 5 at f = 0.75, so it headlined f = 0.5, which gives 72. Experiment 277 also headlines the 0.5 limit (exp 255, 277; 2 bulk supply points, 6 Primaries; provisional).
    • Read the curtailment cost as a ranking, not as money saved. BG2 costs GBP 7.35 million more than the GAM stack on the six Primaries at the 0.5 limit, and the windows around the seven logged load transfers hold too few half-hours to separate the two (exp 277; 6 Primaries, one year; provisional).
    • Pair a 00 UTC farm forecast with a 09 UTC node forecast only for valid times before 12 UTC the next day. Later valid times need a run made after the node forecast (exp 255; 2 bulk supply points; provisional).
    • The scored lead band for curtailment is 12 to 36 hours, because scoring needs a stored forecast in that window (curtailment theme; exp 84; curtailment; provisional).
    • NGED's horizon is 1 to 10 days, so a result scored only at short leads carries that caveat. The assembled solar-farm forecast beats climatology at every lead up to 10 days, by 39% at day 1 and 10% at days 8 to 10 (exp 310; 6 farms; provisional).

    Retraining defaults in R&D

    • Do not use quarterly refits, three refits a year, or warm-start boosting as a proxy for monthly retraining (exp 34, 85; XGBoost refit cadence, Primary demand; established).
    • Screen a demand-feature contrast with monthly refits on a rolling 12-month window, and judge cadence, absolute scores, and tail scores with weekly refits. The rolling refit glossary entry gives the settings (exp 34, 48, 81; Primary demand; established).
    • Keep the convex demand model's monthly refits (0.4 s per substation), and give the small neural network that corrects the median no retraining schedule. Quarterly refits of the convex model scored 0.010 points worse than monthly refits (not shown to be acceptable), weekly refits gained nothing, and retraining the neural network quarterly or monthly scored no better than training it once before the scored year (exp 278; 6 Primaries, one year; provisional).
    • Say in each write-up which schedule was used and whether the contrast could depend on it (exp 34, 85; refit schedules; established).

    How to score and gate an experiment

    • Run a known-answer gate before reading any contrast. Inject a known step or shift into real residuals, or use a fake-ensemble gate, so that a null cannot be a wiring fault. Experiments 128, 142, and 172 narrowly missed or failed their gates, so their contrasts are inconclusive, not refuted (exp 111, 128, 142, 172, 175; DP series; established).
    • State the row definition and the lead range before any score. A "sunny" gap was 0.05 MW over six substations, but 63% of it came from D5, and it shrank to 0.015 MW under a different definition of sunny (exp 178; 6 substations; provisional).
    • Use month-block intervals, and report D5 separately where one substation dominates (exp 111, 178, 180; DP Primaries; provisional).
    • Judge calibration where the loss sits: cold nights and daylight bands, not the all-row average. Cold-night q95 coverage was 0.846 under experiment 160's smooth spread table and 0.896 with widening of the upper quantiles only, while ensemble spread added nothing over all rows (exp 160, 164, 169; DP Primaries; provisional).
    • Score a new model component on top of the family's research default, not on a partial stack. The drift term improved the score by 0.40 on the bare GAM-K and worsened arm D by 0.03 once the filter, the winter night offset, and recipe R were in (exp 167, 170; DP Primaries; provisional). The research default rule names the default and says when a win is adopted.
    • Gains do not always remove the same error: the correction network and the two-number solar summary added on arm A. Together they scored 0.136 below arm A, 99% of the sum of their single gains of 0.099 and 0.038 (exp 220; 5 Primaries, one year; provisional).
    • Quote a neural network's score per seed, or say the seeds were averaged. Averaging the quantiles of the three seeds of the correction network scored 0.123 below arm D at six substations, against 0.104 for the mean of the single-seed scores (exp 201, 220; 6 substations, one year; provisional).
