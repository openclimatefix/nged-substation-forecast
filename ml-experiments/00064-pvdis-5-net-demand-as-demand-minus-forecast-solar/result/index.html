    Experiment 64: does demand minus forecast solar match the best guess? (PVDIS-5)

    Take-home message: giving the XGBoost model the forecast solar output as a starting offset (a fixed amount added to every tree prediction), with its weight fitted on the training window, beat d0, the best-guess recipe of experiment 33, by 0.15 points of capacity. Training the XGBoost model on gross demand (metered power plus the solar panels' output) and then subtracting the forecast solar output was not shown to score within 0.10 points of d0. The starting-offset forecast has a separate solar term, was no worse than d0 over all leads, and scored lower than d0 at leads under 10 days. The open questions are whether the gain survives a capacity table that is current for the validation year, and why the starting offset also scores better at 10 days and beyond, where the weight on the solar term is set to zero.

    We asked whether a forecast of the power that a Primary substation draws can be split into ordinary demand and the output of the solar panels behind the substation without losing accuracy. A Primary substation is a 33/11 kV or 66/11 kV substation in the electricity distribution network. The substation's meter reads demand minus the panels' output. We built four forecasts that each carry an explicit solar term, which is the panels' capacity times a forecast of how much sunnier or cloudier the day will be than usual. We scored them on five Primary substations over daylight half-hours of April to September. We compared each with d0, and with the same forecast given the solar term of another date. The score is the quantile score, an error measure in which lower is better, and 1 point is 1% of a substation's capacity. Three of the four forecasts scored 0.06 to 0.15 points lower than d0. The fourth was not shown to score within 0.10 points of d0.

    The verdict is supported. The decision rule is in the front matter and was committed before any code ran. Contrast N is an arm's quantile score minus the quantile score of d0. A negative value favours the arm. Contrast T is an arm's quantile score minus that of its placebo twin, the same arm given the forecast solar shape of another date, so T measures what the forecast of the sunshine adds. An arm passes when two conditions hold. The upper end of N is below +0.10 points, so the arm is within the margin of d0. The upper end of T is below 0, so the arm beats its placebo twin. Arms sf, a, and am pass; b beats its twin but is not shown to be within the margin of d0. A gain is reported where the upper end of N at leads under 48 hours is below -0.10, which holds for a and am.

    The four arms

    Every arm uses experiment 46's d1l inputs and recipe (experiment 33's XGBoost quantile model, trained on the ENS control member, without radiation inputs) and adds the solar term s: the capacity times the calibrated anomaly of the ensemble-mean ENS sunshine forecast, in power over capacity. The weight w on s is fitted per series, lead band, and season.

    • sf adds w * s to the stored d1l quantiles after the fit. No XGBoost model is refitted.
    • a adds the capacity times the calibrated ENS solar shape as one extra input column.
    • am gives XGBoost the base margin w * s, so the trees fit only the remainder.
    • b trains on gross demand (the metered power plus the capacity times the CAMS solar shape), with lagged inputs converted to gross demand the same way, and subtracts the capacity times the forecast solar shape from each ensemble member's quantiles at inference.

    Each arm has a placebo twin (sf_twin, a_twin, am_twin, b_twin) that replaces the forecast solar shape with that of another forecast run in the same series, local month, half-hour, and lead band. The reference rows are d0, d1l, climatology, and manual_heuristic (experiment 33).

    Arm am is the best arm, and b is the only arm not shown to match d0

    The scores are close together, and the paired contrasts are what separate the arms. The 95% intervals of the absolute scores overlap because every arm's error rises and falls with the weather from week to week, which pairing removes. Absolute scores, in points of capacity, with 95% intervals:

    The twin of b scores 1.07 points worse than b. One candidate explanation is that b subtracts the shape with a weight of 1 built in, so a wrong-dated shape does more harm there than in the arms with a fitted weight; no arm tests this. The size of T for b is not evidence that b uses the forecast sunshine better than the other arms.

    The gain sits mostly within 6 days of the forecast start

    The gain of a is clear up to 6 days and the gain of sf from 1 to 6 days, and both are gone beyond, and am stays below d0 at every lead. At 10 days and beyond, am is -0.082 points (-0.126 to -0.038) from d0 although its weight there is zero, a is +0.036 (+0.006 to +0.065), and sf is +0.013 (-0.019 to +0.051). The weight is zero in that band because the calibrated ENS anomaly has a correlation of 0.03 with the CAMS anomaly there on the training rows (0.80, 0.76, 0.58, and 0.27 in the four shorter bands), and the fitted slope ranged from -8.2 to +6.2 across series and seasons. The 10-plus-day gain of am is unexplained. One candidate is the starting point: am starts from its margin alone, while d1l and a start from XGBoost's estimated base score.

    Arm b is worse than d0 at 6 days and beyond. Its N is +0.249 points (0.035 to 0.479) at 6 to 10 days and +0.296 (0.132 to 0.462) at 10 days and beyond, where the ENS forecast of the subtracted term has little or no skill (its correlation with the CAMS anomaly is 0.27 at 6 to 10 days and 0.03 beyond). This experiment does not test that as the cause.

    The fitted weights are below 1, and lagged power does not explain why

    The fitted weight on the ENS shift is -0.60 to -0.29 at leads under 10 days, well short of the -1 that a perfect shift of the right size would take. These are the means over the five series of the slope of the out-of-fold residual (actual minus median forecast) on the shift that sf applies, fitted per lead band and season on the training window. The slope on the oracle shift (the CAMS anomaly) is -0.80 to -0.47, to compare with experiment 51's -0.69 to -0.42. The effective weights of the arms, measured as the slope of each arm's median minus d1l's median on minus the shift, are 0.49 for sf, 0.41 for a, 0.31 for am (on top of its fitted margin), and 0.64 for b, against the constant 1 built into b.

    Lagged power absorbs none of the sunshine anomaly, cloud proxies absorb about 0.15 at leads under 2 days, and about -0.73 remains with neither. Experiment 51 left open whether the slope below 1 comes from the XGBoost model already absorbing part of the anomaly through its inputs, or from error in the CAMS sunshine estimate. Four cross-fitted variants of d1l (seed 0) give the median oracle slope below, over five series and two seasons.

    Arms sf and am beat d0 in every valid month

    sf and am are below d0 in all six months, a in five (+0.005 in July), and b loses from April to July and wins in August and September. The mean of b's N is +0.460 in April and +0.416 in July, and -0.173 in August and -0.327 in September. These are the sign of a mean, not an interval.

    Departures from the plan

    The experiment departed from its plan in the 11 ways below, all decided before any arm was scored. None changes the hypothesis, the prediction, or the decision rule.

    1. The weight is zero in the 10-plus-day band. The ENS anomaly has a correlation of 0.03 with the CAMS anomaly there, and the fitted slope ranged from -8.2 to +6.2. The rule was fixed from the weights table, which uses training rows only, before any arm was scored.
    2. The capacity is divided by experiment 45's recovery ratio. The refitted season-year constant for 2024/25 is divided by experiment 45's stored recovery ratio (0.71 to 0.79, from an injection test on tuning farms), as experiment 45 defines capacity. The capacities used, in MW, are D9 2.751, D10 2.356, D11 3.457, D14 3.705, and D16 3.578. D14 uses experiment 40's 2023/24 constant, because the experiment 64 refit has no 2024/25 constant for it (all of its weeks are unobserved).
    3. The no-cloud-proxy variant drops 3 columns, dew_point_temperature_2m, precipitation, and pressure_surface, because d1l has no precipitation_type or pressure_msl column.
    4. The placebo is a seeded cyclic shift, not a random permutation. It moves each row's shape by between a quarter and three quarters of its group (series, local month, half-hour, and lead band). A random permutation paired runs that forecast the same valid time, so the permuted anomaly stayed correlated with the real one. After the change, the correlation of the twin's anomaly with the real anomaly is -0.012 on the 256,230 validation rows.
    5. Arm b drops training rows whose lag has no shape. At inference the gross lag is missing (NaN) there, on 2.8% of validation rows for the lag of the same time the day before and 8.6% for the week before. No sensitivity read of this exists. The climatology fallback was not needed.
    6. Two small confounds remain. The reference temperature of sf_temp uses the control member's temperature. Arm am starts from the margin alone, while d1l and a start from XGBoost's base score, a small confound in the a against am contrast.
    7. climatology and manual_heuristic lack 40 of the 68,903 rows, so they are scored on shared rows, outside the paired bootstrap.
    8. The plan's refit count was wrong. The experiment ran 75 refits (5 arms, 3 seeds, 5 series), not "about 90 plus 60".
    9. The weights rest on little data. The training window holds two April-to-June periods and one July-to-September period (2024). The 4 time-block folds of the cross-fit group months by month index modulo 4, so adjacent months train each other.
    10. The held capacity is stale. The held value is 5% to 19% below experiment 45's 2025/26 constants at the five series (D9 5%, D10 17%, D11 15%, D14 13%, D16 19%). D14 holds the 2023/24 constant of experiment 40. A capacity that is too low plausibly weakens the solar term and so works against the placebo contrast, which would make the gains conservative; no run tests this.
    11. The sunniest-decile read and the a against am contrasts are exploratory (95%), not corrected across the four arms.

    What the next experiment should answer

    The evidence supports am as a candidate for a model release, and the decision to port belongs to the maintainer and the roadmap. The next experiment should answer four questions that remain open:

    • Does the gain survive a current capacity? Refit sf and am with capacities that follow the validation year (experiment 45's 2025/26 constants are 6% to 23% higher), because the capacity held here is stale.
    • Do the weights hold with more training data? The July-to-September weights rest on one summer, so a window with a second summer would show whether the weights move.
    • Why does am also beat d0 at 10 days and beyond? The weight is zero there, so the base score, the margin in training rows at shorter leads, and the trees are the candidates.
    • Can the live service supply am's inputs? The live service cannot use am as built: it needs the capacity table, the table of weights, and the ENS calibration slopes at predict time, and am sets a base margin on each prediction matrix. Whether the serving path can do so is a question for the roadmap.

    Arm b stays untested in two respects. Its gross lags are missing on 2.8% and 8.6% of validation rows with no sensitivity read, and its loss to d0 at 6 days and beyond could come from the ENS forecast of the subtracted term, which has no skill there. A variant that subtracts the shape only inside 6 days would separate the two.
