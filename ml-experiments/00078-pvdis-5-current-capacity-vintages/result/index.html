    Experiment 78: does a re-estimated solar capacity lower the error? (PVDIS-5, check 2)

    Take-home message: re-estimating the solar capacity before each forecast run lowers the quantile score of am, the best-scoring forecast of experiment 64, by only 0.018 points, so a capacity table refreshed about once a season-year is enough. The quantile score is an error measure in which lower is better, and 1 point is 1% of a substation's capacity. A larger finding sits beside this one. XGBoost starts each model from an estimated starting prediction (its base score). Starting from zero instead lowered the score of the reference model d0 by 0.07 points, and by about 0.10 points for the model that carries no solar term, so part of the gain that experiment 64 reported over d0 was a start-at-zero effect. The solar term still lowers the score by 0.100 points against a d0 that starts from zero. The next experiment should test the starting point, which needs no extra production work to change.

    We asked whether a better estimate of how much solar capacity sits behind each Primary substation improves a forecast of the substation's power. A Primary substation steps electricity down from 33 kV or 66 kV to 11 kV. Experiment 64 forecast power with an XGBoost model that starts from a solar term: the substation's solar capacity times a forecast of how sunny the day will be, added as a base margin (a fixed starting prediction supplied for each row). That experiment fixed the capacity at the value estimated from data before July 2025. Here we re-estimated the capacity before every forecast run from only the data available then, and predicted again with the same fitted XGBoost models. The score fell by 0.018 points. We also refitted two reference models, d1l and d0, to find out whether the start-at-zero effect that experiment 76 suspected is real. The start-at-zero effect is real.

    The verdict is small gain. The pre-registered rule is in the front matter and was committed before any code ran. The headline contrast C1 is the quantile score of am_sy (am with the season-year vintage of the capacity) minus that of am (experiment 64's held capacity), over all leads. The upper end of C1 is -0.004, below zero but not below the threshold of -0.05. The threshold is a third of experiment 64's gain over d0, because a weekly refit of the capacity table is a production step that a fixed table does not need. C3 and C4 in the table confirm that the solar term carries solar information.

    Contrast Difference in quantile score 98.75% interval
    C1: am_sy minus am, all leads -0.018 -0.036 to -0.004
    C2: am_sy minus am, leads under 48 hours -0.033 -0.086 to +0.012
    C3: am_sy minus am0, all leads (the solar term) -0.190 -0.267 to -0.118
    C4: am_sy minus am_sy_twin, all leads (a wrong-date sunshine forecast) -0.357 -0.496 to -0.227

    The values are in points of capacity and come from 50,000 resamples of whole ISO weeks of forecast start (29 weeks) and of 3 seeds, on experiment 64's 68,903 scored rows per seed: daylight half-hours of April to September at the five Primary substations D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16, in July to September 2025 and April to June 2026. am0 is the XGBoost model with a base margin of zero and no solar term (experiment 76). C2 does not meet the rule for a gain at short leads, because its interval includes zero.

    The arms

    • am, am_twin, d0, d1l: experiment 64's stored forecasts. am has a base margin of the weight times the held capacity times the forecast sunshine anomaly. d0 is the best-guess recipe, and d1l is d0 without the two radiation inputs.
    • am0: experiment 76's stored forecasts: am's recipe with a margin of zero in every row.
    • am_sy, am_sy_twin, am_wk: experiment 64's fitted am and am_twin models, predicted with the capacity of the season-year vintage (am_sy, am_sy_twin) or the weekly vintage (am_wk). Nothing is refitted, so the weights and the capacity in the training margin stay experiment 64's.
    • d1l_r, d0_r, d0_m0: d1l and d0 refitted here with experiment 64's recipe and seeds, and d0 refitted with a zero base margin.

    The season-year vintage re-fits experiment 45's season-year constant before each forecast run. The fit uses the usable half-hours of the weeks up to two weeks before the Monday of the run's week, and divides by experiment 45's recovery ratio. The recovery ratio is experiment 45's measured fraction of injected solar capacity that the fit recovers, and a usable half-hour is one with an observed power and a CAMS solar estimate. A run takes the capacity from the first of three steps that applies. Step 1 is the vintage's constant for the run's season-year (September to August), once that season-year has 8 observed weeks. Step 2 is the previous season-year's constant. Step 3 is experiment 64's held capacity. Step 1 supplied 84% of runs at D9, D10, and D16, 47% at D11, and 29% at D14. D14's weeks from 2023-09-04 to 2025-08-25 are unobserved, so step 3 supplies the other 71% of its runs. The weekly vintage averages the 4 latest observed weeks of experiment 45's frozen weekly fit (a free capacity per week with a fixed penalty on changes), from the vintage with the latest origin up to a week before the run.

    The estimated capacity rose 13% to 31% above the held value by May 2026

    The vintages move away from the held capacity in November 2025 and sit 13% to 31% above it in May 2026. D9's vintage falls 14% below the held value in November, and D10's and D16's rise 9% and 10% above it. The season-year vintage in June 2026 is 3.0 MW at D9 (held 2.751 MW), 2.9 at D10 (2.356), 4.3 at D11 (3.457), 4.8 at D14 (3.705), and 4.6 at D16 (3.578), against experiment 45's final 2025/26 constants of 2.9, 2.9, 4.1, 4.2, and 4.4. The weekly vintage is higher and noisier. In November 2025 the new season-year's own constant takes over at D9, which is why the vintage there falls (2.37 MW against 2.75). Experiment 45 found that the latest weeks of a real-time fit are the least accurate.

    The gain is small and sits mostly in May 2026

    The point estimate is negative at every lead, but only the 2-to-6-day and 10-days-and-beyond intervals exclude zero. The effect is almost zero at 10 days and beyond. The point estimates of am_sy minus am are -0.039 (-0.097 to +0.014) at 0 to 24 hours, -0.027 (-0.061 to +0.003) at 1 to 2 days, -0.029 (-0.053 to -0.009) at 2 to 6 days, -0.013 (-0.032 to +0.003) at 6 to 10 days, and -0.006 (-0.012 to -0.001) at 10 days and beyond. By valid month, the read is about zero from July to September 2025 (the vintage is within 4% of the held capacity there), -0.035 (-0.097 to +0.002) in April 2026, -0.058 (-0.100 to -0.028) in May, and -0.008 (-0.021 to +0.015) in June. At D16 the read is -0.045 (-0.073 to -0.020), at D14 -0.022 (-0.044 to -0.002), at D10 -0.018 (-0.034 to -0.003), at D11 -0.012 (-0.032 to +0.005), and at D9 +0.006 (-0.003 to +0.018). These reads are exploratory, at 95%.

    The weekly vintage does no better than the season-year vintage. am_wk minus am_sy is -0.005 (-0.013 to +0.003), and am_wk minus am is -0.023 (-0.042 to -0.007) over all leads and -0.054 (-0.116 to +0.004) under 48 hours. A weekly refresh would lower the score by a further 0.005 points at most, which the intervals do not separate from zero. Restricted to runs where step 1 supplied the capacity, am_sy minus am is -0.022 (-0.037 to -0.008), so the fallback to the held value dilutes the effect slightly.

    XGBoost's estimated starting point raises the score by 0.07 to 0.10 points

    A refit of d1l in this pipeline scores the same as experiment 46's stored d1l, so the gap that experiment 76 saw is not a difference between pipelines. d1l_r minus d1l is -0.0004 (-0.0020 to +0.0013), and d0_r minus d0 is +0.0004 (-0.0018 to +0.0025).

    D1: d1l_r minus am0, 10 days and beyond, no 14-day correction +0.102 +0.035 to +0.169

    The pre-registered rule reads D1 as a real starting-point effect: the lower end of D1 is above zero and its point estimate is above 0.05. D2 gives the same reading for d0. Over all leads, d0_m0 minus d0_r is -0.071 (-0.099 to -0.047, exploratory), and under 48 hours -0.078 (-0.118 to -0.036).

    XGBoost estimates one base score per quantile level, and the score rises with the level. For d0_r at D9 it runs from 0.403 at the 0.05 level to 0.899 at the 0.95 level (power over capacity), and it is identical across seeds and for d1l_r. The recipe trains a fixed 500 rounds at a learning rate of 0.05, with no early stopping, so no interaction with early stopping exists. The reason a start at zero scores better is not tested here.

    The solar term survives the correction for the starting point. am_sy is 0.100 points (-0.142 to -0.061) below d0_m0 over all leads and 0.227 (-0.361 to -0.103) under 48 hours (post hoc, exploratory), against 0.171 and 0.305 below d0. Experiment 64's gain of am over d0, 0.152, is 0.082 (-0.113 to -0.052) over d0_m0.

    Departures from the plan

    1. The weekly vintage drops weeks older than 8 weeks before the run's Monday. The code review found that D14's weekly vintage would otherwise average weeks from 2023, from before the gap in its record. The change was made before any arm was scored.
    2. The first run of the statistics script grouped the bootstrap by month, not by week. A column name was wrong, so the intervals rested on 7 blocks. The corrected run uses 29 weeks. The verdict changed from refuted to small gain, so every number on this page comes from the corrected run.
    3. Six exploratory reads were added after the results were seen, and are labelled post hoc: d0_m0 against d0_r under 48 hours, am_sy and am against d0_m0 (each over all leads and under 48 hours), and am0 against d0_m0. The reads against d0_m0 carry the claim that the solar term survives a zero start.

    Limits

    • The training margin keeps the held capacity. The weights, and the capacity inside every training row, are experiment 64's, so the contrast measures the capacity at prediction only. A refit with a vintage capacity in the training rows is a larger design that this experiment does not test.
    • The recovery ratio is the full-record value. Each vintage divides by one number per series, from experiment 45's injection test on the full record, so the vintages carry a small look-ahead that am and the vintage arms share.
    • One validation year and 29 weeks of forecast start. July to September 2025 and April to June 2026 are two seasons of one year, and the 2026 months hold the gain.
    • D14 keeps its held capacity for 71% of runs. The vintage for D14 matters only from March 2026.
    • The reason a zero start helps is not tested. The experiment shows the size, not the mechanism.

    What the next experiment should answer

    Does a zero base margin help every XGBoost model in the production recipe, and at which leads? The effect is 0.07 to 0.10 points on the Primary demand models of this experiment, larger than the effect of the capacity, and it costs nothing in production. A test across the series and model types that the best guess d0 covers (BSP and GSP demand, solar, and wind) would settle whether the base score belongs to the production recipe. If the capacity matters for a later model release, the evidence here is consistent with refreshing the capacity table once a season-year.
