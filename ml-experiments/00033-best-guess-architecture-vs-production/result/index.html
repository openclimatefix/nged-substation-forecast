    Experiment 33: does the best-guess architecture beat production, N1, and manual_heuristic?

    We asked whether our best-guess forecasting design beats the design now in production (the stored xgboost_baseline), a single-step model (N1), and a simple baseline (manual_heuristic), for demand, solar, and wind. The best guess is N1 plus a 14-day correction for Primary substation demand, N1 alone for bulk supply point and grid supply point demand, and mixtures of models trained on ERA5 weather (an estimate of past weather) for solar and wind farms. We scored all of them on the same forecast runs from July 2025 to June 2026, for 20 demand series, 6 solar farms, and 3 wind farms. The best guess lowered the error against production by 4.89 points of capacity for Primary demand, 3.81 for solar, and 6.27 for wind, and every interval lay wholly below its threshold.

    Supported: the best guess lowered the quantile score below production's by 4.89 points of capacity for Primary demand (98.33% interval -5.66 to -4.06), 3.81 for solar (-5.07 to -2.64), and 6.27 for wind (-7.65 to -5.28), and each interval lies wholly below its threshold. The quantile score is twice the mean pinball loss over nine quantile levels, the estimate of the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), in points of capacity (1 point is 1% of a series' effective capacity). The best guess for the 16 Primary substations (substations that step power down to the local distribution network) is N1, an XGBoost model per series trained on the ensemble forecast of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ENS) with recent power as inputs, plus an additive correction by the mean error of the previous 14 days and a spread factor of 1.15 on the forecast band. For bulk supply point (BSP) and grid supply point (GSP) substations the best guess is N1 alone. For solar and wind it is a mixture over the 51 ENS members of a model trained on ERA5 weather (the ERA5 reanalysis, an estimate of past weather), with a spread factor and, for wind, a power-curve margin. Production is the stored xgboost_baseline forecast. The rows are the 73 runs of fold_a_every5d_v1 for the 20 demand series and the 53 Tuesday runs of fold_a_tuesdays_v1 for the 6 solar and 3 wind farms, July 2025 to June 2026. The planned intervals use month-block resampling; the level is 98.33% for C1 to C3, and the other contrasts use their family's Bonferroni level (the Planned contrasts section and the appendix give each).

    Production's forecast band is far too narrow. The band from its 5% quantile to its 95% quantile covers 10% of Primary cases and 20% of solar and wind cases, and its 0.95 quantile is exceeded in 49% of Primary cases, 39% of solar cases, and 33% of wind cases. The best guess exceeds its 0.95 quantile in 7%, 4%, and 7% of cases. The 14-day correction accounts for 1.38 of the 4.89-point Primary gap (99.44% interval -2.10 to -0.68; C8), and production still scores 8.59 with the correction and its band is still narrow (coverage 9%). No arm widened production's band, so the page does not say how much of the rest is calibration.

    For Primary demand, the best guess beats manual_heuristic by 0.59 points (-0.94 to -0.29; C4), and the correction supplies nearly all of its gain over N1. N1 with the correction scores 5.34 against 6.33 for N1 (-1.45; C7), and the spread factor takes a further 0.02 off. climatology scores 7.46. On BSP and GSP demand, N1 beats production by 7.25 points (-8.46 to -6.18; C13) and manual_heuristic by 1.04 (-2.01 to -0.09; C14), and the correction does not help (+0.38, -0.12 to +0.92; C16), as experiment 21 found with the same 4 series. Production's correction raises its twCRPS at historical_p99 from 0.067 to 0.107.

    Monthly retraining without the correction scored 5.12 on seed 0, against 5.32 for the best guess on the same seed. Against N1 on seed 0 (6.33) it lowers the score by 1.58 points (-2.18 to -0.86; C12). Adding the correction to monthly retraining raised the seed-0 score from 5.12 to 5.24, and monthly retraining with the correction scored 0.11 below N1 with the correction, an interval that includes zero (-0.24 to +0.03; C11). Monthly retraining also gives the model 12 months of newer data, so the page does not say whether the drift of a model frozen in June 2025 or the newer data explains the gain, and no arm separates them. The arm has one seed and one validation year, so it is a lead and not a ranking. The other notable demand ideas moved the score little: the weekly analogues by -0.05 (-0.10 to -0.01; C9) and one model per type by +0.13 (+0.02 to +0.23; C10).

    For solar and wind, N1 trained on ENS scored within 0.1 and 0.3 points of the best guess over all leads. The best guess minus N1 is -0.08 for solar (-0.24 to +0.12; C17) and -0.32 for wind (-0.91 to +0.20; C20), both unresolved. For wind, N1 scores lower than the best guess at 0 to 24 hours (5.91 against 6.26) and 1 to 2 days (6.51 against 6.70), and the best guess wins only from 2 days ahead (9.02 against 9.56 at 2 to 6 days). The best guess beats manual_heuristic and climatology for solar and wind (C18, C19, C21, C22). No notable idea resolved a gain: CAMS-trained solar -0.02 (C23), the physics hybrid 0.00 (C25), physics alone +0.08 (+0.02 to +0.16, worse; C26), and wind with the power-over-wind-cubed column -0.05 (-0.11 to +0.02; C24).

    Tail scores at historical_p99 separate the arms only where the data allow, and the exceedance counts show why. The best guess differs from production on twCRPS above historical_p99 by -0.003 for Primary demand (95% interval -0.013 to +0.006; C1), -0.004 for solar (-0.007 to -0.001; C2), and 0.000 for wind (C3). For BSP and GSP, N1 is better than production by 0.014 (-0.023 to -0.007; C13). For solar the best guess is worse than N1 by 0.005 (+0.001 to +0.016; C17), and for Primary demand its twCRPS and Brier score at historical_p99 (0.063 and 0.0083) are no better than N1's (0.058 and 0.0079) or manual_heuristic's (0.059 and 0.0081). At historical_p95 the best guess is better than production on twCRPS for all three families (-0.07, -0.24, and -0.04; C1 to C3), and for solar it is worse than N1 (+0.023, +0.003 to +0.057; C17). The wind threshold sits near capacity, and no arm in the event table scored a hit on wind at historical_p99. For Primary demand at 1 to 2 days ahead (seed 0), the best guess flagged 168 of 417 half-hours above historical_p99 with 164 false alarms, against 59 hits and 106 false alarms for production and 21 hits and 22 false alarms for manual_heuristic. The Brier score at historical_p99 mainly measures the scorer's tail assumption, an exponential decay of the exceedance probability beyond the 0.95 quantile.

    Better weather would help wind most and Primary demand least. The ERA5 arms are ceilings for a weather product as accurate as ERA5, because ERA5 and CAMS are estimates of past weather and arrive days late. The ERA5 arms lowered the score by 0.11 points for Primary demand (-0.17 to -0.05; C27), 0.79 for BSP and GSP (C32), 1.05 for solar (C28), and 5.46 for wind (-6.91 to -3.90; C29). CAMS shortwave lowered the solar score by 2.09 (C28b) and the Primary demand score by 0.18, 0.07 more than ERA5 on all seven columns did (C31b). A demand model retrained on ERA5 and fed ERA5 lowered the score by 0.27 against N1 (C30). The demand and solar gain is zero or slightly negative at 0 to 24 hours (C27 +0.03, C28 +0.17), and for wind it grows from 0.71 at 0 to 24 hours to 7.68 at 10 days or more. Wind speed at 100 m alone supplies 4.24 of the 5.46 points.

    Limits

    • The monthly-retraining arm has one seed and one validation year. The arm's interval resamples months only. Its training rows after June 2025 are the control member of every daily run (364 runs, extracted as the stage 1 pilot did and checked to match it exactly on the run of 2025-07-01).
    • The correction was tested on runs 5 days apart. A daily service would build the history from forecasts at leads under 1 day.
    • The intervals resample months and seeds, not series. With 3 wind farms, 6 solar farms, and 4 BSP and GSP series, they understate the difference between farms and substations.
    • Production for the generators is the stored forecast of xgboost_baseline. The stored members give a very narrow band, and the appendix lists xgboost_baseline_retrain_638 and xgboost_baseline_retrain_790 as exploratory rows. Production has 124 of 282,916 solar and wind cases without a stored forecast, and manual_heuristic 195.
    • The generator thresholds sit near capacity. historical_p99 of power over capacity ranges from 0.59 to 1.01 across the 29 series, so a high threshold for solar and wind grades clipping.
    • Several contrasts were expected to be inconclusive and are. CAMS-trained solar, the physics hybrid, and the wind column lie within their intervals of zero.
    • The ceiling arms are not deployable. ERA5 and CAMS are estimates of past weather and not ground truth, so the gap bounds the headroom of a product with their accuracy at the site's grid cell.

    Checks and predictions that did not hold

    • Reproduction: the rebuilt best guess matches experiment 19's stored set exactly (maximum difference 0), and the refitted N1 scores 6.328 against 6.329 for experiment 13's N1.
    • Cases: each family is scored on the cases every arm holds (Primary 768,073 of 805,410, with climatology the smallest arm); each contrast uses the cases of its own two arms. The fewest members of a stored arm are 51 for production and climatology, 1 for solar manual_heuristic, and 8 for wind manual_heuristic, so the exceedance rates of manual_heuristic carry finite-ensemble bias.
    • Predictions that failed: the twCRPS and the Brier score at historical_p99 did not rank the arms as the quantile score does, and the ERA5-trained generator models did not resolvably beat the ENS-trained N1 (C17, C20).

    Plan, written before any code ran

    One experiment compares, on one validation year, the architecture that experiments 1 to 32 recommend against the deployed recipe, three baselines, six notable ideas, and a perfect-weather ceiling. Units are points of capacity (1 point is 1% of a series' effective capacity). The validation rows are the 73 runs of fold_a_every5d_v1 for the 20 demand series (16 Primary substations, 2 BSP, and 2 GSP) and the 53 Tuesday runs of fold_a_tuesdays_v1 for the 6 solar farms and 3 wind farms. No valid time on or after 2026-07-01 is scored.

    Arms

    An arm whose recipe, rows, and seeds an earlier experiment already ran is read from that experiment's stored predictions, not refitted. experiment_store.read_predictions refuses sets on a different row set, and every reused set has three seeds. The table names the source of each reused arm. Every fit below is new code in this experiment's folder.

    Production for the generators is read, not refitted. The power_forecasts Delta table holds xgboost_baseline forecasts for all 31 series, including the 9 generators, on fold mid_2025_to_mid_2026. The arm uses experiment_name xgboost_baseline, the recipe experiment 13 scored as production for demand. The table also holds xgboost_baseline_retrain_638 and xgboost_baseline_retrain_790, reported as exploratory rows. Where a stored production forecast lacks a Tuesday run, the arm is scored on the cases all arms hold, and result.md states the count of dropped cases.

    N1 for generators uses the 00013 recipe with no calendar columns. The recipe, applied to each generator, is one XGBoost model per farm with the nine-level quantile loss, trained on the ENS control member of every daily run with valid times from 2024-04-01 to 2025-06-30, recency weights with a half-life of 2 years anchored at 2025-06-30, the technology's common columns (nine for solar, six for wind), lead_hours, and three power lags from the forecast origin (newest usable observation at origin minus 3.5 hours, latest earlier same half-hour, latest earlier same weekday and half-hour). The calendar columns are left out, because experiment 1 found calendar columns let an ENS-trained generator model memorise valid times (+3.40 wind, +1.13 solar). The 51 member forecasts are pooled as in 00013. No correction is applied to N1 for generators.

    Monthly retraining refits each Primary substation's N1 model on the first day of each month. The refits run from 2025-07 to 2026-06 with a half-life of 0.5 years anchored at the refit date, on the ENS control member of every daily run (the NWP store holds a daily run from 2024-04-01) with valid times at least 3.5 hours before the refit. A run is forecast by the latest refit on or before its origin, so the refit is chosen by origin and not by valid time. The 2025-07-01 refit is close to the half-life-0.5-year fit trained to 2025-06-30 but stops 3 hours earlier. The 14-day correction history spans a change of model at each month boundary, and the page says so. The arm is reported with and without the correction. Building control-member features for the daily runs after 2025-06-30 is new code; if that costs more than about 2 hours, the arm falls back to the 73 validation runs' control members and the page says so. The arm uses seed 0 only (about 1 hour for 16 series by 12 refits), so it is contrasted with the seed-0 rows of the reference arms, and its interval resamples months only.

    Perfect-weather arms

    The perfect-weather arms measure the headroom from better weather, not a deployable model. ERA5 and CAMS are estimates of past weather, not ground truth, so figures label the arms "ERA5-weather ceiling". ERA5 reaches us about 5 days late and CAMS is satellite-derived after the fact, so no serving system could use either at the target time. The page labels every perfect-weather result "ceiling".

    • Demand: the arms ens, era5_all, era5_shortwave, era5_temperature_2m, and era5_wind_speed_10m of 00022 are reused for all 20 series (N1H; 00019 C5 found the half-life neutral). Their contrasts are against 00022's own ens, never the 00019 set. Each is reported with and without the correction, and the correction and spread factor are applied here wherever 00022's stored set lacks them. Two variants are new fits: shortwave from CAMS at the series' own location with ERA5 for the other six columns, and the model retrained on ERA5 over the same 2024-04 to 2025-06 window and fed ERA5. For BSP and GSP the arms are N1 without correction. The contrasts use the forms with correction but without the spread factor, because the factor of 1.15 was tuned for 51-member mixtures and a single input has a narrower band; the forms with the spread factor are reported as exploratory.
    • Solar: the ERA5-trained model of the best guess is fed ERA5 at the valid time (one input per valid time, so the forecast is the model's 9 quantiles); a CAMS-trained variant is fed CAMS shortwave with ERA5 for the other eight columns. Single-variable swap: shortwave only.
    • Wind: ERA5 10 m and 100 m wind speed and direction at the valid time, with the power-curve margin reading ERA5 100 m speed; single-variable swap: 100 m wind speed alone.
    • Spread factor: for solar and wind, each perfect-weather arm is reported with and without the best guess's spread factor, refitted monthly on the arm's own earlier cases as 00020 did. A single-input band is narrower than a pooled mixture, so the unrefitted factor may mislead.
    • Lead bands: 0 to 24 h, 1 to 2 d, 2 to 6 d, 6 to 10 d, 10 days or more. For solar and wind the perfect-weather arm does not depend on lead, so the by-lead gap is the best guess's degradation.
    • Weather available at forecast time: neither ERA5 nor CAMS is available at forecast time. Lagged power uses the observation at least 3.5 hours old, as in 00013; no other lag-time weather enters any arm.

    Metrics

    All arms store 9 quantiles at 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 0.9, and 0.95. For manual_heuristic, production, and climatology, the quantiles are those of the stored members at the 9 levels, as 00013 took them.

    • MAE of the median: the absolute error of q50.
    • Quantile score (the CRPS estimate): as in 00019.
    • twCRPS at historical_p99: replace every quantile and the observation by max(z, r) and take the quantile score. r is the 99th percentile of the series' power over capacity in the constraint direction (high positive power for demand and for generation) over the series' full history to 2026-06-30, as evaluation-metrics.md defines it. The threshold is a fixed scoring constant that no model sees. The page reports, per series, the count of half-hours and of distinct days above the threshold. For the generators the threshold sits near capacity (capacity is itself a P99), so historical_p95 is a co-primary line for solar and wind and an exploratory line for demand.
    • Brier score at the same threshold: the exceedance probability p(r) comes from the quantile function: linear interpolation of the level against the quantile between q05 and q95; above q95 the probability falls by half for every width q95 - q90 beyond q95, starting from 0.05 (an exponential tail), and is 0 beyond q95 when q95 = q90; below q05 the exceedance probability is 1 - 0.05 * 2^(-(q05 - r) / (q10 - q05)). The tails are approximations, and at historical_p99 most cases lie in the upper tail, so the Brier score there mainly measures the tail assumption. The page therefore also reports the Brier score at historical_p95, which is interpolated, and the hit rate and false-alarm count at the threshold for the 1-to-2-day and 2-to-6-day bands. The twCRPS ignores all mass above q95.
    • Exceedance rates of the 0.90 and 0.95 quantiles: the share of cases where the observation exceeds that quantile; the aim is 10% and 5%. The rates need no model of the tail, but they are calibration checks and not tail-skill rankings. The page reports each stored arm's member count; cases of manual_heuristic with fewer than 13 members are flagged, and its rates are read against a finite-ensemble reference.
    • Thresholds and the flexibility use case: NGED buys flexibility ahead of an expected constraint, so the exceedance metrics are also reported for leads of 1 to 2 days and 2 to 6 days, the horizons at which procurement decisions are made. Metrics are never ranked on half-hours chosen by the observed load.
    • Absolute scores for every arm are reported, not only contrasts. BSP, GSP, solar, and wind are reported per type and never averaged into Primary.
    • Intervals: month-block bootstrap, whole validation months and the three seeds resampled, with studies.bootstrap.bootstrap_difference_at_level and n_resamples raised for the Bonferroni level. Intervals resample months, not series, so with three wind farms they understate between-farm uncertainty.

    Checks and compute

    • Leakage checks. Every refit stops at its stated training end; lags and the correction use observations at least 3.5 hours before the origin; ERA5 and CAMS values are those of the valid time only and are used in the perfect-weather arms alone; the thresholds are fixed scoring constants from each series' history to 2026-06-30, which no model sees. A reviewer checks the monthly-retraining code for leakage.
    • Reproduction checks. The reused demand arms must reproduce 00019's stored scores to 3 decimal places on 00019's exact case set when rescored with this experiment's scorer; each family is then scored on its own case intersection and the page reports the dropped counts, and the refitted N1 must match 00013's n1 within 0.02 points.
    • Weather source at lag times. No arm uses weather at lag times; only the power lags read the past, and they read observed power available at the forecast origin.
    • Compute. About 3 hours in total on slots A and B (22 cores, one fit at a time on the GPU): generator N1 (9 series x 3 seeds, minutes); demand fits (N1 refit, 20 series x 3 seeds, about 20 minutes, and the ERA5-retrained variant, about 20 minutes); monthly retraining (about 1 hour, seed 0, plus the feature build); generator perfect-weather predictions (minutes). If the fits would run more than 5 hours in total, the plan proposes a reduction to the manager instead of cutting arms silently.

    Sizing

    • Stored data: prediction sets under data/ml-experiments/00033-.../predictions/; no table.
    • Production serving path: not touched; the production arm is read from stored forecasts.
    • Degradation rule: not touched.
    • Defensible designs: more than one could compare the architectures (for example the choice of thresholds, the tail of the exceedance probability, and the reused-versus-refitted arms), so the plan is reviewed.
    • Callers: the scripts copy the helpers of experiments 13, 19, 20, and 22; the span of code is wide, so the size is complex.
