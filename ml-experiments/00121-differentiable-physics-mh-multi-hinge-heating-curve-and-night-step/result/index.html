    Differentiable physics, MH: multi-hinge heating curve and night step

    Adding night-time levels indexed on the UTC clock removes most of the 01:00 jump in demand that the current forecast misses on cold winter nights, and a five-hinge heating curve adds almost nothing. We asked two questions of GAM-K, the fitted model of primary substation demand (a primary substation is a substation of the electricity distribution network that supplies local feeders) that the differentiable-physics series of experiments uses. First, does letting its heating response bend at five temperatures instead of one lower the forecast error? Second, does a night-time step term, such as the load that Economy 7 meters and storage heaters switch on at about 01:00, lower the error on winter nights? (Economy 7 is a UK off-peak electricity tariff.) We fitted each change at five substations, forecast each one day ahead for the 12 months from July 2025, and scored it on the same rows as the unchanged model, which the page calls B0-K. We tested the fitting code on synthetic substations first. The five-hinge curve lowered the error by 0.016 points against B0-K, a tie. The UTC-indexed night term lowered the error on winter nights by 0.23 points, and the gain held in the previous winter (2024-25). The synthetic tests could not show that a step this small can be detected, so every night label stays not powered and the verdict is inconclusive.

    The quantile score is twice the mean pinball loss over 11 quantile levels, so lower is better. It is measured in points, which are percent of a substation's effective capacity and not MW, after the recent-error filter (a Kalman filter that corrects each forecast with the latest metered error) unless stated. All forecasts use observed ERA5 temperature and CAMS irradiance instead of weather forecasts, so each number is a ceiling for a live forecast. One point is 0.054 to 0.117 MW depending on the substation (effective capacity 5.72 MW at D5, 5.40 at D10, 10.78 at D11, 11.71 at D14, and 9.12 at D16).

    Code Meaning

    MH B0 plus heating hinges at 10, 12, 14, 16, and 18 C (MH-5 replaces B0's; MH-sh shrinks them)

    Take-home message and verdict

    • Night terms lower the winter-night score by 0.233 points. NS-U-K (free night levels on the UTC clock) minus B0-K is −0.233 points on winter nights (99.375% interval −0.276 to −0.180, drawn from only 5 resampled init-time months, so lean on the site and winter consistency), better at 5 of 5 substations, with −0.153 (95% interval −0.183 to −0.121) in the replicate winter 2024-25. The forecast of the 01:00 step on cold winter nights rises from 15 to 45% of the observed step to within 0.2 to 3.1 points of it (figure 2, table below).
    • The five-hinge heating curve ties with the single hinge. MH-K minus B0-K is −0.0155 (97.5% interval −0.035 to +0.004), −0.022 in the replicate year, better at 5 of 5 substations in both years. The label is tie. The gain sits in cold snaps (−0.140 in the scored winter, −0.077 in the replicate winter) and not in winter evening peaks (+0.005).
    • The verdict is inconclusive. The MH label is tie. Every night label is not powered because all three synthetic detection gates failed: a 4-point step is detected in 14%, 14%, and 8% of draws (80% needed, figure 5). The estimates for NS-U-K and MH-K above are results on real data and are reported in full; the pre-registered rule does not let them earn a label.
    • Production status is candidate, not adopted. NS-U and NS-HW are the arms worth an ENS confirmation plan with monthly refits, after an independent audit by a reviewing agent (triggers 1, 2, 4, 5, and 7 below).

    Synthetic tests run before any scored fit

    • Test 1 passed at all four cuts, and B0-K reproduces experiment 114's stored scored-year B0-K exactly (difference 0).
    • Gate 2 passed. On 100 synthetic substations, MH-K minus B0-K is −0.031 (80% interval −0.036 to −0.027) under a five-hinge truth, recovering 83% of the 0.037-point oracle gain, and +0.003 under a single-hinge truth (limit +0.01). The curve-coverage check returned 66%.
    • Gate 4 as written failed, as predicted: NS-L is collinear with B0's profile (added effective degrees of freedom 0.0), and the winter-night criterion cannot be met because winter local time equals UTC.
    • Gate 4' as first written failed (NS-U beat B0 on BST nights in 74% of draws against 80% needed; NS-HW beat B0 on winter nights with a cold step in 72% against 90%). It was redefined after seeing that failure.
    • Gate 4'' (redefined with a stated effect size and power target) failed. At 5% false positives (1 to 4% verified), a 4-point step (0.2 to 0.5 MW) is detected on the label's night rows by NS-U in 14%, NS-HW in 14%, and NS-H in 8% of draws; a 7-point step in 59%, 57%, and 10%. On the step's own half-hour: 58%, 65%, and 16% for 4 points. NS-U does not detect a local-clock step (5%). Because gate 4'' failed, N2, N3, N4, and the indexing contrast are not powered, and the plan fixes the verdict at inconclusive.

    Night terms in detail

    The clock that indexes the night step is not identified. NS-U-K minus NS-L-K on BST nights is −0.029 (95% interval −0.069 to +0.013) in the scored year and −0.077 (−0.107 to −0.040) in the replicate year, better at 5 of 5 substations in both. The indexing label is indexing not identified because gate 4'' failed. Cold BST nights (daily mean below 8 C) number 5 to 7 per substation in the replicate year and 5 to 11 in the scored year, so the clock contrast is cannot tell per substation, except at D10 and D11 in the scored year (11 nights each). As descriptive evidence only, on BST weekday nights the observed step sits at 01:30 local (00:30 UTC) at D10, D16, and D5 (about 1 point), with no step at 00:30 local. The winter gain cannot separate the clocks, because in winter local time equals UTC. Trigger 2 below gives the likely source of the winter gain.

    An evolving night step (NS-UT) is worse than a fixed one. NS-UT-K minus NS-U-K is +0.286 (95% interval +0.264 to +0.302) on winter nights, and NS-UT-K is 0.084 points worse than B0-K on all rows although NS-UT before the filter is 0.098 points better than B0. Its fitted winter level (points, MW in the stored file ns_ut_step_by_winter.json) falls from 5 to 1.4 points at D5 between winters 2021-22 and 2022-23 and rises to 4.7 in 2024-25. D14 is 4.4, 0.2, 2.9, and 0.1. These are descriptive: a fit at each cut sees only the 24 months before it, and winter 2025-26 is outside every fit.

    NS-H, one cold-hinge window from 00:00 to 07:00 UTC, adds nothing (+0.0005). NS-HW, which lets the cold hinge differ by half-hour, gains −0.137. NS-L is a null control: its scores equal B0's to 1e-4 points. The filter-fit control for the night arms agrees: NS-U-K with B0's filter minus B0-K on winter nights is −0.224, against −0.233.

    Consistency checks

    • The median forecast equals the mean forecast. The median minus the mean is 0.0000 points at every substation for B0-K, MH-K, NS-U-K, and NS-HW-K, so the quantile spread does not shift the median forecast.
    • Error of the mean forecast beside each score contrast: C1 −0.031 (score −0.0155), N2 −0.465 (−0.233), N4 −0.280 (−0.137); the signs agree.
    • No arm moves a tail more than 1 point from nominal. After the filter the q95 coverage is 0.941 to 0.942 and the q99 coverage 0.983 for B0-K, MH-K, NS-U-K, and NS-HW-K.
    • Four audit triggers fired. (1) The positive controls failed: gates 4, 4', and 4'' and the pooled-curve coverage check (66% against 70 to 90%). (2) The plan predicted NS-U-K and NS-L-K within 0.01 on winter nights, and they differ by 0.233. The explanation, which is post hoc: B0's profile is one year-round level per local half-hour and day type, so it cannot hold a step that is large in winter and small in summer. NS-U can, so the winter gain may come from the difference between the GMT and BST seasons and not from the UTC clock. (4) Leaving D10 out changes C1 from tie to small gain. (5, applied by analogy) NS-UT-K scores worse than B0-K, which it nests (the explanation, the held last level meeting the filter, is untested); (7, by judgement, since no label is gain) the night term is meant to enter GAM-K. An independent audit by a reviewing agent is planned before the night result is used. No trigger fired for the others: test 1, gate 2(b), and B0-K's reproduction of experiment 114 passed, and C1's interval width (±0.019) sits inside the sister contrast's ±0.023.

    Predictions against outcome

    The heating predictions held and the night predictions held only in sign. C1 fell in the predicted range (−0.016 against −0.03 to +0.01) with the predicted label, MH-K against H-site-K and T-all-K and MH-5-K matched, and gate 2(b) matched. The sign of the night gain was predicted, but its size was not: at D10 it is −0.36 (predicted −0.10 to −0.30), and at D11 (−0.09) and D14 (−0.22) it is far above the predicted "under 0.03". NS-U-K and NS-L-K were predicted within 0.01 on winter nights and differ by 0.233. The tuning-year NS-U-K gain of 0.043 was not expected to carry over and became 0.233 on winter nights. Three other predictions failed: the unfiltered MH gain was not larger than the filtered one (+0.0001 against −0.0155), gate 2 recovered 83% of the oracle gain (predicted 50 to 70%), and the MH gain did not sit in November to March (−0.0155, against a predicted more than 0.02), although cold snaps carry −0.140.

    Caveats

    • Every number is a past-weather ceiling (ERA5 temperature and CAMS irradiance), one scored year with frozen fits, hindsight masks, and one replicate winter.
    • The night step was identified on scored-year rows (experiment 111's stored residuals), so the scored winter is not a clean test for the night arms. The replicate winter confirms the sign and roughly two thirds of the size.
    • The scored year has served experiments 106, 111, 114, and this one, and MH was chosen after seeing experiment 114's scored-year contrasts.
    • Leaks, none reaching a scored row: the 2022-07-01 replica reads the 2023-08-07 capacity vintage 13 months late; the 2023-07-01 cut has the 5-week vintage leak; D9's 2024-07-01 fit reads the 2025-04-14 vintage; the replicate-year filter spread is in sample for the filter hyperparameters.
    • Gate 4'' replaces gate 4' as first written, which is kept above. The redefinition came after the first failure and before any scored fit.
    • Provisional substation cards (fitted to past weather on a screening run) are in the same folder as card_D5.svg, card_D10.svg, card_D11.svg, card_D14.svg, and card_D16.svg. D9 is short-history and has no card.
    • The experiment 114 merge commit (503ce3a3de408d1f0c2bdbada5ba88032ff238bb) is recorded in the hashed decisions.json.

    Appendix: the plan as committed before the fits, with its clarifications

    The question, and what earlier experiments already say

    B0's single hinge already makes the heating response non-negative and convex, so MH asks only whether more bends help. B0 has 16 heating columns: one relu(15.5 - T) hinge for each of 4 smoothings of ERA5 temperature in each of 4 hour bands, with non-negative slopes. Five hinges per column let the slope per degree grow in steps as it gets colder (zero above 18 C, and growing at each of 18, 16, 14, 12, and 10 C). This is a free, monotone, convex temperature curve of the kind the literature fits, in place of one fixed threshold.

    Experiment 114 gives two reasons to expect a small gain and one to expect none.

    • Its loss profile over a single hinge preferred a threshold at or below 12 to 14 C at 4 of 5 substations, yet the per-site threshold lowered the filtered score by only −0.012 points (97.5% interval −0.030 to +0.007). One hinge cannot represent a flat region and a steep region together.
    • Splitting each hinge by half-band gained −0.039 (−0.070 to −0.014). MH tests a different missing piece, the shape across temperature, and is not expected to gain as much.
    • Against: experiment 113 found a band heating slope weak at real noise (80% interval about 38% of the slope wide). Five hinges split each slope five ways on the same mild-weather days.

    Every contrast is a past-weather ceiling (ERA5 temperature of the target half-hour); only the ENS confirmation in "Handed on" tests transfer.

    Base model, years, sites, and rows

    All as in briefs/dp_n3n4.md, so that experiment 114's stored predictions remain comparable.

    • B0 and B0-K: dp_gam with drift="none", capacity="pinned" (studies.dp_pinned_capacity), ridge 0.1, TrainingWindow(months=24), lockdown out. B0-K adds dp_assimilation.run_kalman under the 3.5-hour cut with four filter hyperparameters per site, fitted on each arm's tuning-year residuals by fit_filter_hyperparameters with Part 4's prior.
    • Years: tuning 2023-07-01 to 2024-06-30 (cut 2023-07-01), replicate 2024-07-01 to 2025-06-30, scored 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30. Screening tier: one frozen fit per arm and cut, no refits, one seed. Each cut's spread comes from the replica fit 12 months earlier (dp_gam.replica_residuals), so fits run at 2022-07-01, 2023-07-01, 2024-07-01, and 2025-07-01. Nothing scores a valid time on or after 2026-07-01.
    • Sites: D5, D10, D11, D14, and D16 decide (4 of 5 needed to improve); D9 descriptive in the scored year only. MVA sites, D15, and D18 are not fitted.
    • Masks, leads, predictions: as experiment 114 (hand-filtered switching list padded 24 hours, 09:00 UTC origins, day D+1, the same prediction columns).

    Arms

    Night-step arms (NS)

    Why: a read-only check of experiment 111's stored GAM-K predictions (one winter, November 2025 to February 2026, and one summer pair, July to August 2025 and May to June 2026, weekdays only) found an unmodelled step at 01:00 (the 00:30 to 01:00 half-hour) in winter. Actual demand steps up by 7.1, 10.7, and 7.4 points at D5, D10, and D16 (1.9 to 3.4 points at D11, D14, and D9), and GAM-K's residual step is 4.4, 7.3, and 5.4 points at D5, D10, and D16. In summer the step shrinks to 1.7 points or less, and its position (UTC or local clock) cannot be told apart. The check used one winter and one summer pair, so the summer reading is weak. Winter local time equals UTC, so winter rows cannot separate the two indexings; only British Summer Time (BST) rows can.

    Four arms (NS-HW was added before any replicate or scored fit; see the clarifications), each B0 plus extra columns at B0's ridge 0.1, so each nests B0; each runs filtered (-K) and unfiltered.

    Arm Extra columns Sign
    NS-L one level per half-hour of the 20 half-hours from 22:00 to 08:00 local time free
    NS-U the same, indexed on the UTC clock (22:00 to 08:00 UTC) free
    NS-H B0's 15.5 C hinge (4 smoothings) times an indicator for 00:00 to 07:00 UTC non-negative

    Known-answer test and gate

    Order: test 1, then tuning (the MH-sh multiplier and the filter hyperparameters, hashed with the 114 SHA into decisions.json), then the gate, all before any MH row of the replicate or scored year is fitted or read. The B0-K reproduction in test 1 reads stored B0 output only. A failed test 1 stops the scored reading. A failed gate does not stop it (the data cost is zero), but the verdict is then fixed so that the MH label is not powered and C1 is printed with no label. The gate can fail by design.

    1. Reproduction (blocking). MH with only the 15.5 C hinge reproduces dp_gam.fit_gam for B0 to 1e-8 relative in the parameters; MH with the five added blocks forced to zero reproduces B0's fitted values to 1e-5 points; B0-K's filter on stored B0 residuals reproduces run_kalman called directly; B0-K reproduces experiment 114's stored B0-K scored-year score to 1e-4; no training row after its cut.
    2. MH power check (synthetic, run through the filter, can fail). The truth is B0's real fit at the 2024-07-01 cut with its heating block replaced by a five-hinge curve: B0's slopes spread across the hinges, the cold-end share scaled by a common amplitude per draw.
      • Noise: experiment 113's real-noise rule (e00114_noise.py, copied), whole donor months from the other decision sites' B0 residuals. Donor residuals are in-sample, so they are inflated by the ratio of B0's out-of-sample to in-sample RMSE on the tuning year, per site. Only covariates (temperature, calendar) of the 2024-25 year enter the held-out synthetic year, and no 2024-25 residual does. Power is still an upper bound.
      • Sizing, as a formula: oracle gain = score of B0-K (fitted to the draw) minus score of the true-parameter forecast with B0-K's filter, on the held-out synthetic year, minus the same difference under a single-hinge truth at 15.5 C. The amplitude is set on 5 pilot draws so that the oracle gain is 0.03 points after the filter, the decision margin. The unfiltered oracle gain is printed beside it.
      • Pass if (a) the paired mean of MH-K minus B0-K over the 100 draws (20 per site, B0-K's filter hyperparameters) is at most −0.015, half the oracle gain, with its 80% interval over draws wholly below zero; and (b) on a single-hinge truth at 15.5 C, the paired mean of MH-K minus B0-K over 100 draws is no worse than +0.01. Per-site counts and the share of the oracle gain recovered are descriptive.
      • Failure: not powered; see "Decision rule".

    3. Pooled-curve coverage (descriptive, in the same draws). The 80% month-block bootstrap interval (I1 of experiment 113: calendar quarters kept, widened by sqrt(m/(m-1))) for one pooled summary: the fitted heating response at 5 C divided by the response at 12 C, summed over the four bands and four smoothings. Coverage of the truth over the 100 draws is printed. If coverage lies outside 70 to 90%, no curve summary appears on the page. A pass says the interval is calibrated on synthetic data only, not on real data. The summary is also dropped if any decision site has fewer than 500 half-hours below 5 C in any fit window (the counts below 5 C and below 10 C are printed per site and cut). It carries no label.

    4. Night-step indexing gate (synthetic, unfiltered, can fail). Same generator and noise as step

    5. The truth is B0's real fit plus an injected 4-point step at 01:00 UTC, indexed in UTC in one set of 100 draws and in local time in another (the two differ only in BST rows). Pass if (a) on each truth, the matching arm beats the other indexing on BST nights in at least 80% of draws, (b) on winter nights both arms beat B0 on both truths in at least 90% of draws, and (c) on each truth the mismatched arm does not recover the injected level on BST nights (its fitted level at the injected half-hour is under half the injection). Failure: indexing not identified; the NS-U against NS-L contrast is printed unlabelled.

    • NS-L is redundant by construction. It models a step that grows with cold, which is how storage heating (Economy 7) behaves. It was added after the tuning-year run showed NS-L and NS-U cannot represent a winter-only step. The night family is now four contrasts, so the Bonferroni level is 99.375%.
    • The MH-sh multiplier grid is extended to {10, 100, 1000, 10000, 100000}. The first tuning run chose 1000, the edge of the grid {10, 100, 1000}. The extension is made on the tuning year alone.
    • Gate 4 is run as written, and its result is recorded as it stands. Winter local time equals UTC, and a winter step is already free in B0's profile, so criterion (b) (both NS-L and NS-U beat B0 on winter nights) fails by construction.     • Gate 4' replaces gate 4 as the gate for the indexing contrast and for N4. On the same generator and noise as gate 4: (a) with a flat 4-point step injected at 01:00 UTC, NS-U beats B0 on British Summer Time nights in at least 80% of draws; with the step injected at 01:00 local time, B0 beats NS-U on those nights in at least 80% of draws (B0's profile already holds a local step). (b) with a step that grows with cold injected on winter nights (4 points on average over the winter nights, proportional to the 15.5 C hinge of the first smoothed temperature), NS-HW beats B0 on winter nights in at least 90% of draws, and the mean of NS-HW minus B0 is wholly below zero. A failure of 4'(a) makes the indexing contrast unlabelled (indexing not identified); a failure of 4'(b) makes the N4 label not powered. NS-L is not part of 4'.
    • Winter breakdown and cold snaps (maintainer priority). Every contrast is also reported on November to March rows, on a cold-snap subset, and on evening peaks within them, with paired intervals against BG2 and lagless XGBoost where their stored predictions cover the same rows. A cold snap is the days of the scored winter whose daily mean ERA5 temperature at the site's cell is in the coldest 10% of days of that winter; the rule and the day count are stated, and "too few days" is reported where fewer than 5 days qualify. A winter loss alongside an annual gain is stated in the verdict as a reason to hold the arm back. The page states how many independent winters the evidence holds. The substation cards gain a cold-snap panel and the fitted night step (as a score change, not a level).

The choice of the 01:00 half-hour, of D5, D10, and D16 as the large cases, and of the "3 of 5 sites" rule came from scored rows, so the scored winter is not a clean test for the night arms. Without it the label is not replicated. The evidence is two winters (replicate and scored), plus the tuning winter (2023-24) for descriptive checks.
    • Which gate covers which label. A label whose gate fails is not powered, and the verdict treats not powered as not gain. Gate 2 covers C1 (the MH label). Gate 4'(a) covers N2 (NS-U-K) and the indexing contrast. Gate 4'(b) covers N4 (NS-HW-K), and the same test run for NS-H on the cold truth (NS-H-K beats B0 on winter nights in at least 90% of draws) covers N3. If gate 4'' fails, the labels it covers stay not powered, the verdict stays inconclusive, and the gate is not redefined again.
      • Injected steps. A step of 4 points and a step of 7 points of effective capacity (the sizes seen at D5, D10, and D16; the units bullet below converts to MW) in the half-hour starting 00:30. The night-row noise has a standard deviation of 4.6 to 7.7 points at D5 and D10, so 4 points is about one noise standard deviation. Three kinds: a flat step on the UTC clock all year (utc), a flat step on the local clock all year (local), and a cold-dependent step on the UTC clock (cold, equal to a constant times the 15.5 C hinge of the first smoothed temperature, scaled so the mean over the winter rows of that half-hour equals the stated size). A placebo has no step.
      • Units. The quantile score and every step are in points, that is, percent of the substation's effective capacity, and not in MW. A step of p points is p times the effective capacity divided by 100 in MW. The effective capacity is a single table value per substation (5.72 MW at D5, 5.40 MW at D10, 10.78 MW at D11, 11.71 MW at D14, and 9.12 MW at D16), so the MW conversion of a step carries the interval of the step in points and no additional interval. A 4-point step is 0.23, 0.22, 0.43, 0.47, and 0.36 MW at D5, D10, D11, D14, and D16, and a 7-point step is 0.40, 0.38, 0.75, 0.82, and 0.64 MW. The measured residual steps of 4.4, 7.3, and 5.4 points at D5, D10, and D16 are 0.25, 0.39, and 0.49 MW.
      • Statistic. For an arm, the mean quantile score minus B0's on the label's own rows (BST nights for NS-U, winter nights for NS-H and NS-HW), unfiltered, on the same noise.
      • Critical value. The 5th percentile of the statistic over 100 placebo draws (20 per substation, draws 0 to 19). An arm detects a step in a draw when its statistic is below the critical value, so the false-positive rate on the placebo is 5% by construction. It is checked on 100 further placebo draws (draws 100 to 119), where at most 8% may detect (the target is 5%; 100 draws carry a Monte Carlo error of about 2 points).
      • Pass. (a) NS-U detects the 4-point utc step in at least 80% of draws, and detects the 4-point local step in at most 10% of draws (so the clock is identified); (b) NS-HW detects the 4-point cold step in at least 80% of draws; (c) NS-H detects the 4-point cold step in at least 80% of draws. (a) covers N2 and the indexing contrast, (b) covers N4, and (c) covers N3.
      • Descriptive. The same statistics for 7 points, and on the rows of the injected half-hour only, per substation. These cannot rescue a label.

Gate 4'(a) scores all 22:00-to-08:00 British Summer Time (BST) night rows. Gate 4'(b) scores all winter-night rows. The cold step in gate 4' is a step in the half-hour starting 00:30 UTC whose mean over the winter rows of that half-hour is 4 points. Because a step in one of 20 night half-hours is diluted in these scores, the descriptive 01:00 gate repeats 4' on the rows of the injected half-hour only. It is labelled descriptive and cannot rescue a label.

Night terms change few rows, so the contrast on all other rows should be much tighter than that reference, and the report prints its width. A pass shows only that the upper bound is under +0.02. It does not show equivalence. The winter-night subset has fewer rows than the sister contrast, so its interval is wider, and the 0.05 margin is judged against the printed width.
    • One winter definition for labels. Labels use winter nights (November to February, 22:00 to 08:00 local). The breakdown tables also show November to March, and "summer" in the winter-against-summer contrast means June to August. A winter loss beside an annual gain is stated in the verdict as a reason to hold the arm back from production; it does not change the label. Intervals against BG2 and lagless XGBoost are 95% and exploratory, with no multiplicity correction. The "daily mean" in the cold-night rules is the local calendar day.

    • NS-UT, an evolving UTC night step, is added as an exploratory arm (maintainer steer, before any replicate or scored fit). The size of the Economy 7 effect may change from winter to winter at each substation (experiment 121's tuning year does not show this; the E7 step-by-winter table of PR 1416 suggests it). NS-UT is B0 plus one non-negative level per month of the 24-month training window (25 monthly knots, linear between them) times an indicator for the UTC half-hours starting 00:30 to 07:00, a second-difference penalty of weight 1000 between neighbouring knots (fixed here, not tuned) on top of B0's ridge, and the last knot's level held through the forecast year. The 0.03 margin is therefore not a porting-cost threshold; it justifies an ENS confirmation run, which needs its own plan.     Decision rule

    • Label for C1 (gate passed and MH-K-b0 agrees): gain if the estimate is at most −0.03 points, the 97.5% interval lies wholly below zero, the replicate year's estimate is below zero, and at least 4 of 5 sites improve in the scored year. small gain if the interval lies wholly below zero, the estimate is between −0.03 and zero, and the replicate year's estimate is below zero. not replicated if the scored interval lies wholly below zero but the replicate sign (or, for gain, the site count) fails. worse if the interval lies wholly above zero; tie otherwise. The replicate condition is required because the MH-sh multiplier and the filter's hyperparameters are chosen on the tuning year. The replicate estimate is printed beside every scored estimate in the headline chart and tables.
    • Label for N2 to N4 (each gated as the clarifications say; N1 is a null control): gain if the winter-night estimate is at most −0.05 points, the 99.375% interval lies wholly below zero, the replicate year's winter-night estimate is below zero with its 95% interval wholly below zero or at most half the scored estimate, at least 3 of 5 sites improve (the step is large at three substations), and non-inferiority holds. small gain if the interval lies wholly below zero, the estimate is between −0.05 and zero, and the replicate is below zero. not replicated and worse (interval wholly above zero) as for C1; tie otherwise. Failure of non-inferiority gives worse on other rows and no gain. The NS label is the best label among N2 to N4. The indexing contrast is the only statement about which clock the step follows.
    • Gate failure or control disagreement (MH): label not powered (gate) or inconclusive (filter control); the scored C1 is still computed and printed with no label, and the MH label is not gain. Take-home then says whether the printed estimate reached the margin, as information only.
    • Verdict: supported if both the MH label and the NS label are gain; partly supported if one is (a not powered label never counts as gain); small gain if neither and one is small gain; refuted if none of those and a decision contrast is worse (this refutes only these five hinge positions with this ridge, or these night terms; the weakly identified fits could have overfitted, and the take-home says so); inconclusive otherwise, including not powered, not replicated, tie, and a blocking test that cannot be fixed. A failed MH gate leaves the MH label not powered, which counts as not passing.
    • Handed on: a gain becomes a candidate for GAM-K's base only after an ENS confirmation with Part 6's monthly refits, which needs its own plan and approval. A tie or not powered keeps the single 15.5 C hinge. A night-step gain also needs an ENS confirmation, since the step is in the observed demand and may be shaped by weather. Combining MH with T2 or the night terms, per-site positions, and a knot-count search are not in this plan.

    1. A control fails: test 1, gate 2(b), or B0-K missing 114's stored B0-K by more than 1e-4.
    2. Contradiction: C1 gains more than 0.10 points; the pooled-curve coverage lies within 70 to 90% although experiment 113 found slopes weak; B0-K misses 114's B0-K.
    3. Score and mean-forecast error disagree in sign for C1.
    4. Leaving one site out flips the sign of C1 or changes its label.
    5. A nested arm wins, or a null control moves: NS-L's unfiltered fitted values differ from B0's by more than 1e-5 points, or NS-L-K scores differently from B0-K by more than its own paired half-width; B0 beats MH on the tuning year by more than MH's paired half-width; the single-hinge reproduction moves the score at all; MH-K-b0 moves C1.
    6. Interval width: C1's 97.5% interval more than twice or under half the width of Part 4's C4 at the same level (about ±0.023).
    7. A later design depends on it: a gain would change GAM-K's base, on one year plus a replicate at the screening tier.
