    Does capping D5's solar output at 60% still help when the cap is chosen using only earlier data?

    Choosing the 60% cap at D5 from earlier data alone keeps almost all of its gain, and none of the other five Primaries shows the same pattern clearly. A Primary substation is a substation that feeds homes and businesses; D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16 are anonymised labels for six of them. Experiment 180 found that the D5 forecast has lower error on sunny half-hours when the solar output the forecast subtracts from the substation's power is capped at 60% of the solar capacity held for D5. That cap mimics an export limit on the solar generators behind D5. Experiment 180 chose 60% on some months and scored it on a later year, so the choice never used only earlier data.

    We asked two questions. First, does the gain remain if the cap is chosen at each monthly retraining from the previous 12 months of forecasts, rather than using the scored year? Second, do the other five Primaries show the same levelling-off of net power as the sun rises? Here "as-of" means chosen using only data from before the retraining. The gain is the change in mean absolute error of the median forecast, in megawatts (MW), against a control that has no cap; "sunny" means the sunniest third of a substation's daylight half-hours. The as-of cap kept 97% of the gain (-0.129 MW against -0.133 MW). Only D5 showed the levelling-off. D9 improved by 0.032 MW and D11 worsened by 0.017 MW, and the intervals of both include zero.

    Figure 2 shows the change in error at each fixed ceiling, at D5 and D9.

    Figure 3 shows, per Primary and summer, the solar output implied by net power against the fleet solar shape, a south-facing panel's expected output scaled by the sun's height and the irradiance. Only D5 falls towards the sunniest half-hours.

    Verdict partly supported. The D5 prediction we wrote down before fitting holds. The as-of cap lay in 50% to 70% at 12 of 12 refits (60% at 11, 50% at one), and the as-of arm keeps 0.129 MW of the 0.133 MW gain against a required 0.08 MW. The D5 interval is wholly below zero, and D10 and D16, which we expected to be unaffected, moved by under 0.02 MW. D9's estimated gain is 0.032 MW, which meets the 0.03 MW threshold. Its interval includes zero, and supported required the interval to lie wholly below zero. The decision rule did not cover that case, so we labelled it partly supported. D11's estimate (+0.017 MW) exceeds the 0.01 MW we predicted for the other Primaries, but its interval spans zero.

    Results

    Rows: the 292 forecast runs of the scored year (2025-07 to 2026-06) used in experiment 178, leaving out half-hours affected by switching events, leads 0.5 to 168 hours. The forecasts are the monthly retrainings of the generalised additive model (GAM) of experiment 180, so only the cap differs between arms and the contrast does not depend on the retraining schedule. D5 has 14,045 sunny half-hours and D9 has 15,415. BG2 is the best-guess forecast that experiment 180 compared against.

    • Screen (figure 3): we ranked each Primary's clear May to August half-hours by the fleet solar shape and compared the implied solar output in the top two tenths with that in the fourth to sixth tenths. A ratio well below 1 means solar output levels off as the sun rises. The ratio is 0.77, 0.68, and 0.73 at D5 in the summers of 2022, 2023, and 2024, and 0.98 to 3.74 at D10, D11, D14, and D16. D9 has 0 and 28 clear half-hours in 2022 and 2023, and its 2024 ratio is undefined because the middle tenths are negative, so D9 fails the screen.
    • Replicate months: letting the as-of window grow from 3 to 11 months picks 60% at every refit again (gains of -0.124 MW at D5 and -0.031 MW at D9). Experiment 180 used these months to choose 60%, so this repeats the choice and is not independent evidence.
    • Ceiling sweep (figure 2): at D5 the gain peaks at 50% to 60% (-0.126 and -0.133 MW) and is -0.007 MW at 90%. At D9 the shape is the same at about one third of the size.
    • Caveats: D9 has about six months of data before 2024-07, so its as-of sunny threshold (top third of the daylight shape before 2024-07) differs from the scored year's threshold. The as-of rule selects on the quantity scored.

    Checks

    • Reproduction: the new fixed-60% forecasts at D5 are identical to experiment 180's (largest difference 0 in the median forecast and in the 99% quantile forecast; e00214_checks.py).
    • Median against mean forecast: stage 2 of the forecast chain sets the mean forecast equal to the median, so the difference is 0 by construction and the check cannot show whether the spread of the forecast shifts it. The error contrast of the mean forecast is therefore the same as the median's, and this experiment cannot test whether the two point in different directions.
    • Audit conditions: none was met. The reproduction passed, D10 and D16 stayed inside 0.02 MW, the as-of arm does not beat the fixed-60% arm that it contains, and the D5 interval has about the same width as experiment 180's. Leaving out D11 moves the pooled estimate for the other four Primaries from +0.004 MW to -0.000 MW, and leaving out any other Primary leaves it between +0.004 and +0.007 MW (e00214_checks.py). The pooled interval includes zero in all five cases.

    What to try next

    • The as-of rule adds one ceiling-selection step to each monthly retraining, and it is ready to consider for the next model release.
    • Repeat the screen and the contrast at D9 once it has at least two summers of data before the scored year.
    • The planned combination check can use the as-of ceiling rule in place of the fixed 60% ceiling.
