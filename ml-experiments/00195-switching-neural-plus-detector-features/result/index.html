    Experiment 195: a learned score on detector features finds 24 of 35 real transfers, not 19

    On 35 real transfers lasting over 1 day, a small learned score found 22 to 24, against 19 for RO-NC, the rule-based switching detector with the best score of those we tested, at the same 80 unexplained alarms. A transfer is a period, labelled by hand in experiment 41, in which switching moved load from one Primary substation (a substation that steps transmission voltage down to distribution voltage) to its neighbours. An alarm is unexplained when no logged closure or labelled transfer lies within 24 hours of it, and the 80 are counted over odd-numbered months. RO-NC is the refit-offset neighbour-count detector of experiment 82: it raises a candidate alarm wherever the 24-hour step in a Primary substation's forecast error reaches 2.5 noise units, and it keeps the candidates that a logistic score ranks highest. Our re-run finds 19 transfers where its original run found 18, probably because our end rule differs in one detail from experiment 130's.

    A learned score re-ranks RO-NC's candidates, and a logistic regression does nearly as well. Each score reads 25 numbers per candidate, among them the 24-hour step, the opposite step at a neighbouring Primary substation, and the hour and season. We trained each score only on synthetic transfers, added to the forecast error with the injector of experiment 189 (pull request 1494), plus real stretches with no logged or labelled event. We scored each on real transfers of Primary pairs it had never seen. The best score, a neural network that also reads 4 days of the raw forecast error, found 24 and lost none of RO-NC's (+0.074 in the share of moved MW found, 95% interval +0.013 to +0.175, resampling whole Primary pairs).

    The verdict is partly supported, because the re-ranker clears the recall bar only with a feature that reads 2 days after the step. RO-NC's score reads no such feature. Without it, the re-ranker finds 22 and its interval reaches below zero (-0.002), so only the count bar holds. The logistic regression finds 22 with or without that feature. The extra transfers are small. The learned scores find no more injected transfers than RO-NC does. The gated-mask bar was not run.

    Results

    • Arms. F: features only, as a logistic regression, a gradient-boosted tree, and a neural network of 2,449 weights ("F network"). R: re-ranker of RO-NC's candidates, a neural network of 10,249 weights that reads the features and a raw window of 4 days. C: logistic stack of the F network, RO-NC's score, and the pair statistic (the smaller of the 6-hour step and the opposite step at the best neighbour), fitted on validation worlds only. U and I: union and intersection of a learned arm with RO-NC or the pair alarm, with the split of the alarm count chosen on validation worlds. Comparators: RO-NC, the pair alarm of experiment 156, and their rule-based union.
    • Rows. The 35 real transfers over 1 day, out of 66 labelled transfers, in 18 Primary pairs divided into 3 folds that hold out whole pairs. Alarms are chosen at 80 unexplained odd-month alarms per fold (0.185 per Primary-month, equal for every arm by construction). A transfer is found when an alarm starts within 2 hours of its start and ends within the larger of 6 hours and 10% of its duration of its end (experiment 130's rule). Every arm uses RO-NC's end rule.

    • Pre-registered bars, with every feature. Primary bar: the re-ranker, and the union of the re-ranker with the pair alarm, have a lower interval end above zero and find more than the rule-based union (24 against 18). Count bar: 24 is at least 22, and 5 transfers are gained with none lost. The logistic regression and the F network agree within 2 transfers (22 and 22), as predicted. The other learned arms' lower interval ends run from -0.023 to -0.002, so they do not meet the primary bar. The prediction that recall falls by under 15 percentage points when the receiver is unmetered is met: 0.215 against 0.290 for the re-ranker on injected transfers. The gated-mask bar was not run.
    • The extra transfers are small. The re-ranker's five gains are 0.4 to 0.9 MW each (3.2 of the 42.7 MW in the 35 transfers) and sit in 4 of the 18 Primary pairs, 48 to 93 hours long. The three pairs that carry most of the moved MW keep their RO-NC counts. Dropping any one pair leaves the re-ranker 3 to 5 transfers ahead of RO-NC, and the F network 2 to 3.
    • Transfers that both forecast-error models can see (29 of the 35). Re-ranker 20, RO-NC 15, F network 18. The two models are the convex model and the XGBoost model of experiment 181.
    • The candidate ceiling is 26. RO-NC's candidate list contains an alarm within 2 hours of the start of 29 transfers, and a candidate with the right start and end for 26. The pair-alarm candidates add none. The re-ranker's 24 is 2 below that ceiling.
    • Start error. The median start error of found transfers is 0.5 hours for every arm.
    • Alarm accounting (per fold, at 80 unexplained odd-month alarms). Alarms that look like a balanced transfer (an opposite step at a neighbour of 0.4 to 2.5 times the own step) number 10 for RO-NC, 13.3 for the F network, and 14.7 for the re-ranker. Those extra balanced alarms may be unlogged real transfers, so we do not count them as failures. Unexplained alarms over all months fall from 149 (RO-NC) to 94 (re-ranker), but the scores were trained on the even-month candidates, so that fall is in-sample and is not a result.
    • Injected transfers (odd months, 998 injected transfers on pairs the injector never saw). Start-and-end recall is 0.259 for RO-NC, 0.265 for the re-ranker, 0.261 for the F network, and 0.264 for the logistic regression. Recall with no metered receiver is 0.198 for RO-NC and 0.215 for the re-ranker, against 0.290 and 0.290 with a metered receiver, so the learned scores do not collapse without a metered partner. The pair alarm falls from 0.146 to 0.018, and its intersection with the F network inherits that fall.
    • Hindsight control (post hoc, after the code review). Dropping the "level after the step" feature, which reads 1 to 2 days after the candidate, leaves the logistic regression at 22 (+0.043 [-0.003, +0.124]), and the re-ranker falls to 22 (+0.050 [-0.002, +0.132]). The re-ranker's raw window still reads 4 days ahead. Tables are in printed_report_ablation_no_persist.txt.
    • Post hoc ablation. Without the noise scale (a proxy for the series), the hour, and the season, the re-ranker finds 23 of 35 (+0.064 [+0.012, +0.154]) and the logistic regression 23 (+0.064 [+0.012, +0.154]). The F network falls to 21 (-0.041 [-0.261, +0.115]) because it loses one large transfer. The ablated networks have 2,209 and 10,089 weights. Tables are in printed_report_ablation.txt.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast, mean-forecast error, tail calibration, curtailment cost: not applicable, because no forecast is scored.
    • Audit triggers. None fired. No control failed: the re-implemented pair alarm finds 5, as in experiment 156. The result meets its prediction. Dropping any one pair never changes the sign of the re-ranker's lead. No nested arm beats the arm that contains it. No later design depends on this result yet. No Opus audit was run.
    • Even-month transfers gain more. The re-ranker's net gain over RO-NC is 4 transfers in even-month transfers, the months its injected training transfers sit in, and 1 in odd-month transfers (RO-NC 8, F network 8, and re-ranker 9 of 15). The ablation without season, hour, and noise scale keeps the gain, which argues against a seasonal shortcut, but 15 transfers cannot settle it.

    Caveats and lab shortcuts

    • RO-NC is re-implemented. It finds 19 here against 18 in its original run. Every arm uses the same end rule, so the contrasts are paired.
    • Hindsight inputs. The feature "level after the step" reads 2 days after the start, and the raw window reads 4 days after it, so the learned scores alarm days late. Reading future data is a lab shortcut.
    • Residual leakage. The convex model behind the real forecast errors was trained with the labelled transfers masked, which makes the features of the real transfers look cleaner than they would at serving time. We did not recompute the features from an unmasked model.
    • Negatives. Training negatives are real unexplained candidates in even months. They include probable unlogged transfers, which lowers every learned score slightly. The multi-day drops of one Primary substation that a gas peaker explains (D8), the 17 unlabelled block days of experiment 175, and all labelled or logged spans are excluded from the training negatives. An injected transfer that lands next to one of them still counts as a positive, and none of them is removed from the pool that sets the 80-alarm threshold (the same for every arm). The validation worlds hold the same real negatives as the training worlds, so early stopping, the tree's settings, and the stack were chosen on in-sample negatives and fresh positives.
    • Held-out pairs are held out by pair, not by Primary substation. A Primary substation of a held-out pair can sit in another fold's pair, so training sees synthetic transfers and real quiet stretches on the same Primary substations as the held-out transfers, as in experiment 189.
    • Best of several arms. The re-ranker's interval is not corrected for being the best of 11 learned and combined arms, and its lower end sits just above zero.
    • Intersection arms on injected transfers. Alarms of one world can match alarms of another world on the same series, which inflates the intersection arms' injected recall slightly. The real-record scores are unaffected.
    • Scoring months. Thresholds come from odd months, and transfers are scored in every month, as in experiment 189. We did not rescore on even months with thresholds from odd months only.
    • Not done. The gated-mask test of experiments 163 and 171, features from an unmasked model, and training that leaves one pair out (the leave-one-pair-out table drops a pair from the scores and does not retrain).

    What to try next

    • Test the extra alarms in the gated mask, because that bar decides porting.
    • Swap the odd and even months for training and thresholds, to settle whether the even-month gain is real.
    • Make the injector more realistic. The learned scores find injected transfers no more often than RO-NC does, so injected recall cannot rank detectors yet. A neural component is not the next step, because the logistic regression already matches the features-only neural network.
