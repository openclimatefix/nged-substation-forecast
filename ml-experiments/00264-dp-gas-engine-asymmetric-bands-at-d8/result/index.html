    Can D8's forecast bands carry the uncertainty that an undetectable gas engine adds, by widening in the direction the engine pushes?

    Widening the forecast band with residuals already seen narrows most of the gap at D8, but the gain from modelling a gas engine, beyond simple widening, is not shown. D8 is a primary substation of National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED). D8's half-hourly power readings appear to include the output of a 6 MW gas engine, probably three units of about 2 MW each, which experiment 191, experiment 198, and experiment 199 could not detect. This experiment does not try to detect the engine. It asks whether the forecast's q05-to-q95 band, the range meant to hold 90% of readings, can carry the uncertainty that the engine adds. A residual is the actual reading minus the forecast median, and every arm uses only residuals known when the forecast is made. The unchanged forecast (arm 1) is experiment 191's reference, a generalised additive model with a Kalman filter on recent errors (GAM-K) and no price input. We tested five ways of widening its band. Arm 2 widens only the lower quantiles, arm 2b widens both sides, arm 3 is a mixture that assumes 0 to 3 engine units are running, arm 4 is arm 3 plus a price known only in hindsight, and a post hoc baseline adds the quantiles of the past 365 days of residuals to the reference median.

    Widening the band raised the share of readings inside it from 0.42 (arm 1) to between 0.80 and 0.87 (arms 2b, 3, 4, and the baseline), and lowered the quantile score by 9% to 19% against arm 1. The quantile score is a score for the whole set of 11 forecast quantiles (smaller is better), and one point is 1% of the substation's effective capacity (0.1 MW at D8). By the pre-registered rule the verdict is not supported: no arm holds the share within 5 points of 0.90 in every quarter of the 12 months from 2025-07-02 to 2026-06-30, the July to September 2025 quarter has only weeks of residual history to learn from, and arm 2b raises the quantile score by 0.5% at D10, a substation with no engine. Arm 3 scores 2.5% better than arm 2b, yet the post hoc baseline, which has no engine in it, scores 0.4% better than arm 3, a difference smaller than its uncertainty. With the engine model forced on, arm 3 also fits an average 0.27 and 0.46 chance that an engine unit is running at D10 and D16, which have no engine. Arm 3's gain over arm 2b may therefore come from fitting the shape of D8's tails and not from an engine.

    Arms and rows

    • Rows. Experiment 191's stored reference forecast (arm a0, the GAM-K engine of experiment 152), fold_a_daily_gap_v1, seed 0, scored year 2025-07-02 to 2026-06-30, leads 0.5 to 168 hours, outside the hand-filtered events: 97,012 rows at D8, 96,370 at D10, and 91,754 at D16. The reference reproduces experiment 191's D8 scores (quantile score 10.95, mean absolute error 15.18 points, coverage 0.42).
    • Arm 1 is the reference.
    • Arm 2 recalibrates the lower quantiles q01 to q25, with a separate correction for each combination of four six-hour blocks of the day and four seasons.
    • Arm 2b widens both sides by the same method.
    • Arm 3 is the mixture over 0 to 3 running units of 19.7 points (1.97 MW). Where the visibility switch is off, arm 3 uses the arm 2b band. The switch turns the engine model on when 5% or more of the last 30 days' half-hours had a residual below -25 points (-2.5 MW), and off when the share falls below 3%.
    • Arm 4 is arm 3 plus two price flags known only in hindsight.
    • Post hoc baseline is the reference median plus the quantiles of the trailing 365 days of residuals, with no cells and no engine.
    • Schedule. Each arm refits every 7 daily forecast origins (the moment a forecast is made) on the trailing 365 days of residuals that were known at the block's first origin minus 6 hours. The reference's own refits are monthly, as in experiment 191.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast. Zero for arms 1, 2, and 2b. For arm 3, +0.14 points at D8, and 0.00 at D10 and D16 where the switch is off. For the forced run, +0.16 at D8, -0.04 at D10, and +0.15 at D16. For the post hoc baseline, -0.96, -0.54, and -0.26.
    • Mean-forecast error. Every arm keeps the reference's mean forecast, so the mean absolute error is 15.18 (D8), 5.54 (D10), and 4.70 (D16) points for all of them, and no contrast can disagree in direction with a mean-forecast contrast.
    • Audit triggers 1, 2, and 5 of the seven in the ml-experiment skill fired. A placebo failed (D10, and the forced run at D16). Five predictions failed (P1, P2, P4, P5, and P6), and P3 failed on its score clause. The forced D16 control moved the score by +0.089 points, more than the 0.083 half-width of its own interval.
    • Audit. A fresh Sonnet agent ran the audit, because the maintainer's brief bars Opus. The audit re-ran arms 2b and 3 after shifting every actual after 2026-01-10 by -40 points, and found every row at earlier origins bit-identical, so the as-of rule leaks nothing. It also reproduced the D8 numbers, found no bug in the distribution helpers, and found the post hoc baseline. The audit added the baseline arm and the noise-scale-limit caveat, and changed the interpretation of arm 3. It did not change the stored reference's median or the removal of hand-filtered events before fitting.

    Caveats and what to try next

    • Fit to the year. The descriptive table that chose arm 2b and the switch thresholds came from the scored year, and no second year of D8 forecasts is stored, so every verdict is exploratory.
    • Little residual history in 2025Q3. The earliest D8 forecasts are stored from 2025-07-02, so the first quarter, and the cells of the first seasons, rest on weeks of residuals.
    • Next. Candidates for further work are to store D8 reference forecasts for an earlier period so the fit starts with residuals, and to carry plain two-sided widening (arm 2b) and the past-residual baseline forward after a check at substations with no engine. Do not extend the mixture, or add a neural network to predict its unit probabilities, until NGED can supply the engine's own output, because the mixture has not been shown to measure an engine.

    Plan (as pre-registered)

    What was seen first

    • The reference forecast is too narrow in both directions, not only downwards. Before writing this plan we tabulated experiment 191's stored reference forecasts at D8 by month, using for each valid time the forecast with the shortest lead. In the 12 months the reference's q05-to-q95 coverage runs from 0.27 to 0.51. Between 22% and 44% of readings lie above q95, and 25% to 47% lie below q05. The median distance from q50 to q05 is only 6.8 to 9.8 points (0.7 to 1.0 MW), against a mean absolute error of about 15 points. The share of readings more than 25 points (2.5 MW) below q50 falls from 5% to 16% in July to March to 3.5% to 5.8% in April to June 2026. At D10 and D16 the same table shows coverage of 0.77 to 0.97, so the narrow bands are particular to D8. The lower-tail-only widening that the maintainer's brief names as arm 2 therefore cannot be enough on its own, and we added arm 2b, which widens both sides, to separate "the band is too narrow" from "the band is too narrow in the direction the engine pushes".
    • The table of coverage and tail shares is descriptive, was read before the arms were fixed, and chose arm 2b and the switch thresholds. The switch thresholds (on at 5%, off at 3%) sit between the placebo sites' share of about 0.1% and D8's share of 5% to 16% in July to March, and the write-up reports the switch at two other settings as a sensitivity check.
    • Limits of the design. Arms 2 and 2b move only the tails, so a bias in the median that the engine causes stays in place. Arm 1 is the stored output of experiment 191, whose monthly refits use only data before each month and whose spread was fitted on tuning months, so the bands being widened carry no information from the scored year beyond the stored forecast itself. No D8 forecast before 2025-07-02 is stored, so the first weeks of the first quarter have no resolved residuals.

    Inputs

    • Forecasts. Experiment 191's stored arm a0 (gated variant, which equals the ungated variant at D8 because D8 has no pre-connection rows) at D8 (time_series_id 26), and at the placebo sites D10 (10) and D16 (18). Each row holds q01 to q99, mean, actual, lead_hours, and run. The files are data/ml-experiments/00191-*/work/preds/gated/scored_<site>.parquet.
    • Units. One point is 1% of the site's effective capacity, which is 0.1 MW at D8. The engine unit size of 1.97 MW is 19.7 points, and the "drop" threshold of -2.5 MW is -25 points. The placebo sites use the same constants in points, because the question there is whether the same algorithm with the same settings leaves a site without an engine alone.
    • Price columns for arm 4 only. The stored spread_positive and price_p75 flags in the same files. They are hindsight inputs and never run in a live service.

    The as-of rule

    A residual enters a fit at origin O only if its valid time is at or before O. For each valid time, the training row is the stored forecast with the shortest lead, so each valid time counts once. The residual is actual minus q50. Every fit is refitted every 7 origins and applied to the next 7 daily origins, and fit windows are the trailing 365 days of resolved valid times. A fit needs at least 28 days (1,300 rows) of resolved valid times. Before that, every arm equals arm 1. This is the cold start. D8's scored forecasts begin on 2025-07-02, and no earlier D8 forecast is stored, so the first weeks of the first quarter cannot be improved. The burn-in rule in the decision rule handles that quarter.

    Arms

    1. Control. Arm 1, as above.
    2. Lower-tail widening (the maintainer's arm 2). For each row, the normalised shortfall is s = (q50 - actual) / (q50 - q05), with the denominator floored at 1 point. In each cell of hour-of-day block (00-06, 06-12, 12-18, and 18-24 UTC) by season (December-February, March-May, June-August, September-November), and with the cell falling back to the hour block alone, then to all rows, when it holds under 300 resolved rows, let S_tau be the empirical 1 - tau quantile of s for tau in {0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25}. The new lower quantile is q50 - max(r_tau, S_tau) * (q50 - q05), where r_tau is the reference's own ratio (q50 - q_tau) / (q50 - q05). The widening never narrows a band, and the upper quantiles and the mean are unchanged.
    3. Arm 2b (added by this plan). The same method applied to both sides: the upper quantiles q75, q90, q95, and q99 are recalibrated the same way from the normalised overshoot (actual - q50) / (q95 - q50).
    4. Mixture over running units (arm 3). The reading is modelled as the reference's distribution, with its spread multiplied by a noise scale c, shifted by delta - k * g, where k is the number of running units (0, 1, 2, or 3), g is 1.97 MW (19.7 points), and delta = g * E[k] is set per row so that the mixture's mean equals the reference's mean (the reference was trained on readings that include the engine). The reference distribution is the piecewise-linear cumulative distribution through its 11 quantiles, with an exponential tail beyond q01 and q99 whose density is continuous at those quantiles. The probability of k follows a multinomial logistic model of the hour block, the season, a weekend flag, and the share of half-hours in the 14 days before the origin with a residual below -25 points. The logistic model is fitted by expectation-maximisation on resolved residuals, with c on the grid {1, 1.5, 2} (capped at 2 so that one wide component cannot imitate the mixture) and the likelihood-maximising c kept, a ridge penalty of 1.0 on the logistic weights, and 30 expectation-maximisation iterations. The forecast quantiles come from inverting the mixture's cumulative distribution. A known-answer test on simulated readings (e00264_recovery.py) checks that the fit recovers the noise scale and the unit probabilities before the real run. The engine visibility switch turns on when the share of half-hours in the 30 days before the origin with a residual below -25 points reaches 5%, and turns off when the share falls below 3%. The mixture is fitted only on rows whose own origin had the switch on. Where the switch is off the forecast is arm 2b, not arm 1, because the upper-side miss is not caused by the engine.
    5. Arm 4. Arm 3 with spread_positive and price_p75 at the valid time added to the logistic model. These binary flags are a hindsight upper bound for the value of a price forecast, and they are coarser than the spark-spread level that the brief names.
    6. Placebo (arm 5). Arms 2, 2b, and 3 at D10 and D16, with the switch free, and with the switch forced on (a stress test that is the real placebo for the mixture, because the free switch is off at both sites).
    7. Not run. The small neural network for the mixture probabilities. The brief welcomes it only if it beats arm 3 by a pre-registered margin, and the plan sets none, so the write-up lists it under "what to try next".

    The as-of rule has a reporting latency. A reading counts as known 6 hours after its valid time, because NGED's delivery lag is unknown. A fit at origin T therefore uses valid times at or before T minus 6 hours, and so do the 14-day and 30-day drop shares. A fit is frozen at its block's first origin, so residuals that resolve during the block do not enter its fit.

    Contrasts and scoring

    • Contrasts. At D8: arms 2, 2b, 3, and 4 each minus arm 1 on the quantile score, arm 3 minus arm 2b, and arm 4 minus arm 3. Each is a mean paired difference with a 95% interval from 20,000 resamples of the 12 calendar months of the scored year, and a Bonferroni-corrected 99.2% interval (6 contrasts) beside it.
    • Cuts. All rows; the four quarters; q05-to-q95 coverage and the quantile score at leads 0 to 24, 24 to 72, and 72 to 168 hours (the fit uses shortest-lead residuals, so long-lead coverage is reported but not gated); the share of cells that fell back to the hour block, per quarter; months with the switch on and off; winter (November to March) evenings (16:00 to 20:00 UTC).
    • Tail table. Coverage and pinball loss at q01, q05, q95, and q99 per arm.
    • Checks. The median forecast minus the mean forecast, per site and arm. The error of the mean forecast beside each quantile-score contrast. The leave-one-month-out sign of each D8 contrast. Which of the seven audit triggers of the ml-experiment skill fired.
    • Curtailment. studies.node_curtailment needs a bulk supply point's forecast and its generators' forecasts, and this experiment forecasts a single Primary, so the metric cannot be run. The stand-in is the Brier score of the event "the reading falls below D8's 2nd percentile of readings in the 12 months before 2025-07-02", computed from each arm's 11 quantiles by linear interpolation of its cumulative distribution. The write-up labels the Brier score a stand-in and does not call it a curtailment cost.

    Compute

    CPU only, 4 cores at most, thread caps of 1, inside systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=16G -p OOMPolicy=continue. The fits use the 17,000 shortest-lead rows per site and refit 52 times, so the whole run should take minutes. The three sites run in parallel workers.

    Stop rule

    Stop and report if arm 1, rebuilt from the stored files, does not reproduce experiment 191's D8 absolute scores (quantile score 10.95, mean absolute error 15.18 points, q05-to-q95 coverage 0.42) to within 0.01, or if any arm's row keys differ from arm 1's.

    Anonymisation

    The metered generator is named G2 only. No generator time series with a name or an identifier is published. D8, D10, and D16 are substations and may be named.
