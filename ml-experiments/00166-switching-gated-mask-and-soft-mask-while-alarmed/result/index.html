    A mask on recent power that trusts only balanced pair alarms, and a soft mask that inflates the filter's observation noise

    When a detector flags that load may have been switched between substations, a forecast that ignores recent power only for trustworthy flags keeps 99.6% of the gain after a transfer and removes almost all the extra error elsewhere, but one year of data cannot show that the extra error is below the limit we set. Our forecast corrects itself using recent power. After a load transfer between substations has been recorded in NGED's switching log (a "logged transfer"), recent power misleads the correction, so experiment 165 ignored recent power (a "mask") whenever a switching alarm was active. That helped after a transfer but added error on other days. Here the "gated" mask ignores recent power only for alarms whose opposite steps at a neighbouring substation roughly cancel (a balance score of 0.7 or more). In the 72 hours after a logged transfer ends, the forecast error score is 6.34 points of a substation's effective capacity with the gated mask, against 13.69 with no mask and 10.09 for best guess 2 (BG2, the production-style XGBoost forecast of experiment 87). On the 559,137 real rows outside logged transfers the gated mask scores 0.009 points worse than no mask (95% interval from resampling calendar months, -0.068 to +0.081), where the mask from either alarm of experiment 165 scored 0.102 worse. The limit set before scoring was an interval within +/-0.03, so the verdict is not supported. A soft mask, which keeps the readings but treats them as noisier, changes almost nothing (at the three pre-registered factors) or gains and harms about as much as a hard mask (at factors we added afterwards). The next step is to re-test the gated mask on a longer period.

    We asked whether recent power can be ignored less bluntly while a switching alarm is active. The forecast, called the stack here, is a frozen generalised additive model (GAM) with a Kalman filter (Kalman, 1960) that corrects each forecast using its recent errors, as in experiment 163. Experiment 165 set the filter's readings missing from the moment an alarm was known until the end rule (the rule that decides an alarm has ended) of experiment 147 and experiment 148 fired. That mask helped after a transfer and added error elsewhere. Here the gated mask applies only to the pair alarms of experiment 156 (two metered substations step in opposite directions within hours) whose balance score is at least 0.7. The balance score is the smaller of the ratio of the two steps and its inverse, so 1 means the steps cancel exactly. The soft mask keeps every reading and multiplies the filter's observation-noise variance, from the moment the alarm is known until the end rule fires, by a factor that rises with the balance score. Scores are twice the mean pinball loss over 11 quantiles (a standard loss for quantile forecasts), in points of effective capacity; lower is better.

    By the pre-registered rule the verdict is not supported, for different reasons in the two parts. Part 1 (the gated mask) passes the gain test, G2: it keeps 99.6% of the hard mask's gain, against a required 70%. It fails the no-harm test, G1, because the interval is wider than the required +/-0.03, although the point estimate of +0.009 lies inside it. Six of the 13 transfer-substation pairs (one logged transfer seen at one substation) have a gated alarm known before the transfer ends, so the part is scored. Part 2 (the soft mask) fails the gain test, S2, at every pre-registered factor: at best the soft mask recovers 0.53 of the 7.38 points. The other two soft-mask tests pass trivially, because a soft mask that does nothing also does no harm.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast, identical for every stack arm: D10 -0.064, D11 -0.027, D14 +0.033, D16 +0.014, D5 -0.135, D9 -0.021 points of capacity.
    • Error of the median forecast beside the quantile contrast. Outside logged transfers (shared rows): K0 4.773, MASK_GATED 4.809, MASK_UNION 4.961, SOFT_F1000 4.778. After a transfer: K0 18.232, MASK_GATED 9.666, MASK_UNION 9.616, SOFT_F1000 17.653. Both measures agree with the quantile score in direction.
    • Triggers. Trigger 2 fired in part: the interval for G1 is wider than the +/-0.03 the plan required. The explanation was tested and holds: the arm scores -0.000 (-0.001 to +0.001) on the 514,228 rows with no gated alarm, so the width comes from the 44,909 rows outside logged transfers while a gated alarm is active or has just ended, and the extra error is confined to them. That width is 0.149 here and 0.147 for the pair alarm in experiment 165, so trigger 6 did not fire. Trigger 4 fired in part: leaving out D5 or D16 makes the interval for the gated gain after a transfer include zero, but the sign holds, and the not supported verdict rests on interval width, which no leave-one-out check changes. Triggers 1, 3, 5, and 7 did not fire: there is no control, the two error measures agree, the gated mask (a subset of the pair-alarm mask) scores within 0.015 points of it after a transfer, and no later design depends on this result yet. No audit was run.

    Caveats

    • Six of 13 pairs. The gain after a transfer rests on 3 transfer-substation pairs, at D5 and D16. One year of data is the limit, not the gate.
    • The gate threshold of 0.7 was fixed after counting how many alarms pass. It was not tuned on any forecast score; gates of 0.6 and 0.8, planned as post hoc checks, were not run.
    • Lab conditions. The filter's residual uses the analysis proxy, the alarm tables are experiment 156's, tuned on odd months of the same stream, and the end of each alarm is known by the 6-hour rule of experiment 148.
    • The soft mask applies to the pair alarm only. Its comparison with MASK_UNION in S1 mixes the change of alarm set with the change of mechanism, so the comparison with MASK_NEW and MASK_GATED in the post hoc sweep is the cleaner one.

    What the next experiment should answer

    The next question is whether the gated mask keeps its no-harm point estimate on a longer history. The interval half-width is about 0.075 points, so reaching +/-0.03 needs roughly six times as many months of rows, if the residual stream can be rebuilt for a longer history. A second question is why only 6 of 13 transfer-substation pairs have a pair alarm that passes the gate.
