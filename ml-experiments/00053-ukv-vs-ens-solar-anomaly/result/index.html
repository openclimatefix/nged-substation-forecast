    Experiment 53: does the UKV forecast the solar anomaly better than ENS?

    We asked whether the Met Office UKV (a 2 km weather model of the United Kingdom that runs to 2 to 5 days ahead) predicts how sunny each half-hour will be better than the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts ensemble forecast (ENS) that the forecast uses today. We converted each forecast's sunshine into the output of a generic solar fleet, subtracted that forecast's own monthly average for each half-hour of the day (the "anomaly"), and compared the result with the anomaly of satellite-based sunshine estimates from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) at 5 Primary substations, on 72 forecast runs from July 2025 to June 2026. The UKV alone was worse than ENS at every lead time. A mix of ENS and UKV, with weights fitted on the earlier 15 months, had a squared error 4% to 14% lower than ENS within 2 days in a cross-validation on those 15 months. In the validation year its correlation with CAMS was higher than ENS's by only 0.02 to 0.03 within 24 hours, and several of those gains are within the range that chance alone could produce. The mix was no better beyond 54 hours. The one UKV we found with a long matching history is the CEDA archive, which differs from the live UKV and is licensed for non-commercial use, so any gain is an upper bound. This experiment was run post hoc and was not pre-registered, as explained below.

    The verdict is small gain for the blend, and no gain for the UKV alone. The blend clears the 1% cross-validation margin of the stage 1 gate in every lead band within 54 hours, and the UKV alone clears it in none (relative mean squared error +12% to +31% against ENS). The gate therefore does not stop the experiment as refuted, and the verdict follows the pre-stated all-months gate. The correlation evidence from the validation year is weaker than the gate: the blend-minus-ENS interval touches zero at 0 to 6 hours (+0.019, -0.000 to +0.038) and includes zero over 0 to 54 hours (+0.011, -0.006 to +0.027), and at 48 to 54 hours the blend's validation mean squared error (0.0382) is worse than ENS's (0.0337), although the cross-validation says the blend is 3.8% better there. As the brief says, the experiment stops at the gate: the demand contrasts were not run, and the manager decides whether to run them (see "The demand step was not run").

    What was fixed beforehand and what was chosen after seeing results. The brief for PVDIS-8b and the PVDIS-8 plan fixed the three arms (ENS, UKV alone, a fitted blend), the lead bands, the 1% margin, and the 15-fold month-block cross-validation before any score existed. No prediction was written down. The ukv3w control and the t54_late variant were added after the first scores were seen, so they are post hoc, and neither changes the verdict.

    All figures are for 5 Primary substations (D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16, anonymised as in experiment 46), daylight half-hours of April to September, valid from July 2025 to June 2026, on 72 forecast runs (one every 5 days). The 1,000 to 4,714 half-hour rows per lead band come from only 6 or 7 calendar months of forecast origin (July to September 2025 and March to June 2026), so every interval below, which resamples those months, understates the true uncertainty.

    What was compared, and when each forecast would have been available

    Lead is counted from the forecast origin that the production service has, which is the 00 UTC ENS run plus 9 hours, 09:00 UTC. Each arm uses the freshest forecast available at that origin.

    • ENS is the daily 00 UTC run, as in experiment 46.
    • UKV, t54 is the 06 UTC run of the same day (leads to 54 hours from the run, so to 51 hours from the origin), assuming a run is available 3 hours after its initialisation.
    • UKV, t120 is the 03 UTC run of the same day, whose T120 files reach 120 hours from the run (114 hours from the origin, since the run starts 6 hours before the origin).
    • UKV, t54_late is the 00 UTC run of the same day, a sensitivity check that assumes the 06 UTC run is not yet available (leads to 45 hours from the origin).
    • CAMS is the reference: experiment 39's half-hour irradiance for the substation, kept where CAMS reliability is at least 0.9.

    The five arms are ENS alone, the UKV alone (native steps), the blend, and two controls that separate the time resolution of the UKV from its skill. Control ukv3h subsamples the UKV to ENS's 3-hourly steps and passes the instantaneous values through the production ENS resampler, labelled as if they were ENS's 3-hour window means, as the maintainer asked. Control ukv3w first forms 3-hour window means from the UKV (the trapezoid mean of the instantaneous values inside each window) and then uses the same resampler. The second control was added after the first scores because the first has a half-window timing error built in: in the hour-of-day figure below, the mean fleet output u of ukv3h peaks at 10 UTC, while CAMS, ENS, the UKV, and ukv3w all peak at 12 UTC.

    The calibration is a slope through the origin of the CAMS anomaly on a forecast's anomaly, fitted per series and lead band on the training window (valid times to 2025-06-30), leaving out site-band cells with fewer than 50 fitting rows. The blend regresses the CAMS anomaly on the ENS and UKV anomalies, with two coefficients and no intercept, and its weights are fitted rather than equal. Fitted on the whole training window, the ENS weight is 0.57 to 0.83 per series and band and the UKV weight is 0.17 to 0.46, with no negative weight (t54).

    Each source's climatology keeps, for every valid time, the run with the shortest lead. The same rule applies to ENS, the UKV, and the controls, so they have the same lead mix. Experiment 46 averages its ENS climatology over all leads instead. The two ENS climatologies correlate at 0.998, and the rebuilt ENS anomaly correlates with experiment 46's calibrated ENS anomaly at 0.986 to 0.989 (t120, t54, t54_late). That difference follows from the lead rule and is not a defect, so the script requires only a correlation above 0.98.

    The cross-validation removes the held-out month from the climatology. The fitted climatology therefore uses the neighbouring months only for a held-out month other than April to June, which has two neighbours inside the window, while the validation scores use the full 15-month climatology. This affects every arm alike.

    The latency of the 06 UTC run is assumed, not measured. The t54 arm assumes that the 06 UTC run is available 3 hours after its initialisation, so just in time for the 09:00 UTC origin, with no slack. No source for the real delivery time of the live UKV was checked. The t54_late and t120 arms are the cautious readings. The latency at which the blend's gain would disappear was not measured: even when the 06 UTC run is replaced by the 00 UTC run (a delay of more than 3 hours), the blend's cross-validated error is still 3.6% to 7.7% lower in every band.

    Shortwave conventions and how each was aligned with CAMS

    CAMS and ENS are half-hour or window means labelled at the period end, and the UKV and MOGREPS-UK are instantaneous values, so only the UKV and MOGREPS-UK need the interpolation.

    Source Shortwave convention Valid-time stamp Alignment with CAMS
    CAMS (experiment 39) Half-hour mean irradiance End of the half-hour None needed
    ENS Mean over the native window (3 hours to 144 hours, 6 hours after) End of the window The production resampler _upsample_nwp_to_half_hourly shapes each window by the clear-sky index and conserves its energy, giving half-hour means ending at the stamp
    CEDA UKV, shortwave_down Instantaneous flux, not a mean The instant, init time plus step; hourly steps to 48 hours, then 3-hourly The clear-sky index (flux over the Haurwitz clear-sky flux at the same instant) at each step is interpolated linearly in time to the midpoint of each half-hour, then multiplied by the clear-sky flux at that midpoint
    MOGREPS-UK, shortwave_total Instantaneous, as the archive notes (no cell methods or time bounds in the files) The instant; hourly steps The same as the UKV

    The same fleet chain then turns each half-hour irradiance into u. A clear-sky index is only formed where the clear-sky flux is at least 20 W/m2, and a midpoint more than 3 hours from a usable step takes the nearest step's index, so nothing is interpolated across a night. Beyond 48 hours the native UKV steps are 3-hourly, so the interpolation there spans 3 hours. The index is capped at 1.5. Each site's UKV value is the cell whose centre is nearest (the largest distance is 1.09 km).

    The check on MOGREPS-UK

    MOGREPS-UK, the live ensemble of the Met Office, is no better than ENS on 14 days of overlap with CAMS. Its point estimates are above ENS's in some bands, but an equal-weight blend is not distinguishable from ENS. MOGREPS-UK has 3 members and about 7 weeks of archive (27 August to 16 October 2026), so nothing was calibrated or fitted. The blend here averages the two anomalies with equal weight. The check starts after the holdout date of 2026-07-01, so it uses holdout-period data that no calibration touched, outside the validation year. MOGREPS-UK is anomalised against the CAMS climatology of experiment 46, while ENS is anomalised against ENS's own climatology, so MOGREPS-UK's bias is not removed. The mean squared error of MOGREPS-UK is therefore unfavourable, and its correlation is not affected. CAMS ends on 11 September 2026, so the reference covers only 14 forecast origin days (27 August to 10 September). Correlation with the CAMS anomaly, 95% interval from resampling whole origin days:

Over all 49 origin days from 27 August 2026, the MOGREPS-UK anomaly correlates with the ENS anomaly at 0.768 within 24 hours, 0.699 at 24 to 54 hours, and 0.545 at 54 to 120 hours. That correlation between the two anomalies is not a skill score, because neither anomaly is the reference. MOGREPS-UK's mean u is 0.303 against ENS's 0.373 and CAMS's 0.378 on those rows.

    The demand step was not run

    Experiment 46's contrasts C3 and C6 (the demand forecast with the solar term, at 0 to 48 hours) were not run with the blend. The blend's gain is a ceiling from a product that is not the live one (the CEDA UKV) and whose licence is non-commercial. The interval of the blend's gain over ENS across 0 to 54 hours includes zero (+0.011, -0.006 to +0.027). The scores here predict a change in the demand contrast of the order of a 0.02 gain in correlation, which experiment 46's intervals (half-widths of 0.13 to 0.32 points of capacity at 0 to 48 hours) could not resolve. A non-live product with a licence that forbids production is a weak case for the spend, so the manager decides whether to run the contrasts.

    What these data could not show

    • The live UKV is not the CEDA UKV. The CEDA archive differs statistically from the live feed, the CEDA licence is non-commercial, and the live product has no history at the Primaries, so the blend's gain is a ceiling.
    • The delivery delay of the 06 UTC run is assumed. See "What was compared" above: it was not measured, and the blend's gain survived the t54_late reading, so no latency at which it disappears was found.
    • The demand-forecast effect is untested. A 0.02 gain in correlation of the anomaly need not carry through experiment 46's solar term, whose coefficient the residual fitted below 1.
    • The 48 to 54 hour band is thin. It holds 1,225 rows from leads of 48 to 51 hours and its intervals are wide.
    • Calibration and the blend use 15 months. Each source's climatology is fitted on the same 15 months, with neighbouring local months pooled as in experiment 46.
