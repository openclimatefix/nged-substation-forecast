    Does the metered solar-farm forecast beat climatology at leads of 1 to 10 days, and which single model family is best?

    The assembled forecast still beats climatology at day 10, so it can serve forecasts of solar-farm power from 1 to 10 days ahead; plant-fed XGBoost cannot. National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED) meters six solar farms, labelled A to F here. Experiment 300 scored a forecast of each farm's power at leads of 12 to 36 hours. The forecast starts from a physics model of the farm's output (the "plant"), corrects the plant's 11 quantiles with a generalised additive model (GAM) and a mixture-density neural network (MDN), and blends those two with XGBoost. We refitted the same forecast at leads of 12 to 252 hours (day 1 to day 10), in six groups of lead times (day 1, day 2, day 3, days 4 to 5, days 6 to 7, and days 8 to 10). We scored it against climatology (the farm's power for the same month and half-hour of day in earlier history), manual_heuristic (quantiles of 13 earlier observed powers), and the GAM, the MDN, and plant-fed XGBoost. "Own history" means the forecast trains on the farm's earlier months, and "zero history" means it trains on the other five farms only. The quantile score is twice the mean pinball loss over the 11 quantiles, in points of capacity (1 point is 1% of the farm's capacity), and lower is better.

    The verdict is partly supported. The blend beat XGBoost in every day band, and removing the farm's own power from earlier days changed the score by less than its interval, as predicted. The blend's improvement in quantile score over climatology did not fall under 5% by days 8 to 10, so that part of the prediction failed: the improvement is 10.1% (98.75% interval -13.8% to -5.6%, own history). The stop rule did not fire.

    Method and schedule

    • Lead bands. Day d takes, for each half-hour, the 00 UTC ensemble run whose lead is 12 + 24(d-1) to 36 + 24(d-1) hours. A run is usable 9 hours after its init_time, and the first half-hour scored is 12 hours after it. Bands pool days 4 and 5, days 6 and 7, and days 8 to 10. Each band has its own fit, blend weights, and scores. Day 1 reproduces experiment 300's weather exactly, and its blend scores 5.30 against 5.27 there, a difference that fits the change of refit schedule.
    • Ensemble step. The ensemble's stamps are 3-hourly to lead 144 hours and 6-hourly after. From day 6 the shortwave value is a 6-hour mean, spread over the 12 half-hours of its period by the cosine of the sun's zenith angle (the angle between the sun and the vertical), and the temperature is interpolated linearly between 6-hourly stamps. The plant's response to each of 51 members is computed per half-hour.
    • Own power. The farm's observed power enters at a lag of ceil(lead / 24) days. That power is known at run time. The weather at every lead is the ensemble run itself, which exists at forecast time.
    • Schedule. Quarterly refits (the screening schedule) on all earlier months, each refit's training rows ending one day before the quarter starts, predicting the runs of its 3 months. Weekly refits were not run, so absolute scores could shift a little. We did not test whether the contrasts between arms depend on the schedule. The MDN has three seeds, averaged. A run belongs to the month of its init_time, and the 12 scored months are the months of the runs.
    • Arms. climatology, manual_heuristic, the plant alone ("P2", binned by forecast and ensemble spread), the GAM (depth-1 boosted trees on P2's 11 quantiles as base margin), the MDN (three-component Gaussian mixture on the residual from P2), XGBoost (depth 6, same inputs, no base margin), the blend (per-level simplex weights fitted on earlier months, equal weights for the first three months), and the GAM, MDN, XGBoost, and blend without lagged power (own history only).
    • Row set. The scored-year runs (2025-09 to 2026-08) with finite quantiles in every arm, including both floors; the code checks equal row counts. Quantiles are sorted before any loss.
    • Reference floors. The manual_heuristic takes the quantiles of 13 earlier observed powers (analogues 1 to 6 weeks and 49 to 55 weeks before the half-hour). An analogue counts only when it was observed before the run, so the 1-week analogue is dropped at leads over 7 days. climatology uses the farm's powers before the scored year.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast, own history (points of capacity, farms pooled). Blend +0.04 at day 1 and +0.72 at days 8 to 10, GAM +0.27 and +0.51, MDN +0.42 and +0.32, XGBoost -0.91 and -0.78, climatology -2.07 at every band. XGBoost's median sits below its mean forecast, as in experiment 300; the blend's median sits above it by under 0.8 points.
    • Error of the mean forecast beside each score contrast. Blend minus XGBoost on the absolute error of the mean forecast is -4.0% at day 1 (9.92 against 10.34) and -4.5% at days 8 to 10 (15.60 against 16.34). Blend minus climatology is -42.8% at day 1 and -10.1% at days 8 to 10 (15.60 against 17.35). The error of the mean forecast and the score point the same way in every band, so trigger 3 did not fire.
    • Audit triggers. Trigger 2 (a result contradicting its prediction) fired: the margin over climatology stays near 10% at days 8 to 10 against a predicted fall under 5%. We tested an explanation after the fact. We refitted P2 (own history, days 8 to 10) with each row's plant forecast and spread replaced by their average over the training rows of the same month and half-hour. That plant (P2 alone), which carries the season and the sun's path but none of the ensemble's day-specific weather, scores 8.24 against 8.63 for climatology (-4.6%). P2 alone with the real ensemble weather scores 7.91 (-8.4%), against the blend's 7.77 (-10.1%). So about 4.6 percentage points of the plant's 8.4% margin come from conditioning on season and sun geometry, which climatology's coarse cells (one year of farm history per month and half-hour) do not do, and about 3.8 points come from the ensemble's forecast weather. At day 1 the same check scores 8.41 for P2 alone with averaged weather and 5.55 with the real ensemble weather, so the weather matters most at short leads. The check is post hoc (e00310_climatological_plant.py). Other triggers. Trigger 4 (leaving one farm out flips a contrast) did not fire: the blend beats climatology and XGBoost at all six farms in every band, so dropping a farm cannot flip a sign (Figure 4). Trigger 5 did not fire: no ablated arm beats its full arm by more than its interval's half-width. Triggers 1 (a failed control), 3 (the mean-forecast error disagrees), 6 (an interval more than twice or under half as wide as a sister experiment's: day 1's interval for blend minus XGBoost is 2.3 points wide against 2.3 in experiment 300), and 7 (a later design depends on the result) did not fire. No audit was required.

    Caveats and next steps

    • Post hoc. The climatological-plant check and the day-by-day slices were added after the scores. The 98.75% level covers the four planned contrasts per band only.
    • Not covered. Weekly refits, leads under 12 hours or over 252 hours, the curtailment value (the farms are single series and the node metric needs a substation with its generators), and the GAM, MDN, and XGBoost without lagged power in zero history.
    • Climatology uses older history. climatology is frozen at the data before 2025-09, while the forecasts refit each quarter on all earlier months. The margins over climatology in later quarters therefore include the forecasts' use of newer history.
    • Intervals. Only the four planned contrasts per band are at the 98.75% level. The per-farm, per-day, and extra contrasts use the same interval width and are descriptive. Every arm holds the same row count in each band and setting, the _nolag arms included.
    • Ensemble fields after lead 144 hours. The shortwave value is treated as a 6-hour mean there. The eight extra ensemble fields (humidity, longwave, precipitation, pressure, wind, and 500 hPa height) come from experiment 270's extraction, and we did not check that their period meaning is the same across lead 144 hours.
    • Lower tail. Actual output falls below the 0.01 quantile 3.2 to 3.5 times too often for the blend, and up to 4.4 times too often for XGBoost, at every lead, as in experiment 300.
    • Next. Weekly refits of the MDN alone at days 1 to 10 would give the absolute scores for a first production model; a longer ensemble history would replace the farm history behind climatology's coarse cells.
