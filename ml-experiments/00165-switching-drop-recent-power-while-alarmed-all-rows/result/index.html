    A forecast that ignores recent power whenever an LSC alarm is active, scored on every real row

    Ignoring recent power while a switching alarm is active helps only in the days after a logged transfer ends, and the mask from either alarm fails the pre-registered limit on rows outside transfers. The stack scores 6.31 points of a substation's effective capacity in the 72 hours after a logged transfer ends, against 13.69 with no mask and 10.09 for best guess 2 (BG2). On the 559,137 real rows outside logged transfers, the mask scores 0.102 points worse than no mask (95% interval from resampling calendar months, -0.013 to +0.205). The extra error is largest while an alarm is active on rows that match no logged transfer, and in months outside winter (November to March). The next step is a mask gated on how reliable the alarm is, or used in winter only.

    We asked whether the filter on recent errors should be switched off while a switching alarm is active. The forecast, called the stack here, is a frozen generalised additive model (GAM) with a Kalman filter that corrects each forecast using its recent errors, as in experiment 163. The mask sets the filter's readings missing from the moment an alarm is known until the end rule of experiment 147 and experiment 148 fires (the rule that decides a switching event has ended), and the filter then restarts. The alarms are the pair alarm of experiment 156 (two metered substations step in opposite directions within hours) and the refit-offset neighbour-count detector (RO-NC). Experiment 163 scored the mask on logged transfers and on ordinary rows that left out the hand-filtered switching events. This experiment scores every real row at the six decision Primary substations, in the scored year July 2025 to June 2026, and splits the rows by logged transfer and by the alarm active at the forecast origin. Scores are twice the mean pinball loss over 11 quantiles (the standard loss for quantile forecasts), in points of effective capacity; lower is better.

    By the pre-registered rule the verdict is not supported. The gain test passes: the mask from either alarm scores 7.38 points lower than no mask after a transfer ends on the 7 transfers BG2 also forecasts, and 4.95 points lower on all 11 transfers, with intervals that end just below zero. The no-harm test fails: the upper end of the interval for the mask from either alarm on the real rows outside logged transfers is +0.205, against a required +0.01. The mask from the pair alarm alone also fails, with an upper end of +0.105. The test while a transfer runs fails as predicted, because the observed power then contains the moved load.

    • Arms. K0 (the stack with no mask), MASK_NEW (mask from the pair alarm), MASK_UNION (mask from either alarm, the headline), MASK_RONC and GAM_ONLY (the generalised additive model alone, with no recent power) as references, ORACLE (the logged interval masked from its logged onset, on logged-transfer rows only), BG2 (experiment 87), experiment 155's arm C, the manual heuristic, and climatology. The stack arms share the K0 spread of experiment 115, so they differ in location only. The GAM and the filter are frozen, so no refit schedule applies.
    • Rows. The scored year holds 586,232 rows with a reading at D5, D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16 (anonymised substation labels), and 581,078 of them are shared with BG2. Of the 586,232 rows, 27,095 lie within a logged transfer or the 72 hours after it, and 559,137 are outside. Scores that involve BG2, and the absolute scores below, use the rows shared with BG2. The alarm is active at the forecast origin on 14.7% of rows for the pair alarm (D5 32.2%, D9 0%), 10.2% for RO-NC, and 22.2% for the union (D5 35.7%, D10 28.6%).
    • Absolute scores, outside logged transfers (shared rows). K0 2.725, MASK_NEW 2.758, MASK_UNION 2.826, MASK_RONC 2.986, GAM_ONLY 3.029, BG2 2.693, arm C 2.664, manual heuristic 3.390, climatology 3.652.

    Checks

    • Median minus mean forecast, identical for every stack arm: D10 -0.064, D11 -0.027, D14 +0.033, D16 +0.014, D5 -0.135, D9 -0.021 points of capacity.
    • Error of the median forecast beside the quantile contrast. Outside logged transfers (shared rows): K0 4.773, MASK_NEW 4.853, MASK_UNION 4.961, GAM_ONLY 5.334, BG2 4.734. After a transfer: K0 18.232, GAM_ONLY 10.726, MASK_NEW 9.639, MASK_UNION 9.616, BG2 15.467. Both agree with the quantile score in direction.
    • Triggers. Trigger 4 fired in part: leaving D5 or D16 out makes the interval of MASK_UNION minus K0 after a transfer include zero (C3), but the sign holds and the not supported verdict rests on C1, whose upper end stays at or above +0.17 whichever substation is left out, so no verdict could change and no audit was run. Trigger 5 holds as in experiment 163: the mask scores level with the logged-interval mask (ORACLE) after a transfer (-0.05 points, 95% -0.14 to +0.25), so the logged interval is not a ceiling. Triggers 1, 2, 3, 6, and 7 did not fire: the no-alarm rows score within +0.004 of K0, every result lies in its predicted range, the two error measures agree, the interval widths match experiment 163's, and no later design depends on this result.

    Caveats

    • 11 transfers, 7 shared with BG2. The 72-hour test rests on 11 logged transfers. The per-transfer figure shows most of the gain at two transfers, as in experiment 163.
    • Active alarms are common. The union alarm is active at the forecast origin on 22.2% of rows, and 111,159 of those rows lie outside logged transfers. Some of those rows are real switching that the log lacks, so the extra error from the mask on those rows may be overstated.
    • Lab conditions. The filter's residual uses the analysis proxy, and the alarm tables are experiment 156's, tuned on odd months of the same stream (experiment 156).
    • Masked history persists. After an alarm ends, the readings inside it stay missing in the filter's history, That missing history explains the +0.004 on rows with no alarm active.

    What the next experiment should answer

    The next question is whether a mask from the pair alarm alone, or a mask used only in winter, keeps the gain after a transfer and keeps the extra error outside logged transfers under +0.01. A gate on the alarm's step size, or a partial mask that down-weights recent power instead of removing it, are the two variants the pattern of extra error suggests.
