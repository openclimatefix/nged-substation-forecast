    Do the candidate changes for solar and wind add up to a better pooled mixture?

    We asked whether combining the small improvements found earlier for solar and wind farm forecasts gives a better forecast than the current method. We built the full combination, removed each improvement in turn, and measured the accuracy of the forecast range. For wind, the combination improved accuracy by 0.071 points of capacity, and a starting guess taken from the turbine power curve supplied most of it. For solar, no improvement helped detectably.

    Verdict: partly supported. For wind, the full stack (a power-curve base margin and a spread factor) lowers the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) by 0.071 points of capacity, and the base margin carries the gain. For solar, no component detectably helped and the full stack was 0.035 points higher, with an interval that does not rule out a gain of 0.075. ENS is the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts ensemble, ERA5 its reanalysis of past weather, and CAMS the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service's satellite irradiance estimate. The production recipe (arm base) is the pooled ERA5-trained mixture of experiment 00002. This is proposal P22 of the research plan. This experiment combines the changes that experiment 00012, experiment 00014, and experiment 00016 flagged as candidates or small gains, and drops each in turn. Gains of this size may not add, because the CAMS-trained stage 1a XGBoost model and the stage 0 mapping act on the same ENS irradiance bias.

    Size

    • What gets stored: private prediction sets and tables only. No schema in packages/contracts/ changes.
    • Production serving path: not touched.
    • Degradation rule: not touched.
    • More than one defensible design: yes. The stage 0 form (a regression, as in 00012, or a quantile mapping, as in 00014), the spread-factor grouping, and the form of the wind base margin for a multi-quantile objective all admit alternatives. This page fixes one of each.
    • Code whose callers cannot be named without searching: no. The scripts copy the patterns of experiments 00012, 00014, and 00016, which an experiment may not import.

    The size is complex, so the plan, the code, and the write-up each get a Sonnet review.

    Which earlier advice this plan follows

    • Followed: keep the training start at 2019-09 and leave out the clear-sky-ratio target (00012); pair a CAMS-trained stage 1a with a stage 0 mapping onto the same irradiance and check the member spread afterwards (00014); scale the solar mixture's quantiles by one factor, and the wind mixture's on two lead groups (under 72 h, and 72 h or more), refitted monthly on earlier cases (00016); report absolute scores and the climatology beside every contrast (00001).
    • Departs from: 00012's stage 0 was a regression on the control member, which gave no gain on the member average. This plan uses 00014's empirical quantile mapping, which kept the member spread. Fitting stage 0 on the whole ensemble is not possible, because the ENS training table holds member 0 only.

    Design

    Rows and series. The 6 solar farms and 3 wind farms of fold_a_tuesdays_v1: 53 runs on Tuesdays with valid times from 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30, three seeds. Only labels appear in the write-up.

    Stage 1a quantile model. One XGBoost model per series and seed with the 9-level multi-quantile objective of experiment 00002 (500 rounds, eta 0.05, depth 6), the same columns as 00001 (9 for solar, 6 for wind), no calendar and no power-lag column, trained on cleaned power at valid times from 2019-09 to before 2025-06-17. The pooled mixture treats the 51 members' 9 quantiles as equal-weight atoms, as in 00002.

    Weather source at forecast time. ENS members only. ERA5 and CAMS are read at training only, so nothing needs to exist at forecast time that is late (ERA5 arrives about 5 days late). No arm has a lag column.

    Fit windows. Every fit uses cleaned power and ERA5 or CAMS weather at valid times before 2025-06-17: the stage 1a quantile models, the wind power curve (refitted for each seed on the series' own training rows), and the stage 0 mappings. Training reads the ERA5 100 m wind speed and forecasting reads the ENS speed, which the base margin inherits. A margin makes XGBoost ignore its base score, and the same margin goes to all 9 levels, so the predicted quantiles are sorted afterwards. The code guards that the margin changes the predictions.

    Spread factor. Each arm refits its own factor on its own earlier cases (a factor from experiment 00016 would not fit different quantiles). A fit needs at least 5,000 non-trivial earlier cases (cases whose uncorrected 95% quantile exceeds 1% of capacity); a month or lead group without a fit stays uncorrected. The first month never has earlier cases, and the short wind lead groups fit late in the year, which bounds what any spread-factor contrast can show. The two wind lead groups were chosen after experiment 00016's results and are validated here on the same year.

    Stage 0 checks (descriptive). The write-up reports, for each solar arm with a stage 0 mapping, the ratio of the mapped ENS daylight shortwave to the target irradiance by month, and the member spread before and after, as experiment 00014 did.

    Components.

    Component Technology What it changes
    CAMS-trained solar The shortwave column of training rows is CAMS irradiance at the farm, not ERA5 shortwave
    Stage 0 solar An empirical quantile mapping per lead band and zenith bin (00014's design) maps each ENS member's shortwave onto the irradiance the stage 1a trained on, CAMS for a CAMS-trained arm and ERA5 for an ERA5-trained arm. The mapping is fitted on the control member's pairs with valid times before 2025-06-17, pooled over the 6 farms (the run asserts the latest valid time)
    Base margin wind The 100 m wind-speed power curve (binned mean power, running maximum) is the booster's base margin for all 9 quantiles
    Spread factor both The quantiles are scaled about the median by the factor nearest 90% coverage of the 5% to 95% band, fitted for each scored month on non-trivial cases with valid times before the first run of that month. Solar uses one factor, wind one for leads under 72 h (the first two lead bands) and one for longer leads

    Arms.

    Arm Solar Wind
    base ERA5-trained, raw ENS ERA5-trained, raw ENS
    cams CAMS-trained n/a
    cams_s0 CAMS-trained, stage 0 n/a
    margin n/a base margin
    base_spread base + spread factor base + spread factor
    full cams_s0 + spread factor margin + spread factor
    full_no_cams ERA5-trained, stage 0 onto ERA5, spread factor (drops the CAMS-trained pair of training and stage 0 target) n/a
    full_no_s0 cams + spread factor n/a

    Dropping the spread factor from the full stack gives cams_s0 (solar) and margin (wind). Dropping the base margin from the wind stack gives base_spread.

    Planned contrasts

    The experiment plans 12 contrasts. Each is the arm named first minus the arm named second, scored per technology.

    Id Solar Id Wind
    S1 full - base (headline) W1 full - base (headline)
    S2 cams - base W2 margin - base
    S3 cams_s0 - cams W3 base_spread - base
    S4 base_spread - base W4 base_spread - full (drop the margin)
    S5 full_no_cams - full (drop CAMS) W5 margin - full (drop the spread factor)
    S6 full_no_s0 - full (drop stage 0)
    S7 cams_s0 - full (drop the spread factor)

    The write-up also reports each arm's absolute CRPS and its 5% to 95% coverage by lead band, with the climatology row of experiment 00002.

    Results

    • Absolute CRPS, all leads (points of capacity). Solar: recipe 5.03, cams 5.02, cams_s0 5.05, base_spread 5.03, full 5.06, full_no_cams 5.05, full_no_s0 5.02, climatology 5.63. Wind: recipe 11.11, margin 11.05, base_spread 11.09, full 11.04, climatology 13.03. The seed spread is 0.001. The recipe reproduces experiment 00002 (solar 5.03 against 5.05, wind 11.11 against 11.11).
    • Wind. W1, full minus recipe: -0.071 (-0.133 to -0.014), a gain (at most -0.05, interval below zero). W2, base margin alone: -0.055 (-0.110 to -0.007). W3, spread factor alone: -0.021 (-0.043 to -0.002). Dropping the margin from the stack raises the CRPS by 0.050 (W4, +0.005 to +0.098), so the margin stays. Dropping the spread factor raises it by 0.016 (W5, -0.004 to +0.036), so the spread factor does not stay by the rule.
    • Solar. S1, full minus recipe: +0.035 (-0.075 to +0.116), inconclusive. S2, CAMS-trained: -0.009 (-0.127 to +0.072). S3, stage 0 on top of CAMS-trained: +0.036 (-0.093 to +0.208). S4, spread factor: +0.006 (-0.004 to +0.025). Dropping CAMS (S5), stage 0 (S6), or the spread factor (S7) changes the CRPS by -0.010, -0.044, and -0.008, so no component stays.
    • Coverage. The spread factor brings the 5% to 95% band to 87.9% to 92.5% in every lead band for both technologies, against 94.3% to 95.3% for the solar recipe and 89.5% to 95.3% for the wind recipe.

    • Stage 0 check. Over the validation year, the mapped ENS daylight shortwave is 0.988 of CAMS (raw ENS: 1.045), but the monthly ratio ranges from 0.83 (November) to 1.10 (July), and the mean member spread is 25% wider than before the mapping.
    • Prediction against result. The CAMS-trained gain (S2, predicted near -0.08) came out at -0.009, and stage 0 (S3, predicted +0.05 to -0.07) at +0.036, inside its range. For the full stack, the solar prediction (-0.10, plausible range +0.02 to -0.20) was wrong in sign. The wind prediction (-0.04, reaching only a small gain) was too cautious: the stack reached the gain threshold. The solar spread factor was predicted to be dropped, and was.

    Failures and limits

    • The claims rest on 6 solar farms and 3 wind farms in one validation year. The solar paired intervals are 0.19 to 0.30 points wide, so a gain under 0.1 points is not resolved for solar.
    • Stage 0 was fitted on the ENS control member's pairs only, pooled over the 6 farms, and corrects the year's mean but not individual months.
    • The wind spread-factor groups (under 72 h, and 72 h or more) were chosen after experiment 00016's results and are validated here on the same year. The first month, and the short wind groups for most of the year, have too few earlier cases for a fit and stay uncorrected.
    • Coverage uses the recipe's non-trivial cases for every arm, while each arm's spread factor is fitted on the arm's own earlier non-trivial cases.
    • No post hoc tweak was run. A stage 0 that keeps the member spread would need a refit of the CAMS-trained stage 1a models, which were not saved.

    Lessons

    • A change that helps the mean absolute error of the member average need not help the pooled mixture's CRPS. CAMS-trained solar moved the first by -0.14 in experiment 00012 and the second by -0.009 here.
    • Stage 0 lowered the Primary demand quantile score by 0.067 in experiment 00014 and did not lower the solar CRPS here. The mapping widened the daylight spread of the 51 members by about a quarter over the year, which may be why the pooled mixture did not improve.
