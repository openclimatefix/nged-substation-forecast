    Experiment 74: do same-time analogues add anything once the solar forecast is explicit? (AN1)

    Take-home message: telling the XGBoost model of a Primary substation's power about sunshine, in either of two ways, lowered its daylight error by 0.09 to 0.15 points, and the two ways help in different conditions. One point is 1% of the substation's capacity. The explicit forecast of the solar panels' output wins at leads under 2 days (a lead is the time from the forecast start to the half-hour being forecast) and on cloudy rows (rows with a clear-sky index under 0.3, which is the ratio of the measured sunshine to the cloud-free sunshine). The analogues, which are recent days at the same time of day with similar sunshine, match the explicit forecast on sunny rows and in August and September. Using both scored best, at 4.518 points against 4.584 for the explicit forecast alone, but one validation year (the 12 months from July 2025 to June 2026) cannot resolve a gain that small. The next test needs more forecast weeks and one new variant, analogues matched on the day type alone. The verdict is inconclusive.

    We asked how to tell a forecast of a Primary substation's power about the sunshine. A Primary substation steps 33 kV or 66 kV down to 11 kV in the electricity distribution network. Solar panels behind the substation's meter cut the metered power on a sunny day, so the forecast needs the sunshine. We tried two ways on five substations over daylight half-hours of April to September. A row is one half-hour forecast of one substation from one forecast run. In the explicit way, the panels' output is forecast as their capacity times the forecast sunshine, with a fitted weight, and the XGBoost model receives that output as a starting offset: a value added to the model's prediction for each row before its trees. The forecast sunshine comes from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts ensemble (ENS). In the analogue way, we search the recent days for the 3 whose measured sunshine was closest to the sunshine forecast for the target, among days at the same half-hour and of the same day type (working day, weekend, or bank holiday). The measured sunshine is a satellite-based estimate, CAMS, from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service. The XGBoost model then receives the metered power on those 3 days as input columns. We refitted the XGBoost model every month on the latest year of data for both ways. Experiment 34 found that a model that is never refitted overstates the value of an input that follows the recent level of power. Both ways beat the same monthly refit without either input. The explicit way beat it by 0.15 points, and the analogue way by 0.095 points. The experiment could not tell which way is better, and could not tell whether the analogues add anything once the offset is in the model.

    Legend of the arms. R0 is the monthly refit without either input, the reference. E is the refit with the explicit offset. E_twin is E given the sunshine forecast of another date, a placebo that keeps the capacity and the spread of the sunshine and removes its timing. A1, A3, and A5 are the refits with 1, 3, and 5 analogues. A3E has the 3 analogues and the offset. A3S has 3 days drawn at random from the same recent days, ignoring sunshine and day type, a placebo for the matching. R0_rad is R0 plus the radiation inputs, a context arm. The analogue columns are the mean and the standard deviation of the 3 days' power, the mean gap in sunshine, and the number of days found.

    The planned contrasts

    The decision rule was committed before any code ran. The rule has six contrasts on the quantile score, an error measure where lower is better. Every contrast is a paired difference of two arms' scores, with whole weeks of forecast start resampled, and every interval is 99.17% (a 5% error split across six). Seed 0 decides the verdict. C3 is the head-to-head contrast. C4 asks whether the analogues add anything once the offset is in, and needs its upper end below -0.05 to be worth porting, which means building the analogue columns in the live service. The rule has four verdicts. refuted needs C3's upper end below zero or C4's upper end below -0.05. supported needs C1's upper end below zero, C3's lower end above zero, and C4's lower end at or above -0.05. small gain is the same as supported except that C4's upper end is below zero. Neither of the first two is met, so the verdict is inconclusive: C3 runs from -0.053 to +0.161, and C4 from -0.149 to +0.015.

    The values are differences in quantile score, in points of capacity. A negative value favours the first arm. The rows are 68,903 rows from 29 weeks of forecast start, at five Primary substations anonymised as D9, D10, D11, D14, and D16, one forecast run every 5 days in July to September 2025 and April to June 2026. Every interval except C1's has a half-width above the 0.05 porting threshold, so C4 could not rule out a gain of that size. The half-width is half the interval's width: 0.05 (C1), 0.09 (C2), 0.11 (C3), 0.08 (C4), 0.07 (C5), and 0.08 (C6).

    For context, three frozen models of experiment 64 score 4.620 (d0, its best guess), 4.723 (d1l, the best guess without radiation inputs), and 4.466 (am, the offset model) on the same rows. They carry the 14-day correction, an average of the recent forecast errors added to the forecast, so no contrast uses them.

    The offset wins at short leads and on cloudy rows; the analogues catch up on sunny rows

    Both gains are largest within 2 days of the forecast start and are gone from 10 days, and the offset gains more at the shortest leads. At leads under 48 hours, C1 is -0.439 (95% interval -0.578 to -0.306) and C2 is -0.237 (-0.316 to -0.172), so C3 is +0.203 (+0.060 to +0.346). The analogues add to the offset in that range: C4 is -0.153 (-0.227 to -0.086). From 10 days, no contrast differs from zero. All reads on this page other than C1 to C6 are exploratory, at 95%.

    The split by sunshine shows why the two ways differ. On cloudy rows (a clear-sky index under 0.3), the analogues trail the offset by 0.429 points (0.224 to 0.657). On sunny rows (an index of 0.7 or more), C3 is -0.050 (-0.199 to +0.098) and C4 is -0.138 (-0.251 to -0.023). On these 5 substations' rows, the forecast sunshine helped most on cloudy days, and a similar past day helped most on sunny days. By substation, C3 favours the offset at D9 (+0.166, +0.066 to +0.269) and D11 (+0.101, +0.051 to +0.153), and is under 0.04 in size, in either direction, at D14 and D16.

    By month, the analogues match the offset only in August and September. C3 is +0.110, +0.139, +0.067, and +0.043 in April to July, +0.006 in August, and -0.062 in September, so the analogues trail the offset in the first four months. C4 is -0.163 in August and -0.175 in September. The monthly figures are means of each month's differences, each from 5 to 7 weeks of forecast start. The 95% intervals are in the printed report, and most of C3's include zero.

    Other reads

    Every read in this list is exploratory, at 95%, except C5 and C6, which are planned and use 99.17%.

    • Matching probably helps on sunny rows. A3 against A3S, the 3 random days, is -0.081 (-0.165 to +0.001) over all rows (C6, planned), so the upper end is a hair above zero. The gain from matching is -0.197 (-0.275 to -0.130) at leads under 48 hours and -0.188 (-0.307 to -0.060) on sunny rows. On cloudy rows the contrast is +0.137 (+0.002 to +0.293), which means that matched analogues are worse than random days there.
    • One analogue scored as well as three. A1 minus A3 is -0.008 (-0.037 to +0.019), and A5 minus A3 is +0.034 (+0.014 to +0.060).
    • The offset adds to the analogues. A3E minus A3, the offset's gain on top of the analogues, is -0.121 (-0.157 to -0.086).
    • The offset carries solar information. C5 (planned) shows the offset beats its twin by 0.246 (0.173 to 0.322), and experiment 64 found the same direction (0.300).
    • At night the analogues are close to random days. At night rows of April to September, A3 minus A3S is -0.020 (-0.042 to +0.002), A3 minus R0 is -0.006 (-0.030 to +0.017), and E minus R0 is +0.004 (-0.001 to +0.009).
    • Radiation inputs help the reference too. R0_rad minus R0 is -0.120 (-0.151 to -0.090), a context read that no contrast uses.
    • Coverage. The 0.05 quantile lies above the actual power on 13.5% to 15.0% of sunny rows in every arm, and the 0.95 quantile lies above it on 80.6% to 82.9% of cloudy rows.
    • No analogue exists for 8.7% of rows (a bank holiday, or no day of the same type with observed power and CAMS data in the window), and 89.4% of rows have all 3.

    Seeds 1 and 2 changed no planned contrast by more than 0.003 points. The widths of the intervals come from the weeks of forecast start, not from fit noise. The verdict from seeds 0 to 2 together is also inconclusive (C3 lower end -0.048, C4 upper end +0.016). The seed check is post hoc.

    Departures from the plan

    The experiment departed from its plan in four ways, none of which changes the hypothesis, the prediction, or the decision rule.

    1. A ninth arm, R0_rad, was fitted as context. It enters no contrast.
    2. Seeds 1 and 2 were fitted after the verdict, as a post hoc check.
    3. The prediction sets are stored under lower-case names, r0, e, a3, and so on, for seed 0, and with the suffix -s012 for seeds 0 to 2, because a stored set cannot be overwritten.
    4. The common starting level is the median of the training labels, not zero. Experiments 76 and 78 found that starting from XGBoost's estimated base score raises the score by 0.07 to 0.10 points against a start at zero in the same pipeline. The plan gave every arm one common level so that the contrasts do not carry that effect. Whether a start at zero would change R0, or the ranking of the arms, is not tested.

    Limits that the plan stated hold. The capacity table is experiment 64's and is 5% to 19% below experiment 45's 2025/26 values, which experiment 78 found worsens am by about 0.02 points. The weights and the sunshine calibration are fixed on data to 2025-06-30. The analogues are matched in training to the ensemble-mean sunshine and at inference to each member's own, which widens the pooled spread of the analogue arms. The candidates share a half-hour of the day in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), so a window that straddles a clock change shifts a local-time profile by an hour. The placebo twin of E is a cyclic shift over the whole window's rows. A donor for a row from the early window may therefore be a forecast issued after that refit. Only a sunshine anomaly crosses that boundary. No power or label does.

    What the next experiment should answer

    The next experiment should test the analogue columns on top of the offset with more forecast weeks, one analogue, and a variant matched on the day type alone. A second validation year with as many weeks (experiment R11, which needs the ENS backfill) would cut the half-width of C4 from 0.08 to about 0.06, because the half-width falls with the square root of the number of weeks. That is still above 0.05, so the plan should either set the threshold near 0.06 or ask for a third year. The plan should also read sunny and cloudy rows separately and add the day-type-only variant, because C6 does not separate the sunshine from the day type. The decision whether to port either way belongs to the maintainer and the roadmap. The offset costs a table of capacities and weights. The analogue columns cost the last 3 to 16 days of telemetry, a CAMS-like sunshine record, and a bank-holiday calendar at every forecast.

    Reviews

    Plan review (Sonnet), triaged. Accepted: give every arm one starting level, state the rule that separates small gain from supported, rebuild the training inputs for the whole P-roll window, state how E_twin is trained, note the column-count difference between arms, one definition of day type, and the prose faults. Rejected: none.

    Code review (Sonnet), triaged. No leakage and no wrong join. Accepted: add the ninth arm to the plan, make the guard in known_rows check the origin as well as the valid time, and state the mismatch between the training and inference sunshine shape. Rejected: a check of an A3 variant matched to the ensemble-mean shape at inference, because the arm of experiment 64 shares the same design and the experiment answers the plan's question without it.

    Number check (Sonnet), triaged. Accepted: the September value, the context scores copied from experiment 74's own printout, the night prediction row, the half-width range, the exploratory labels, the claim that the monthly figures have no interval, and the unsupported statement that a second year would resolve a 0.05-point gain. Rejected: none.

    Prose review (Sonnet), triaged. Rejected: moving all the legend ahead of the take-home paragraph, which would bury the conclusion.
